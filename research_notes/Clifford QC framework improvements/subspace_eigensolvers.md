# Quantum subspace eigensolvers, 2022–2026: what the literature establishes and what A-CASE/DA-CASE can borrow

Scope note: compiled 14 Nov 2026. Items marked **[preprint]** are unrefereed arXiv postings as of this date. Items marked **[background]** predate 2023 or were not re-fetched in this session and are cited from their arXiv records. "Single-reference, Hadamard-free" means that every matrix element is a Pauli expectation on one prepared state, as in A-CASE. Project facts quoted in Inferences (7,371-word bank, 3.019 → 0.342 mHa warm start, and so on) come from the task brief and the clifford_qc README, not from outside sources.

## 1. Krylov subspace diagonalization: convergence and noise theory, the largest hardware demonstrations, and their costs

### Takeaway
The theory is now mature. With thresholding, the ground-energy error is provably stable. Kirby (2024) shows that the error grows linearly in the matrix-element error, and Lee et al. (2024) give non-asymptotic bounds for shot noise. Refinements in 2025–2026 improve the regularizer: rotation-before-thresholding gives up to ~100× fewer samples with a practical heuristic. The largest coherent Krylov (KQD) run is a 56-site Heisenberg lattice on 57 qubits with D = 10. Sample-based Krylov (SKQD, SqDRIFT) has reached 85 qubits and, as a preprint, 100 qubits. It does this by giving up overlap and Hamiltonian estimation in favour of bitstring sampling. All coherent KQD variants need time evolution and usually controlled operations or Hadamard tests, which A-CASE avoids.

### Cited Findings
**Theory**
- Epperly, Lin and Nakatsukasa, "A theory of quantum subspace diagonalization", SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 43(3):1263–1290 (2022). The paper extends classical matrix perturbation theory to show that QSD can compute the smallest eigenvalue accurately "under natural conditions", even though the GEP is ill-conditioned and noisy. It recommends thresholding the overlap matrix at a level proportional to the noise. It argues that "no/tiny threshold + identify real eigenvalues" is less robust. The supplement gives an *AutoThresholding* sanity check that tries successively smaller thresholds and stops when the estimates move by more than a relative tolerance — [arXiv 2110.07492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07492); [SIAM supplement](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/suppl/10.1137/21M145954X/suppl_file/supplement.pdf)
- Kirby, "Analysis of quantum Krylov algorithms with errors", Quantum 8, 1457 (Aug 2024). This is a non-asymptotic analysis for real-time-evolution Krylov with generic errors. The upper-bound error is **linear** in the input error rate, whereas earlier analysis proved only square-root (sublinear) scaling while numerics showed roughly linear scaling. A separate lower bound limits how far noise can push the estimate below the true ground energy (violation of variationality). The method maps the generic errors to an effective Hamiltonian in an effective Krylov space — [arXiv 2401.01246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01246); [IBM listing](https://research.ibm.com/publications/analysis-of-quantum-krylov-algorithms-with-errors)
- G. Lee, D. Lee and Huh, "Sampling error analysis in quantum Krylov subspace diagonalization", Quantum 8, 1477 (Sep 2024). The paper models Hadamard-test shot noise as Gaussian perturbations of the GEP, gives a non-asymptotic framework linking sampling noise to eigenvalue error, and proposes discarding ill-conditioned basis vectors at a "truncation point". On the 1D Hubbard model the bound predicts the observed errors in well-conditioned regions — [arXiv 2307.16279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16279v3)
- G. Lee, Choi, Huh and Izmaylov, "Efficient strategies for reducing sampling error in QKSD", Digital Discovery 4, 954–969 (2025). It compares two Hamiltonian decompositions (LCU and diagonalizable fragments) and proposes a *shifting technique*, which "eliminates redundant Hamiltonian components that annihilate either the bra or ket states", and *coefficient splitting*, which optimizes common terms across circuits. Sampling cost falls by **20–500×** on small molecules — [arXiv 2409.02504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02504)
- Zhang, Wang, Xu and Li, "Measurement-efficient quantum Krylov subspace diagonalisation", Quantum (Aug 2024, q-2024-08-13-1438). It uses a "Gaussian-power" basis (Hamiltonian powers times a Gaussian, written as an integral of real-time evolution) and reports "typically 10^4 to 10^12 times fewer measurements" than other QKSD variants on Heisenberg and Hubbard lattices. That figure is from the v1 abstract; check the published version — [arXiv 2301.13353](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13353); [Quantum](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2024-08-13-1438/)
- De Vriendt et al., "A New Angle on Quantum Subspace Diagonalization for Quantum Chemistry" **[preprint, Feb 2026]**. The method applies an eigenvector-preserving SO(2) rotation of the pencil before thresholding, H_θ = H cosθ − S sinθ and S_θ = S cosθ + H sinθ, and first projects the noisy S onto the nearest Hermitian PSD matrix with unit diagonal. A heuristic picks θ to minimize the batch variance of the ground energy. With the heuristic, it reaches target errors with up to ~**100× fewer samples**; with an oracle angle the reduction is up to **10^4×**. For Fe(III)-NTA in a (5,5) active space at noise 10^-7, the heuristic cut the error from 0.89 mHa (naive thresholding) to 0.38 mHa. For butadiene with the oracle angle at noise 10^-4, the error was 0.019–0.027 mHa against 0.14 mHa for the best naive run at noise 10^-5 — [arXiv 2602.11985](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11985)
- Oumarou et al., "Optimizing and comparing quantum resources of statistical phase estimation and Krylov subspace diagonalization" **[preprint, Mar 2026]**. It argues that existing depth bounds are loose and that the Krylov dimension K and shot count M are correlated, and it reports roughly a two-thirds reduction in circuit depth. This comes from a search snippet only; the full text was not read — [arXiv 2603.15552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15552v1)

**Filtered and signal-processing relatives (all need time evolution plus Hadamard-type circuits)**
- QCELS (Ding & Lin, PRX Quantum 4, 020331, 2023) fits Hadamard-test signals ⟨ψ|e^{-iHt}|ψ⟩ to a complex exponential by nonlinear least squares. It needs one ancilla, its total cost is Heisenberg-limited Õ(ε^-1), and its depth is about two orders of magnitude below QPE in the paper's numerics. MM-QCELS (Quantum 7, 1136, 2023) estimates several eigenvalues at once — [arXiv 2211.11973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11973v1); [arXiv 2303.05714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05714v3)
- ODMD (Shen, Camps et al., Quantum, Aug 2025) replaces the GEP with least squares (dynamic mode decomposition) on real-time measurements. It needs at most one ancilla, converges exponentially without noise, and is resilient to perturbative noise, but needs hyperparameter tuning under heavy noise. Fourier-denoised ODMD **[preprint, May 2025]** hard-thresholds small DFT components and reports convergence in high-noise regimes at no extra quantum cost — [Quantum q-2025-08-27-1836](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2025-08-27-1836/); [arXiv 2505.10735](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.10735v1)
- Background: Klymko et al., real-time-evolution Krylov on hardware, PRX Quantum 3, 020323 (2022) — [arXiv 2103.08563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08563). Cortes & Gray, Krylov for ground and excited states, PRA 105, 022417 (2022) — [arXiv 2109.06868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06868). Motta et al. QITE/QLanczos, Nat. Phys. 16, 205 (2020) — [arXiv 1901.07653](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.07653) **[background]**

**Hardware**
- Yoshioka, Amico, Kirby et al., "Krylov diagonalization of large many-body Hamiltonians on a quantum processor", Nat. Commun. 16, 5014 (2025). The model is the spin-½ antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model on heavy-hex lattices of 56, 44 and 42 sites (particle sectors k = 1, 3, 5) on a 133-qubit Heron R1 (ibm_montecarlo), using 57, 45 and 43 qubits including **one control ancilla**. Other settings: Krylov dimension **D = 10**; two second-order Trotter steps; PEA error mitigation with a sparse Pauli–Lindblad model, plus TREX readout mitigation, Pauli twirling and dynamical decoupling; eigenvalue thresholding of S, with ε "as small as possible while still giving exponential suppression"; bootstrap standard deviations. The k = 3 and k = 5 results agree with ideal simulation within error bars. The k = 1 results fall **below** the true lowest energy, which the authors attribute to noise-induced leakage out of the sector — [arXiv 2407.14431](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14431v3)
- Yu, Robledo-Moreno, Iosue, …, Kirby, Motta, Mezzacapo, Sharma, "Quantum-centric algorithm for sample-based Krylov diagonalization" (SKQD) **[preprint; v3 Sep 2025, no journal listed]**. The paper proves polynomial-time convergence under KQD assumptions plus a sparse ground state. Hardware ran on ibm_fez (Heron r2, 156 qubits), with post-processing on Frontier. Results: single-impurity Anderson model with 41 bath sites on **85 qubits**, up to **6,153 two-qubit gates** (~337 per Trotter step); **10^5 shots per Krylov dimension**; relative error against DMRG ~10^-4 → ~10^-5 as U goes from 1 to 10. A 4-impurity model was run on ~70 qubits (the text is internally inconsistent: 70 vs 64) with relative error ~10^-4, or ~6×10^-5 after orbital optimization. Numerically, SKQD outperforms KQD under shot noise — [arXiv 2501.09702](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09702v3)
- SKQD follow-ups. Schwinger model with a θ-term on a 4-qubit trapped-ion device plus IBM devices **[preprint, Oct 2025]** — [arXiv 2510.26951](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2510.26951). 18- and 30-qubit Heisenberg chains on hardware **[preprint]** — [arXiv 2512.17141](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2512.17141). 12-site kagome **[preprint]** — [arXiv 2605.29521](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29521). Grandadam et al. (accepted at an IEEE QCE26 workshop) show that under depolarizing noise the SKQD guarantee carries an **exponential depth penalty** in shots. On a 20-site (40-qubit) SIAM, device noise broke the predicted convergence, and approximate compilation of the Krylov circuits restored it while also cutting shots and classical subspace dimension — [arXiv 2609.05957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05957)
- SqDRIFT (Piccinelli et al., IBM) **[preprint, Aug 2025, rev. Jan 2026]** replaces coherent Trotter circuits with qDRIFT random circuits, keeps SKQD-type convergence guarantees under the sparse-ground-state assumption, and was applied to polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons beyond exact diagonalization — [arXiv 2508.02578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02578). Piccinelli et al. applied it to a half-Möbius-topology molecule with active spaces of 36 orbitals (**72 qubits**), extended to 50 orbitals (**100 qubits**). The abstract gives no energy errors and claims no advantage **[preprint, Mar 2026]** — [arXiv 2603.08696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08696)
- Quantinuum's InQuanto team describes a Krylov method as a cheaper alternative to (variational) QPE whose main cost is time-evolution depth. The blog gives no date and no hardware numbers — [Quantinuum blog](https://www.quantinuum.com/blog/new-krylov-method-reduces-cost-of-the-variational-quantum-phase-estimation-near-term-quantum-algorithm)

### Inferences
- Krylov's accuracy edge over A-CASE at matched basis size (the project's own finding) matches the theory: a powers-of-H or e^{-iHt} basis gives Chebyshev/Lanczos-type exponential convergence in D, which a greedy operator basis does not guarantee. A-CASE cannot import time evolution without breaking its Hadamard-free design. It can import the **noise theory**, though. The Kirby-style linear error bound and the Lee et al. truncation-point analysis are statements about a perturbed (H, S) pencil, and they apply to any GEP whose entries carry known variances, including entries reconstructed from a Pauli bank.
- Rotation-before-thresholding (2602.11985) and the nearest-correlation-matrix projection of S are drop-in classical post-processing steps for A-CASE's finite-shot solve, with zero extra measurements. They are the cheapest direct test against the project's "thresholding trades median accuracy for tail control" finding.
- The "shifting technique" carries over directly. For A-CASE, H_ij = ⟨ψ|A_i†HA_j|ψ⟩ is unchanged if H is replaced by H − Σ_k c_k (Q_k − q_k), where the Q_k are symmetries (N, S_z, …) whose eigenvalue q_k is fixed on the virtual states. Choosing c_k to minimize the variance of the Pauli-bank estimator reduces shot cost without changing the solve. This mirrors the 20–500× gains Lee et al. report for QKSD, but has not been tested in the A-CASE setting.

### Gaps
- The exact functional form of the Epperly–Lin–Nakatsukasa bound (exponent of the noise level, dependence on overlap and gap) was not extracted, because only the abstract was read.
- Shot totals for the 56-qubit KQD experiment were not given in the text extracted, nor was the numerical threshold ε.
- No 2025–2026 Hadamard-free Krylov hardware demonstration at >50 qubits was found. Every large coherent Krylov run uses a control ancilla or a sampling (SKQD) workaround.

## 2. Sample-based quantum diagonalization (QSCI/SQD), configuration recovery, the scaling critiques, and hybrids

### Takeaway
SQD dominates hardware headlines: 77 qubits for [4Fe-4S] in 2025, 85 qubits for SKQD, up to 100 qubits for SqDRIFT as a preprint, and 50-qubit open-shell Co complexes. The critique literature of 2025–2026 converges on two points. In same-active-space comparisons, quantum sampling yields less compact determinant sets than classical selected CI (Reinholdt et al., JCTC 2025, and a 2026 ML review that confirms the negative verdict). For delocalized lattice ground states, even optimal configuration selection needs exponentially many determinants (Gaberle & Jattana 2026). The hybrids (SKQD, SqDRIFT, TE-QSCI, HI-VQE, ADAPT-QSCI, ext-SQD, HSQD) mainly change the sampling state or add post-selection steps; none removes the compactness critique. This bears on A-CASE: its finding that operator dressing of QSCI samples gave no gain over a sample-independent selected-CI control is consistent with the literature.

### Cited Findings
- Background: Kanno et al. introduced QSCI (sample configurations from an approximate ground state, then diagonalize H classically in their span) — [arXiv 2302.11320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11320) **[background]**
- Robledo-Moreno, Motta et al., "Chemistry beyond the scale of exact diagonalization on a quantum-centric supercomputer", Sci. Adv. 11, eadu9991 (2025). The systems are N2, [2Fe-2S] and [4Fe-4S], on up to **77 qubits** on Heron plus the Fugaku supercomputer, with up to 10,570 gates and at most ~3.5k two-qubit gates. A self-consistent *configuration recovery* step corrects bitstrings with the wrong electron count. Sandeep Sharma (Caltech) is quoted as saying the paper "doesn't quite get us to the point where you can definitively say that we are better than any classical algorithm" — [arXiv 2405.05068](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05068); [Caltech news](https://thisis.caltech.edu/news/new-hybrid-quantumclassical-computing-approach-used-to-study-chemical-systems)
- Reinholdt et al., "Critical limitations in quantum-selected configuration interaction methods", J. Chem. Theory Comput. 21(14), 6811–6822 (2025); the v1 title was "Exposing a Fatal Flaw in Sample-based Quantum Diagonalization Methods". The test systems are N2 and [2Fe-2S]. Repeated sampling keeps returning configurations already found, which is worst at high target accuracy or with an approximate ansatz. Where sampling is not the bottleneck, the CI expansions are **less compact** than those from classical heuristics, so QSCI "falls behind more efficient classical counterparts" — [arXiv 2501.07231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07231)
- Gaberle & Jattana, "A critical assessment of SQD for Heisenberg and Hubbard models" **[preprint, May 2026]**. Using exact ground states, which removes all sampling and device noise, the minimum number of configurations needed for fixed accuracy "grows exponentially with the system size" even when configurations are added optimally in order of decreasing probability. The authors attribute this to delocalization, not to sampling inefficiency — [arXiv 2605.02494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02494)
- Bonilla Vargas, "Machine learning for SQD: a review of generative configuration recovery and the classical-simulability frontier" **[preprint, Aug/Sep 2026]**. The review reaches a negative verdict on whether the quantum sampler beats classical selected CI and credits Reinholdt et al. with priority. Its one generative advantage, on N2 against a noise-matched classical recovery loop, is a margin "five seeds cannot resolve". The method loses by "over a factor of two" to a classical selector that needs no sampler. The review shows that determinant counts are undefined until the orbital gauge is fixed (alpha-string weights are not invariant under rotations within degenerate shells), proposes a **ten-element benchmarking standard**, and retracted one v1 claim after a self-audit — [arXiv 2608.05314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05314)
- A W4-11 hardware benchmark **[preprint]** reports that SQD with configuration spaces of CISD dimension remains less accurate than CISD. It attributes the gap to suboptimal sampling, amplified by noise and by limits of configuration recovery. This is from a search snippet only — [arXiv 2512.01012](https://api.emergentmind.com/papers/2512.01012)
- ext-SQD (Barison, Robledo-Moreno, Motta), Quantum Sci. Technol. 10, 025034 (2025), targets low-lying excited states. The test systems are N2 bond breaking in cc-pVDZ and methyl-capped [2Fe-2S] in a (30e,20o) active space on **45 qubits** of Heron with **3,170 gates**. It is reported as more accurate and more efficient than QSE with single and double excitations — [arXiv 2411.00468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00468v1)
- TE-QSCI (Mikkelsen & Nakagawa, Phys. Rev. Research, Oct 2025) samples from e^{-iHt}|HF⟩ or its time average, so no optimization is needed. The time evolution only picks determinants and is never used for expectation values, so it can be imprecise. Tests covered N2, NH3 and hydrogen chains — [arXiv 2412.13839](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13839v3)
- ADAPT-QSCI (Nakagawa et al., JCTC, Dec 2024) grows the QSCI input circuit adaptively with classically chosen rotations. It reports accurate small-molecule energies at 8 qubits with 1% two-qubit and readout error rates — [arXiv 2311.01105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01105v2)
- HI-VQE (Qunova) **[preprint, Mar 2025]** combines variational training, iterative subspace construction and pre-diagonalization configuration screening — [arXiv 2503.06292](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.06292v2). Follow-up work on N2 and an Fe–S cluster is benchmarked against HCI **[preprint, Jan 2026]** — [arXiv 2601.06935](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06935v1). The claim of validation "up to 56 qubits" is company-reported — [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/qunova-launches-hi-vqe-chemistry-algorithm-on-aws-marketplace-for-braket-integration/)
- HSQD (Qunova) **[preprint, Feb 2026]** reports, for N2 (10e,26o) on IBM hardware, accuracy matching SQD with **half the qubits** and **40% fewer measurements** — [alphaXiv 2602.01165](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2602.01165)
- Open-shell SQD with implicit solvent (IEF-PCM) on [Co(H2O)5CO2]^2+/3+ (singlet, quintet, doublet and quartet states) used IBM Heron samples with active spaces of up to **50 qubits**. It "closely reproduces" CCSD and HCI in the same active spaces, but the abstract gives no numbers **[preprint, Jul–Sep 2026]** — [arXiv 2607.16389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16389)
- Cleveland Clinic: Kaliakin, Shajan, Liang and Merz, "Implicit solvent SQD", J. Phys. Chem. B 129(23) (2025), studied water, methanol, ethanol and methylamine on IBM hardware. Solvation energies came within 1 kcal/mol of CASCI-IEF-PCM and of experiment (per secondary coverage) — [arXiv 2502.10189](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.10189); [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/06/05/quantum-chemistry-moves-closer-to-practical-use-with-solvent-ready-algorithms-cleveland-clinic-researchers-report/)
- IQM: SQD with an LUCJ ansatz on up to 16 qubits of a 24-qubit Sirius device, for H2, LiH, BeH2, H2O and NH3 **[preprint, rev. Jul 2026]** — [alphaXiv 2604.01983](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.01983)

### Inferences
- The SQD literature confirms that "selected CI on a quantum-generated determinant list" is only as good as the determinant ranking, and that classical CIPSI/HCI rankings usually win at matched space. A-CASE's QSCI comparator therefore should always be reported next to a sample-free classical SCI control at the same determinant count, as the project already does. Following Bonilla Vargas, it should also be reported with the orbital gauge fixed.
- SKQD and SqDRIFT get their robustness from **not estimating S and H from noisy expectations**: they only need the right bitstrings. This is the opposite trade-off from A-CASE, which estimates everything from expectations and needs no deep circuits. The two families are complementary rather than competing. A-CASE-style operator dressing is unlikely to help an SQD space that is already near-CISD, which is consistent with the project's negative result.

### Gaps
- No quantitative "shots-to-chemical-accuracy" comparison between SQD and an expectation-value subspace method at the same active space was found.
- The ML review's exact N2 margins and the W4-11 paper's numbers were not extracted.

## 3. Operator-generated, single-reference constructions (QSE, qEOM, VQSE, QAE, GCIM, NOQE, QDavidson, MRSQK, PQSE, Q-SENSE, shadow QSE): which are Hadamard-free, and what they cost to measure

### Takeaway
A-CASE's measurement model (basis A_i|ψ⟩, all S_ij and H_ij as Pauli expectations on one state) has clear prior art. Examples are QSE and virtual QSE, Bharti–Haug's iterative quantum-assisted eigensolver (explicitly "no Hadamard test"), VQSE with operator selection (2026), and above all **QSE with classical shadows**. Shadow QSE scaled to **80 qubits on hardware (PRL 2026)** and, in simulation, to **10^4-dimensional** operator subspaces chosen by a 2×2 {I, G_i} energy-lowering screen, which is essentially A-CASE's growth score. Methods built on separately prepared states (NOQE, QDavidson, MRSQK, KQD) need Hadamard tests or overlap circuits. GCIM computes its elements from Pauli strings in principle, but its noise analysis assumes Hadamard tests. Every source finds that measurement cost scales with the square of the basis size times the Hamiltonian term count. The proposed mitigations are commutator cancellations (qEOM), symmetry restrictions (seniority, spin), operator truncation, and informationally complete measurement.

### Cited Findings
**Hadamard-free (single reference, Pauli expectations only)**
- Background: QSE (McClean et al., PRA 95, 042308, 2017) — [arXiv 1603.05681](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05681); QSE for error decoding (McClean et al., Nat. Commun. 11, 636, 2020) — [arXiv 1903.05786](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.05786); virtual QSE (Takeshita et al., PRX 10, 011004, 2020), which adds virtual-orbital correlation through extra measurements on the active-space reference and notes sensitivity to regularization when A and B are both noisy — [arXiv 2002.12902](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.12902) **[background]**
- Bharti & Haug, "Iterative quantum assisted eigensolver" (2020/2021). The basis is built Krylov-like from products of Hamiltonian Pauli strings acting on one state. Every overlap reduces to a directly measurable Pauli expectation, "without the Hadamard test", and the classical step is a constrained optimization — [arXiv 2010.05638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05638)
- Getelina, Sharma, Iadecola, Orth and Yao, "Quantum subspace expansion in the presence of hardware noise", APL Quantum 1, 036127 (2024). The paper benchmarks the Bharti–Haug eigensolver under noise on 1D and 2D mixed-field Ising models, on ibmq_quito (5 qubits), ibmq_guadalupe (16 qubits) and fake_guadalupe. It proposes basis selection from the **trace of the noisy overlap matrix** and approaches the exact result with probabilistic error reduction — [arXiv 2404.09132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09132)
- Lamp et al., "Optimizing subspace expansion in quantum chemistry through operator selection and reference state choice" **[preprint, Aug 2026]**. VQSE matrices are measured on the unchanged reference, H_ij = ⟨Ψ_ref|O_i†HO_j|Ψ_ref⟩, and Wick's theorem bounds the requirement at the 4-RDM (~N_A^8). The hardware run measured all unique real Pauli strings with qubit-wise commuting grouping: 5 strings for 2-qubit and 14 for 3-qubit circuits on ibm_aachen, apparently ~10^5 shots per setting. Adding extra double excitations raises the overhead from N_A^8 to N_A^12. Keeping only the **top 10% of operators by coefficient magnitude still reached chemical accuracy**. The full pool (equivalent to MR-CISD) with ε = 10^-2 reached chemical accuracy on hardware — [arXiv 2608.16362](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16362v1)
- Boyd, Koczor and Cai, "High-dimensional subspace expansion using classical shadows", Phys. Rev. A 111, 022423 (2025). The expansion operators are Pauli strings up to a chosen weight. Each candidate G_i is scored by the energy lowering ΔE_i of a **2-dimensional expansion in {I, G_i}**, and the top K are kept. The screening costs O(N_G N_H) with N_G ≳ 10^6 candidates. In simulation, K reaches 3,000–10,000 (14-qubit spin chain, near the 10,690 3-local Paulis) and K = 2,000 for LiH (12 qubits, UCCSD). The energy-error floor scales as ~ε^0.6. Error drops by almost one order of magnitude at shot noise ~10^-3 and almost two at ~10^-5. LiH improves by two orders of magnitude over direct VQE estimation after a few VQE steps. Near-ground-state inputs need ~10^8 snapshots for SSE to reliably beat direct estimation — [arXiv 2406.11533](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11533); [venue per search listing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11533)
- Fischer, Bultrini, Tavernelli and Tacchino, "Large-scale implementation of quantum subspace expansion with classical shadows", Phys. Rev. Lett. 137, 160602 (2026). The experiment ran on ibm_fez (Heron r2) for a spin model with three-body interactions across a phase transition, on **up to 80 qubits**. The state was an MPS preparation with 540 CZ gates at N = 80. The measurement used **32,768 random X/Y/Z bases × 8 shots = 262,144 shots per circuit**, and ~5.6×10^13 single-qubit Pauli traces were evaluated. The "Krylov+" expansion is {I, H, up to five best Paulis per weight w = 1…5 from random pools of 150}, with L = 27 at N = 16 and L = 14 at N = 80. Ground energies and local order parameters were recovered within statistical error — [arXiv 2510.25640](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25640v1)
- Q-SENSE (Patel, Jayakumar, Huang, Zeng, Izmaylov) **[preprint, rev. Dec 2025]** uses seniority operators as artificial symmetries to build orthogonal basis states from short circuits. Only a small fraction of Hamiltonian terms couple different seniority sectors, which cuts the terms that must be measured — [arXiv 2509.01061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01061)
- Quantum computed moments (QCM) form a single-reference, Pauli-only route: measure ⟨H^k⟩ on one trial state and apply Lanczos cumulant theory. The method has been demonstrated on 2D magnets up to 5×5 (25 qubits) and on H6 from an HF state on hardware, reaching 99.9% of the exact energy. A 2026 Phys. Rev. Applied paper extends it to the water dipole moment — [arXiv 2009.13140 summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2009.13140); [PR Applied](https://journals.aps.org/prapplied/pdf/10.1103/y4nq-kcw1); [arXiv 2109.12790](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.12790)

**Generator-coordinate / adaptive non-orthogonal**
- GCIM / ADAPT-GCIM (Zheng, Peng, Li, Yang, Kowalski; npj Quantum Inf. 2024). The method uses UCC generators to build non-orthogonal, overcomplete generating functions, and the resulting GEP gives lower bounds on VQE/ADAPT-VQE energies. Matrix elements are obtained by "converting B_i†HB_j and B_i†B_j to the sums of Pauli strings, then grouping", but the finite-shot noise model assumes Hadamard tests, and "evad[ing] Hadamard test" is listed as future work. Measurement cost "scales as the squared number of generating functions". **Finite-shot errors in S matter more than those in H.** ADAPT-GCIM costs O(N_iter^2), against O(Ñ_opt × N_iter) for ADAPT-VQE. The extracted text reports a hardware H4 run on ibm_osaka with error-mitigated error "0.046 a.u. → 3.9×10^-9 a.u.". That second number is implausibly small for hardware and should be verified against the paper — [arXiv 2312.07691](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.07691v3); [PNNL highlight](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/unleashing-quantum-computing-quantum-chemistry-constrained-optimization)

**Separately prepared states (need Hadamard tests or overlap circuits)**
- NOQE, "Say NO to optimization" (Baek et al., Berkeley/LBNL/Google) diagonalizes over non-orthogonal ansatz states (NOCI) without variational optimization. The quantum computer supplies the off-diagonal elements, which are exponentially costly classically. Tests were stretched H2 and square H4. Journal status was not verified — [arXiv 2205.09039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09039)
- Background: NOVQE (Huggins et al., NJP 2020) — [arXiv 1909.09114](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09114); MRSQK (Stair, Huang, Evangelista, JCTC 2020) — [arXiv 1911.05163](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.05163) **[background]**
- QDavidson (Tkachenko, Cincio, Boldyrev, Tretiak, Dub, Zhang). H and S are measured "using an ancillary qubit and the Hadamard test". Each residual (H − E_I)|Ψ_I⟩ is replaced by e^{-Δτ(H−E_I)}|Ψ_I⟩, which is mapped QITE-style to e^{-iÂ} with Â = Σ a_α P_α by solving a linear system. A preconditioned version maps (H − E_i)^{-1}(H − E) directly. A new vector is accepted only if its component orthogonal to the subspace exceeds ε. The pools are JW singles and doubles reduced to odd-Y Pauli words: **12, 40 and 828** words for C2H4, C3H3+ and C6H6. Benzene is (6e,6o), a 12-qubit Hamiltonian with 407 terms. Shot cost is O(|h_max|^2 M p^-2 N_K^2) for H and O(p^-2 N_K^2 P^2) for the mapping. Precision 10^-4 sufficed for C2H4, with ~10^8 shots per circuit and **~10^10 shots in total**. QLanczos needed depth 480 after 8 iterations for the accuracy QDavidson reached at peak depth 120. From a single HF start, benzene converged slowly; **multiple initial determinants** gave chemical accuracy for S0, T1 and T2 after 11 iterations — [arXiv 2204.10741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10741) (PDF text read locally; journal status not verified)
- Partitioned QSE (O'Leary, Anderson, Jaksch, Kiffner), Quantum 9, 1726 (2025), splits one ill-conditioned Krylov-basis QSE into several smaller, better-conditioned problems with the **same quantum data**. It adds polynomial classical overhead and chooses the partitioning by energy variance — [Quantum](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2025-05-05-1726/); [arXiv 2403.08868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08868v3)

**Measurement-cost context**
- Partitioning Pauli strings into commuting groups is equivalent to graph colouring (NP-hard), so greedy heuristics are standard. For fermionic diagonal terms, Baranyai's theorem gives O(N^3) groups for O(N^4) terms — [arXiv 2305.04783](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.04783)
- Background: Gonthier et al., "Measurements as a roadblock to near-term practical quantum advantage in chemistry", PRR 4, 033154 (2022) — [arXiv 2012.04001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.04001) **[background]**

### Inferences
- **Prior-art alert.** Boyd–Koczor–Cai's "K-significant" selection (rank Pauli G_i by the energy lowering of a 2×2 {I, G_i} GEP) is functionally A-CASE's overlap-aware 2×2 candidate score, applied to Pauli strings and shadow data rather than to Pauli-sum generators. Bharti–Haug QAE is the earlier Hadamard-free Pauli-product Krylov. A-CASE papers should cite and benchmark against both. The distinctive A-CASE elements are Pauli-*sum* generators, symmetry-leakage rejection, the PRD residual engine and the second-moment residual bank.
- The shadow route is the most promising way to **decouple the Pauli-word bank from the measurement schedule**. With randomized or derandomized local measurements, every low-weight Pauli in the bank is estimated from the same shots, and the bank size then costs classical time, not shots. Fischer et al. evaluated ~10^13 traces from 2.6×10^5 shots per circuit. Molecular JW words are high-weight, though, and local-shadow variance grows as 3^weight, so this works best for short-range (lattice) generators. For molecules it would need fermionic or grouped measurement (inference only; not tested in the sources here).
- GCIM's finding that S errors dominate suggests allocating more shots to the Pauli words that enter S, which are the low-order words A_i†A_j, than to those that enter only H.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that reports the size of the Pauli-word bank per added basis vector for a Pauli-sum-generator method comparable to A-CASE's 7,371 words for 9 vectors. Lamp et al. give only scaling (N_A^8 → N_A^12) and tiny hardware counts.
- GCIM's hardware error figure needs verification, and NOQE's and QDavidson's journal versions were not confirmed.

## 4. Regularization and inference for noisy generalized eigenproblems

### Takeaway
Three tiers have emerged. (i) **Truncation and thresholding** in S's eigenbasis (Epperly; Lee et al.; used in every hardware Krylov paper), now improved by **nearest-correlation-matrix projection plus pencil rotation** (2026, up to ~100× fewer samples with a practical angle heuristic). (ii) **Statistically aware replacements for the GEP**: QSE as an *energy minimization under an explicit error budget*, with ratio-estimator error bars and no S inversion (Fischer et al., PRL 2026, 80 qubits), and Bayesian posterior sampling over (H, S) with physical-constraint likelihoods (trimmed sampling, Hicks & Lee 2023). (iii) **Signal-fitting alternatives** (QCELS, ODMD, Fourier denoising), which need time-series data. No source was found that recommends Tikhonov/ridge over thresholding for QSD; the explicit guidance (Epperly) favours thresholding at roughly the noise level. Error bars in practice come from bootstrap over shot batches.

### Cited Findings
- **Thresholding guidance.** Threshold in proportion to the noise level; AutoThresholding gives a check when the noise is unknown — [arXiv 2110.07492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07492). On hardware, Lamp et al. set ε = 10^-2 from readout-error calibration (ε = 10^-12 noiseless), following Epperly's "slightly above the expected noise" rule — [arXiv 2608.16362](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16362v1). Yoshioka et al. chose ε "as small as possible while still giving exponential suppression" and bootstrapped the uncertainties — [arXiv 2407.14431](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14431v3)
- **Truncation point.** Discard ill-conditioned basis vectors; the predicted error matches observation in well-conditioned regions — [arXiv 2307.16279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16279v3)
- **Rotation plus projection.** Project S to the nearest X with X ⪰ 0 and diag(X) = 1 (a CVXPY solve), rotate the pencil by θ, then threshold. θ is chosen by minimizing the batch variance of E0 over θ ∈ [0, π]. Simulations used 100 noisy batches × 1,250 instances and a noise-aware stopping rule ε = γ·max(σ_j, σ_{j−1}), with γ = 1.0 for polyenes and 0.75 for Fe(III)-NTA. Rotation retained 1–3 more Krylov states than naive thresholding in some regions and reached chemical accuracy at 10× higher noise for Fe(III)-NTA **[preprint]** — [arXiv 2602.11985](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11985)
- **Dynamic eigen-truncation with a fallback.** Restrict to the leading K̃ eigenvectors of S (S̃ = Q†SQ, H̃ = Q†HQ), choose K̃ where a moving variance of E0 over truncations is minimal, and output the direct estimate whenever SSE's energy is higher — [arXiv 2406.11533](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11533)
- **Constrained-optimization QSE.** Minimize Ĥ(c) = x̄/ȳ (ratio of sample means over POVM outcomes) subject to ε̂(c) ≤ ε_max. The variance comes from a second-order Taylor expansion that includes the covariance from reusing shots across matrix entries. S is never inverted, and ε_max (15% of the unmitigated energy at N = 80) trades bias for variance. The SVD-truncation alternative had to drop up to 6 of 22 singular values at N = 48 to stay variational and gave no tunable error bars. The solver was COBYLA from c = (1, 0, …), averaging ~1,438 iterations — [arXiv 2510.25640](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25640v1)
- **Bayesian trimmed sampling.** Hicks & Lee, Phys. Rev. Research (Apr 2023), sample (H, S) from priors set by the per-element error bars, weight them by likelihoods encoding physical constraints, and report weighted medians and error bars for energies and observables. They report it "performs far better than standard regularization methods" and that it yields reliable error estimates — [arXiv 2209.02083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02083v1); [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/c02e954a596b4e7bac7c4a4a81ae2baa)
- **Partitioning instead of truncation.** PQSE solves a sequence of smaller, better-conditioned QSEs on the same data — [Quantum 9, 1726](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2025-05-05-1726/)
- **Asymmetric noise sensitivity.** In GCIM, finite-shot errors in S matter more than errors in H — [arXiv 2312.07691](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.07691v3)
- **Shot-noise scaling of the floor.** In shadow-based SSE the energy floor scales as ~ε^0.6 with shot noise, and SSE reliably beats direct estimation only at ~10^8 snapshots for near-ground-state inputs — [arXiv 2406.11533](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11533)
- **Precision needed.** QDavidson needed element precision ~10^-4 for numerical stability on C2H4, which meant ~10^10 total shots — [arXiv 2204.10741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10741)
- **Non-variationality bound.** Kirby gives a lower bound on how far below E0 a noisy Krylov estimate can fall, which is useful for flagging unphysical Ritz values — [arXiv 2401.01246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01246). In hardware, a leakage-induced below-E0 result occurred at k = 1 in the 56-qubit run — [arXiv 2407.14431](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14431v3)
- **Least-squares alternatives** (ODMD, FDODMD, QCELS) need real-time signals and therefore Hadamard-type circuits — [Quantum 2025](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2025-08-27-1836/); [arXiv 2505.10735](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.10735v1); [arXiv 2211.11973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11973v1)

### Inferences
- For A-CASE's fragile finite-shot solve, a ranked menu of zero-extra-measurement changes:
  1. Project the sampled S to the nearest unit-diagonal PSD matrix before anything else.
  2. Add a θ-rotation scan chosen by bootstrap variance.
  3. Replace the hard threshold with the Fischer-style program "min c†Hc / c†Sc subject to a propagated standard error ≤ budget". Its inputs are covariances that the Pauli bank already holds, since the README says the bank keeps within-setting covariance.
  4. Report bootstrap or trimmed-sampling posteriors for E0 rather than point estimates.

  Items 1–3 target exactly the "median vs tail" trade-off the project observed.
- Because the A-CASE bank shares shots across many S_ij and H_ij, the **covariance term** in the ratio-estimator variance (which Fischer et al. include explicitly) is large and probably dominant. Thresholding rules that assume independent Gaussian entries (Lee et al., De Vriendt et al.) may misjudge the noise level for a shared bank.
- The finding that "finite-shot adaptive growth is unreliable at eight qubits" fits the GCIM observation about S-error sensitivity and the Boyd–Koczor–Cai ~10^8-snapshot threshold. Noisy 2×2 scores are dominated by S noise when candidates are near-dependent. A variance-penalized score (expected ΔE minus k·σ(ΔE)) or a requirement that ΔE exceed its bootstrap spread is the natural fix. This is an inference; none of the sources tests it.

### Gaps
- No source compares Tikhonov/ridge regularization quantitatively against thresholding for QSD or QSE. No maximum-likelihood GEP estimator specific to quantum subspace methods (beyond Hicks–Lee's Bayesian approach) was found, and no Gaussian-process approach either.
- No study of thresholding under **correlated** (shared-shot) matrix-element noise was found.

## 5. Basis selection, pruning, and reducing Pauli words per added basis vector

### Takeaway
Selection rules in use are: 2×2 energy-lowering screens (shadow QSE); gradients (ADAPT-GCIM); coefficient-magnitude truncation (top 10% suffices in VQSE); energy variance (PQSE partitioning); trace or conditioning of the noisy S (Getelina et al.); and residual-norm Davidson growth with an orthogonal-component acceptance test (QDavidson). For Pauli-word reduction, the levers with published evidence are commutator forms that cancel terms (qEOM vs QSE), artificial symmetries that zero most couplings (seniority, Q-SENSE), spin- and symmetry-tailored pools (FeS: 35 vs 261 operators), the shifting technique (20–500× for QKSD), and informationally complete measurement, which makes bank width cost classical work only.

### Cited Findings
- **2×2 screen and top-K.** Screening costs O(N_G N_H), so >10^6 candidates are feasible; K up to 10^4 — [arXiv 2406.11533](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11533)
- **Random-pool plus keep-best per Pauli weight.** Up to 5 of 150 random Paulis per weight w = 1…5 were kept, giving a 14-dimensional subspace that sufficed at 80 qubits — [arXiv 2510.25640](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25640v1)
- **Gradient-adaptive GCIM.** ADAPT-GCIM picks the operators with the largest energy gradients and re-diagonalizes. On N2 (8 qubits) it converged with 18 of 26 SD-pool operators to errors of 10^-7–10^-9 Ha. On FeS (6e,6o; 12 qubits), a 35-operator quintet-tailored pool beat a 261-operator GSD pool, while the standard SD pool on the open-shell reference **did not converge**. On U2, SingletSD (54 operators) missed chemical accuracy and SingletGSD (330 operators) reached it, at an estimated ~5×10^4 shot-count increase **[preprint, Jan 2026]** — [arXiv 2601.10813](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10813v1)
- **Operator truncation.** Keeping the top 10% of VQSE operators by coefficient magnitude preserved chemical accuracy **[preprint]** — [arXiv 2608.16362](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16362v1)
- **Residual (Davidson) growth.** QDavidson accepts a new vector only if the norm of its component orthogonal to the current span (computed through S^-1) exceeds ε. It also offers a preconditioned residual mapping via (H − E_i)^-1 — [arXiv 2204.10741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10741)
- **Noisy-overlap trace criterion** for choosing basis states under hardware noise — [arXiv 2404.09132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09132)
- **Energy-variance-guided partitioning** (PQSE) — [arXiv 2403.08868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08868v3)
- **Commutator forms.** qEOM "typically leads to cancellations in the operators, so the number of operator terms is reduced compared to quantum subspace expansion" (used for Lehmann Green's functions in DMFT) — [arXiv 2404.09527](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.09527). Background: qEOM (Ollitrault et al., PRR 2, 043140, 2020) — [arXiv 1910.12890](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12890) **[background]**
- **Artificial symmetry.** Seniority-sector bases mean only a small fraction of H terms couple sectors — [arXiv 2509.01061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01061)
- **Shifting and coefficient splitting.** 20–500× lower sampling cost; Hamiltonian fragments (LCU vs diagonalizable fragments) change the cost — [arXiv 2409.02504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02504)
- **Restricting excitation operators to a compact (contextual) subspace** reduces Pauli-string counts in CS-QSE. This is from a search snippet only — [Liu & Deng PDF](https://scai.sufe.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/e9/ac/80b9a26a4999a4464ef7a1b16703/10c60098-3552-4a81-8f0d-f202a2602383.pdf)
- **Scaling of the measured quantity.** Measurement cost ∝ (number of generating functions)^2 in GCIM — [arXiv 2312.07691](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.07691v3); VQSE needs RDMs up to 4th order (N_A^8), rising to N_A^12 with richer pools — [arXiv 2608.16362](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16362v1)

### Inferences
- **Concrete A-CASE levers for the bank** (7,371 words for 9 vectors; 14–16k-word selection cache):
  - (a) Score candidates with a *word-cost-aware* criterion, ΔE per new Pauli word added to the bank. Many candidates that differ little in ΔE differ a lot in how many new words A_i†HA_j introduces. No source tests this; it follows from cost ∝ words.
  - (b) Write H_ij through commutators, ⟨A_i†[H, A_j]⟩ + ⟨A_i†A_j H⟩, where the second term collapses to known reference data when |ψ⟩ is close to an eigenstate. More generally, use qEOM-style double commutators to cancel words, as qEOM does relative to QSE.
  - (c) Impose spin or seniority symmetry on the generator pool, so that most cross-sector H couplings vanish identically and drop out of the bank.
  - (d) Use symmetry shifting to cut variance and possibly words.
  - (e) Truncate generator Pauli sums to their top coefficients, since the VQSE 10% result suggests heavy truncation is tolerable.
  - (f) For lattice models, adopt shadow-based acquisition so that bank width costs classical time only.
- A-CASE's PRD engine is the analogue of QDavidson. QDavidson needs Hadamard tests and a QITE-style mapping of residuals to unitaries, whereas A-CASE realises the residual direction as an operator A_j applied to the same reference, which is cheaper. A useful addition from QDavidson is the **orthogonal-component acceptance test** with a Davidson-style (H − E)^-1 diagonal preconditioner. A-CASE's near-dependence rejection already approximates this.

### Gaps
- No published bound on the Pauli words per added basis vector for Pauli-sum-generated subspaces was found.
- No study applying double-factorized or THC Hamiltonians to *single-reference operator-generated* QSE was found. Lee et al. 2025 use fragment decompositions only for QKSD.

## 6. Reference-state strategies (multireference, low-depth ansatz, warm starts)

### Takeaway
Across method families, accuracy is governed by (i) the reference's overlap with the target and (ii) whether the operator pool can reach the missing configurations. A richer pool can make the result reference-independent: in VQSE, active-space singles plus number operators recover MR-CISD regardless of the HF/UHF/GHF reference. Warm starts from a few VQE or ADAPT steps followed by subspace expansion consistently help. Shadow SSE improves LiH by two orders of magnitude after a few VQE steps, mirroring A-CASE's ADAPT warm start (3.019 → 0.342 mHa). Multiple initial determinants speed up Davidson-type growth markedly. Open-shell references need spin-compatible pools.

### Cited Findings
- The reference's overlap with FCI and the pool's expressivity are the two governing factors. With active→virtual operators only, results depend strongly on the reference, and enlarging the active space does not guarantee improvement. Adding singles and active-space number operators gives MR-CISD-level accuracy for any reference. Broken-symmetry HF references were tested on H2 dissociation **[preprint]** — [arXiv 2608.16362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16362)
- Combining VQE with QSE "balances quantum and classical resources" and mitigates shallow-depth training limits on hardware — [arXiv 2404.09132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09132)
- Shadow SSE from a VQE state: LiH improves by two orders of magnitude after a few VQE steps, but near-ground-state inputs need ~10^8 snapshots to beat direct estimation reliably — [arXiv 2406.11533](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11533)
- In QDavidson, a single-determinant start converged slowly for benzene, while multiple initial determinants reached chemical accuracy for S0, T1 and T2 in 11 iterations — [arXiv 2204.10741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10741)
- Fischer et al. used a classically compressed MPS state (540 CZ at 80 qubits) as the QSE reference — [arXiv 2510.25640](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25640v1)
- ADAPT-QSCI builds the sampling state adaptively; ext-SQD and SQD use LUCJ-type circuits — [arXiv 2311.01105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01105v2); [arXiv 2411.00468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00468v1)
- The pool must be "compatible with the symmetry and spin structure of the reference state". A closed-shell SD pool fails on a quintet FeS reference **[preprint]** — [arXiv 2601.10813](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10813v1)
- Krylov convergence needs the initial state's overlap with the ground state, and SKQD needs a sparse ground state as well — [arXiv 2501.09702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09702)

### Inferences
- The A-CASE warm-start gain fits the literature pattern, and the VQSE result suggests the gain is partly a pool-expressivity effect. Before investing in deeper warm starts, test whether adding cheap "orbital-relaxation" generators (singles and number operators in the active space) to the A-CASE pool reproduces part of the warm-start gain from the plain HF reference.
- Multi-root or multi-determinant seeding is a direct, Hadamard-free extension for A-CASE. Seed the basis with a few A_i that create the leading excited determinants, analogous to QDavidson's multiple initial states. Overlaps and couplings are still single-reference Pauli expectations if the determinants are generated as A_i|HF⟩.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study was found of matchgate or Gaussian-state references for operator-generated QSE, nor of CASSCF-orbital references specifically for QSE/A-CASE-type methods.

## 7. Excited states, observables and response from the same subspace

### Takeaway
Best practice is to reuse the subspace eigenvectors for excited states (qEOM or QSE roots), for Lehmann-representation Green's functions, and for mitigated observables. Measurement cost and conditioning are the limits. qEOM's commutator form uses fewer operator terms than QSE. Noise filtering in occupation-number QSE removes spurious satellite peaks. ext-SQD reports better excited-state accuracy than QSE with singles and doubles. Quantitative excited-state accuracy at matched shots is rarely reported.

### Cited Findings
- **ext-SQD vs QSE-SD.** ext-SQD improves on QSE with single and double excitations "in both accuracy and efficiency". Tests: N2 along bond breaking (cc-pVDZ) and [2Fe-2S] (30e,20o) on 45 qubits — [arXiv 2411.00468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00468v1)
- **Occupation-number QSE for single-particle Green's functions** (Gauthier et al., PRA 110, 032624, 2024). Agreement with the exact local spectral function for a 2-site model, and similar accuracy suggested for 4 sites in classical simulation. "Noise filtering removes satellite peaks" in NISQ spectral weights — [arXiv 2312.13497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13497v2)
- **qEOM in DMFT** for impurity Green's functions via the Lehmann representation, with fewer operator terms than QSE — [arXiv 2404.09527](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.09527). VQE+QSE Lehmann Green's functions — [arXiv 2309.09914](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/2309.09914)
- **QSE-mitigated observables.** QSE mitigated local order parameters at up to 80 qubits — [arXiv 2510.25640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25640)
- **QCM for dipole moments.** Moments-based estimation improved the water dipole moment over direct measurement without extra depth (Phys. Rev. Applied 2026) — [PR Applied](https://journals.aps.org/prapplied/pdf/10.1103/y4nq-kcw1)
- **QDavidson excited states.** Exact energies for C2H4 and C3H3+ in 1–2 iterations; benzene S1 and T1 within 10^-2 Ha after ~30 iterations from one determinant — [arXiv 2204.10741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10741)
- **Multiple eigenvalues from one initial state** with MM-QCELS (needs Hadamard tests) — [arXiv 2303.05714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05714v3)

### Inferences
- A-CASE already has the second-moment bank (⟨A_i†H²A_j⟩) and folded-spectrum roots. These give residual norms and Weinstein-type intervals that most excited-state QSE papers do not report. Reporting per-root residuals and intervals alongside qEOM-style excitation energies would be a differentiator.
- Commutator (qEOM) formulations should be tested within A-CASE specifically to *shrink the bank* for excited-state work, since the DMFT source reports term cancellation.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 source gives excited-state errors at a stated total shot budget for qEOM or QSE on molecules beyond ~12 qubits.
- No reliable source was found on QSE-based linear response (polarizabilities) at matched shots.

## 8. Benchmark systems, metrics, and what a credible "accuracy-matched cost" comparison needs

### Takeaway
By 2026 the field expects four things: (i) chemically hard, *published* Hamiltonians (N2 dissociation; FeS and [2Fe-2S]/[4Fe-4S]; actinide U2; transition-metal open-shell complexes; Heisenberg and Hubbard or Anderson-impurity lattices at 40–100 qubits); (ii) errors against a stated reference (CASCI/FCI in the same active space, DMRG or HCI beyond it) and against the 1.59 mHa chemical-accuracy line; (iii) same-active-space comparisons against strong classical baselines (HCI/CIPSI, CCSD(T), DMRG) at the same determinant or operator count; (iv) multi-seed statistics with a fixed orbital gauge, explicit shot totals, error-mitigation overheads and classical post-processing cost. Hardware scale is now 50–100 qubits for SQD-family methods and 56–80 qubits for expectation-value methods (KQD, shadow QSE). A-CASE has nothing beyond 12 qubits and no hardware run, so it is far behind on scale.

### Cited Findings
- "Chemically decisive benchmarks" (Sundar, Abraham, Peng, Asthana) **[preprint, Jan 2026]** covers N2 (8 qubits), FeS (6e,6o; 12 qubits, quintet), [2Fe-2S] in (4e,4o), (6e,6o) and (8e,6o) with a DMRG (30e,20o) reference, and U2 (6e,6o; spin-free X2C). Metrics are error against CASCI, chemical accuracy 1.59×10^-3 Ha, excitation-rank contribution analysis, and overlaps. Jordan–Wigner Hamiltonians are released openly. The authors warn that reduced active spaces "should not be interpreted as a systematically convergent sequence" — [arXiv 2601.10813](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10813v1)
- Bonilla Vargas proposes a ten-element benchmarking standard for SQD: same-active-space comparisons, fixed orbital gauge, multiple seeds, noise-matched classical controls and reproducible code **[preprint]** — [arXiv 2608.05314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05314)
- Reinholdt et al. use **compactness at matched accuracy** against classical SCI as the decisive metric — [arXiv 2501.07231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07231)
- Hardware scale of the reference points: SQD 77 qubits ([4Fe-4S]) — [Sci. Adv. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05068); SKQD 85 qubits (SIAM) — [arXiv 2501.09702](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09702v3); SqDRIFT 72–100 qubits — [arXiv 2603.08696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08696); KQD 56 sites — [Nat. ### Inferences
- A credible accuracy-matched cost comparison for A-CASE/DA-CASE would report:
  - **total shots** (including the selection cache, not just the final bank) needed to reach a fixed error, such as 1.6 mHa and 0.16 mHa, with seed distributions (median and 90th percentile);
  - the same for QSE, Krylov (Hadamard-test cost counted), ADAPT-VQE (optimizer shots counted) and ADAPT-GCIM at equal targets;
  - **classical post-processing cost** and the Pauli-bank memory;
  - a sample-free classical baseline (CIPSI/HCI determinant count at the same accuracy, or CISD/MR-CISD), since VQSE(all) equals MR-CISD;
  - at least one N2 dissociation curve with non-parallelity error, one open-shell case (FeS quintet or a [2Fe-2S] reduced space from the released Hamiltonians), and one lattice model at ≥20 qubits, where the shadow route makes a wide Pauli bank affordable.
- The project's eight-qubit H4 results are useful as method diagnostics, but they sit well below current expectations. Moving to the openly released 8–12-qubit "chemically decisive" Hamiltonians is a cheap step that allows direct comparison with ADAPT-GCIM's published errors.

### Gaps
- No source was found that already reports shots-to-chemical-accuracy for *both* an expectation-value subspace method and SQD on the same hardware and Hamiltonian.
- No Fe–S or Hubbard result above ~20 qubits was found for any Hadamard-free, operator-generated method other than the shadow-QSE spin-model work.
