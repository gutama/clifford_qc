\noindent\textbf{Reading the table.} A-CASE reaches chemical accuracy on all 4 molecules. CCSD is more accurate on 4 of 4 (LiH, BeH$_2$, HF, H$_2$O) at a small fraction of the cost. The adaptive subspace is not competitive with classical coupled cluster on these systems and is not presented as if it were; what it shows is that the operator-response subspace clears the threshold at small $M$ against a sector two to three orders larger.

\noindent\textbf{The stopping rule is oracle-assisted.} Growth halts on the first step whose error against the \emph{exact} energy falls below threshold, so the $M$ column answers how small the basis can be while clearing the bar --- a property of the selector, not a cost the method could reproduce without knowing the answer.

\noindent$^\dagger$ The complete SD basis spans the entire sector for this molecule, so agreement with exact diagonalization is arithmetic rather than accuracy.
