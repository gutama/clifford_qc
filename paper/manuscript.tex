\begin{abstract}
Adaptive variational eigensolvers (ADAPT-VQE) grow a circuit by repeatedly appending the pool operator with the largest energy gradient, but on hardware that ranking must be inferred from finite measurements, and a wrong choice commits the ansatz before optimization begins. We introduce an operator-centric ADAPT-VQE framework, built on a sparse realization of the complex Clifford algebra $\Cl(2n,\C)\cong M(2^n,\C)$, that makes this selection step measurement-efficient and statistically certified. Every commutator-gradient observable $G_j=-\tfrac{\ii}{2}[H,P_j]$ is stored collectively in one candidate-by-word matrix over the shared Pauli-word set $\calW=\bigcup_j\supp(G_j)$, so all candidate estimates are linear reconstructions from a single cumulative cache; qubit-wise-commuting (QWC) grouping collapses that cache to one circuit per group, and each candidate variance is computed covariance-aware from the joint group statistics (the per-word diagonal underestimates it twofold on correlated candidates). A best-arm selector with simultaneous confidence bounds---asymptotic normal or finite-schedule-valid empirical-Bernstein---returns one of four explicit outcomes and controls the per-call wrong-selection probability: a \emph{strict} selector abstains unless the leader is resolved (a finite-sample certificate under the empirical-Bernstein bound, asymptotic resolution under the normal bound), while a \emph{fallback} run proceeds but labels uncertified choices. Across $25{,}600$ calibration selections the wrong-selection rate is zero in all eight conditions, at $0.9963$--$1.0000$ coverage; certification costs abstention ($14$--$21\%$ for the normal bound, $96$--$100\%$ for the empirical-Bernstein bound). QWC grouping gives a $7\times$ reduction in measured units per step and cuts circuits threefold at fixed accuracy. On molecules, a FAST-inspired determinant-population proxy is intrinsically misaligned with the commutator gradient: on LiH and near-equilibrium H$_4$ its top pick carries at most $1.5\times10^{-5}$ of the maximum gradient (the misalignment lifts only at dissociation), and it plateaus at $33.89$~mHa on the correlated H$_4$ chain---worse than random---whereas an exact-gradient trajectory reaches chemical accuracy after nine operators and ends at $0.392816$~mHa; this H$_4$ run is an exact-gradient baseline, not a certified finite-shot trajectory, and at equilibrium the proxy is $\sim\!400\times$ cheaper. We claim no asymptotic speedup over matrix methods; the contribution is a reproducible, algebraically unified, and statistically calibrated account of when finite-shot adaptive selection can be certified and when proxy bias cannot be repaired by additional measurement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) pairs a parameterized state with a classical optimizer to approximate low-energy eigenstates \cite{peruzzo2014vqe,mcclean2016theory}. Adaptive variants such as ADAPT-VQE grow the ansatz operator by operator, appending at each step the pool generator with the largest instantaneous energy gradient \cite{grimsley2019adapt,tang2021qubitadapt}. Analytic circuit gradients remove finite-difference bias \cite{mitarai2018qcl,schuld2019gradients,wierichs2022general}, but adaptation relocates the difficulty: the pool gradients must themselves be \emph{ranked} from finite measurements, and because a selection permanently fixes the next ansatz slot before any parameter is optimized, a ranking error is not refunded by later reoptimization \cite{scriva2024challenges}. This selection step, not parameter optimization, is the object of the present paper.

Several lines of work attack the measurement cost of selection from complementary directions. Anastasiou \emph{et al.} reduce the number of distinct commuting gradient measurements \cite{anastasiou2023gradients}; the FAST theory of Majland \emph{et al.} replaces commutator gradients by a sampled determinant-population proxy for fermionic chemistry \cite{majland2023fast}; Long \emph{et al.} use layering and subpool exploration to cut processor calls and depth \cite{long2024layering}; and Ikhtiarudin \emph{et al.} reuse Pauli outcomes across candidates and allocate shots by variance \cite{ikhtiarudin2025shot}. What has been missing is a selection procedure that (i) estimates the \emph{exact} commutator gradient rather than a proxy, (ii) reuses every shared measurement globally across the pool, and (iii) attaches an explicit, user-controlled probability that the chosen operator is correct---so that ``the measurement budget was exhausted without resolving the ranking'' is a reported outcome rather than a silent guess.

We provide such a procedure inside a sparse operator-centric realization of the complex Clifford algebra $\Cl(2n,\C)\cong M(2^n,\C)$ \cite{somaroo1998operations,hrdina2022clifford,silva2025operator}, in which states, gates, observables, channels, and selection gradients are all elements of one Pauli-word algebra. The algebraic foundation---the phase-consistent Pauli-word/Clifford-blade dictionary, the distinction between Pauli-word rotations and Spin rotors, and the exact transpose-parity rule that eliminates the even-$Y$ candidate sector for real Hamiltonians---is developed in a companion manuscript \cite{utama2025operatorcentric}; Sec.~\ref{sec:foundation} recalls only what the selection framework needs. The contribution of the present work is fourfold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item a \emph{global commutator bank} with a \emph{covariance-aware} variance: all selection observables are stored in one sparse candidate-by-word matrix, and each candidate's variance is reconstructed from the joint statistics of qubit-wise-commuting measurement groups, correcting the per-word diagonal that would otherwise render the intervals over-confident (Sec.~\ref{sec:bank});
    \item shared-word cumulative caching and QWC grouping, which pay for each Pauli word once per round and collapse the cache to one circuit per commuting group (Sec.~\ref{sec:grouping});
    \item a best-arm selector with simultaneous, selectable asymptotic normal or \emph{finite-schedule-valid} empirical-Bernstein confidence bounds, a four-way explicit outcome, and a stated \emph{certification guarantee} (Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}) that separates strict selection---which abstains unless the best arm is resolved---from \emph{confidence-guided fallback}, and separates a genuine finite-sample certificate (empirical-Bernstein) from asymptotic normal resolution (Sec.~\ref{sec:confidence});
    \item a study whose headline is the empirical \emph{calibration} of that guarantee---wrong-selection probability versus $\delta$, with coverage, regret, abstention, and cost---followed by a strengthened baseline ladder and a molecular analysis, including an infinite-shot ($N\to\infty$) proxy diagnostic that shows the determinant-population proxy fails intrinsically, not from shot noise, wherever correlation matters (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}

We benchmark on the open and periodic transverse-field Ising model (TFIM),
\begin{equation}
H(J,h)=-J\sum_{\langle j,k\rangle}Z_jZ_k-h\sum_{j}X_j,
\label{eq:tfim}
\end{equation}
whose critical point is $h/J=1$ \cite{pfeuty1970ising}; a random-field Ising ensemble; the XXZ chain; and the molecules H$_2$, LiH, BeH$_2$, and the H$_4$ chain. All numerical claims are bounded to $n\le6$ for spin systems and active spaces no larger than the four-electron, four-spatial-orbital ($8$-qubit) H$_4$ model; we claim correctness and qualitative behavior, not favorable asymptotic scaling.

\section{Operator-centric selection observables}
\label{sec:foundation}

\subsection{One algebra for gradients and measurements}

Let $\gamma_0,\dots,\gamma_{2n-1}$ generate $\Cl(2n,\C)$ with $\gamma_a\gamma_b+\gamma_b\gamma_a=2\delta_{ab}\mathbf 1$; the standard irreducible representation gives $\Cl(2n,\C)\cong M(2^n,\C)$. Every $n$-qubit operator is stored sparsely in the Hermitian Pauli-word basis $\calP_n=\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}$,
\begin{equation}
A=\sum_{W\in\calP_n}a_W\,W,\qquad \Tr(A^\dagger B)=2^n\!\sum_W a_W^*b_W,
\label{eq:pauliexp}
\end{equation}
with the Jordan--Wigner strings $\gamma_{2j}=Z_0\cdots Z_{j-1}X_j$, $\gamma_{2j+1}=Z_0\cdots Z_{j-1}Y_j$ providing the exact bridge from anticommuting Clifford generators to local Paulis \cite{jordan1928,utama2025operatorcentric}. Density operators, unitaries, Hermitian observables, and Kraus maps are all elements of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pauliexp}; crucially for what follows, so is every ADAPT selection gradient. The advantage exploited here is not expressivity---$\Cl(2n,\C)$ and $M(2^n,\C)$ are isomorphic---but \emph{uniformity}: a candidate's exact gradient, its finite-shot estimator, and its uncertainty are the same typed object, and shared structure between candidates is visible as shared Pauli words.

\subsection{The selection observable and the odd-$Y$ sector}
\label{sec:oddy}

Appending a candidate rotation $R_P(\theta)=e^{-\ii\theta P/2}$ to the current state $\rho$ has initial energy slope
\begin{equation}
g_P=\left.\partial_\theta \Tr\!\left[HR_P(\theta)\rho R_P^\dagger(\theta)\right]\right|_{\theta=0}=\Tr(G_P\,\rho),\quad G_P=-\tfrac{\ii}{2}[H,P],
\label{eq:gp}
\end{equation}
with $G_P=G_P^\dagger$. Ranking candidates therefore requires no candidate-specific shifted circuits: it requires the current state and the expectation values of the Pauli words appearing in the Hermitian observables $\{G_P\}$. A single structural fact prunes half of the pool exactly. Writing $\nu_Y(W)$ for the number of $Y$ factors in $W$, computational-basis transposition acts as $W^T=(-1)^{\nu_Y(W)}W$, and one has the following \cite{utama2025operatorcentric}.

\begin{proposition}[Transpose-parity selection rule]
\label{prop:oddy}
Let $H$ and $\rho$ be real symmetric. If a Hermitian Pauli word $P$ has even $\nu_Y(P)$, then $\Tr(\rho[H,P])=0$, so $g_P=0$. Moreover, an odd-$Y$ rotation preserves real symmetry, so for a real reference and an odd-$Y$ ansatz every even-$Y$ candidate has zero gradient at every step.
\end{proposition}

Consequently the pool may be restricted to odd-$Y$ words with \emph{no change} to the exact ADAPT trajectory, only a smaller candidate and observable count. This restriction is the first, exact, layer of measurement reduction, and it holds for every real Hamiltonian in this study; it is an algebraic pool symmetry, distinct from---though in the same spirit as---the symmetry-preserving ansatz constructions of Refs.~\cite{yoo2025symmetry} and the explicit Clifford transformations of fermionic operators developed for many-body computation \cite{evangelista2025exact,magoulas2026clifford}. We verify it numerically in Sec.~\ref{sec:results} by confirming that odd-$Y$-restricted and unrestricted pools produce identical exact trajectories.

\section{Measurement-efficient certified selection}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Global commutator bank}
\label{sec:bank}

For a pool $\{P_j\}_{j=1}^{m}$ and a real Hamiltonian $H=\sum_w h_w W_w$, each selection observable expands as $G_j=\sum_{w} c_{jw}W_w$ with real coefficients $c_{jw}$ (Hermiticity of $G_j$ and reality of $H$). Rather than give each candidate its own accumulator, we form the union word set
\begin{equation}
\calW=\bigcup_{j}\supp(G_j)
\label{eq:wordset}
\end{equation}
once, and store the sparse candidate-by-word matrix $C=(c_{jw})$. The point estimate of candidate $j$ is a linear reconstruction from shared per-word means $\widehat m_w$,
\begin{equation}
\widehat g_j=\sum_{w\in\calW}c_{jw}\,\widehat m_w,
\label{eq:bankest}
\end{equation}
computed against a single cache of measurements. Because pool generators differ by local dressing, the $G_j$ overlap heavily: across the TFIM and random-Ising pools the number of per-candidate word slots exceeds $|\calW|$ by a factor that grows with the pool (naive $42$, shared $28$, and only $6$ measured groups for the $n=4$ local pool; Sec.~\ref{sec:res-baselines}), and every unit of that factor is a measurement the shared cache pays for once rather than $m$ times.

\emph{Covariance-aware variance.} The variance of $\widehat g_j$ cannot be taken as the per-word diagonal $\sum_w c_{jw}^2\,\widehat\Var(\widehat m_w)$: words measured together in one QWC group (below) are read from the same shots, so their sample means are correlated, and the diagonal is generally wrong. On the Bell state, for example, the candidate $Z_0+Z_1$ has true variance $4/N$ while the diagonal reports $2/N$, a twofold \emph{under}-estimate that would render the confidence intervals over-confident and void the guarantee of Sec.~\ref{sec:confidence}. We instead compute the variance group by group. Fixing the grouping for the whole selection step makes each group's shots i.i.d.\ and its joint outcome histogram a sufficient statistic; a candidate's per-shot combined value within group $g$,
\begin{equation}
v_{j,g}=\sum_{w\in g}c_{jw}\,o_w\in[-B_{j,g},B_{j,g}],\quad B_{j,g}=\!\sum_{w\in g}|c_{jw}|,
\label{eq:groupval}
\end{equation}
with $o_w=\pm1$ the word's outcome, has an exact sample mean and variance from the histogram, and because distinct groups are independent circuits,
\begin{equation}
\widehat g_j=\sum_g \overline{v_{j,g}},\qquad
\widehat\Var(\widehat g_j)=\sum_g \frac{\widehat s^2_{j,g}}{N_g},
\label{eq:covvar}
\end{equation}
where $\overline{v_{j,g}}$ and $\widehat s^2_{j,g}$ are the group sample mean and variance and $N_g$ the group shot count. Equation~\eqref{eq:covvar} carries the within-group covariance exactly; we verify it reproduces the empirical variance where the diagonal does not.

\subsection{Cumulative caching and QWC grouping}
\label{sec:grouping}

Within a selection step the state is fixed, so all rounds of measurement accumulate into one cumulative cache; escalation adds shots rather than discarding earlier samples, and the reconstruction \eqref{eq:bankest} reads the running totals. To reduce the number of distinct \emph{circuits}, form the finite simple QWC-incompatibility graph whose vertices are the distinct words of $\calW$ and whose edges join non-QWC pairs, then color it once per step by a deterministic largest-degree-first greedy rule. Each color class is a QWC group: two words qubit-wise commute when at every qubit their letters agree or one is the identity, so an entire class is diagonal in one product measurement basis and is read out by a single circuit. The greedy color count is a constructive upper bound on the minimum QWC circuit count, not a claim of chromatic optimality. The cache stores each group's joint outcome histogram (Sec.~\ref{sec:bank}); sampling a group jointly from its rotated-basis computational distribution yields correlated per-word outcomes with the correct marginals, so grouping changes $N_{\text{circuits}}$ and the covariance structure, but not the point estimate \eqref{eq:bankest}. Fixing the partition for the whole step keeps each group's shots i.i.d.\ across rounds, which is what makes the histogram sufficient. This grouping is the mechanism behind the circuit reductions in Sec.~\ref{sec:res-spin}.

\subsection{Simultaneous confidence bounds and the four-way outcome}
\label{sec:confidence}

From the group variances \eqref{eq:covvar} the selector forms, for each active candidate, a two-sided radius $r_j$ on $|\widehat g_j|$. Let $M$ be the fixed candidate family declared before measurement, let $R$ be the number of rounds in the predeclared allocation schedule, and let $q_j$ be the number of QWC groups touched by candidate $j$. The family size $M$ is not reduced after data-dependent elimination. We use one of two selectable forms:
\begin{align}
\text{normal:}\quad & r_j=z_{1-\delta''/2}\,\sqrt{\textstyle\sum_g \widehat s^2_{j,g}/N_g},
\label{eq:rnormal}\\
\text{finite-schedule-valid:}\quad & r_j=\sum_{g=1}^{q_j}\!\Bigl[\sqrt{\tfrac{2\widehat s^2_{j,g}\ln(3/\delta_{j,g})}{N_g}}+\tfrac{6B_{j,g}\ln(3/\delta_{j,g})}{N_g}\Bigr],
\label{eq:reb}
\end{align}
with normal per-test budget $\delta''=\delta/(RM)$ (Bonferroni) and empirical-Bernstein group budget $\delta_{j,g}=\delta/(RMq_j)$. The coefficient $6B_{j,g}$ follows because $v_{j,g}\in[-B_{j,g},B_{j,g}]$ has full range $2B_{j,g}$ and the implemented Audibert--Munos--Szepesv\'ari term is three times that range \cite{audibert2009exploration}. Equation~\eqref{eq:rnormal} is covariance-aware but only asymptotic. Equation~\eqref{eq:reb} is a genuine fixed-$N$ finite-sample bound on each group mean; summing the group radii and union-bounding over all $(r,j,g)$ events makes it valid uniformly over the declared finite schedule (\emph{finite-schedule-valid}). It is not anytime-valid: holding simultaneously over all sample sizes would require a confidence sequence \cite{howard2021timeuniform}, which we do not construct. The selector maintains the intervals $[\,|\widehat g_j|-r_j,\ |\widehat g_j|+r_j\,]$ and applies a best-arm identification rule \cite{evendar2006action,jamieson2014best}: the empirical leader $\hat\jmath$ is \emph{resolved} only when its lower bound strictly exceeds every rival's upper bound,
\begin{equation}
|\widehat g_{\hat\jmath}|-r_{\hat\jmath}\;>\;\max_{k\neq\hat\jmath}\bigl(|\widehat g_k|+r_k\bigr),
\label{eq:resolve}
\end{equation}
and above the gradient threshold. Candidates whose upper bound falls below the leader's lower bound are eliminated (successive elimination). Each selection terminates in exactly one of four states: \textsc{resolved\_best}; \textsc{resolved\_near\_optimal} (leader clears threshold, residual gap within a tolerance $\varepsilon$); \textsc{below\_threshold} (an ADAPT stopping signal); or \textsc{budget\_exhausted\_ambiguous} (ceiling reached, ranking unresolved). The selector never silently returns the empirical maximum while ambiguity remains.

\begin{proposition}[Certification guarantee]
\label{prop:cert}
Consider one call to the \emph{strict empirical-Bernstein selector} at a fixed ansatz state: it makes decisions only at the $R$ rounds of the predeclared schedule, uses Eq.~\eqref{eq:reb} with the fixed-family and group budget above, returns \textsc{resolved\_best} only when Eq.~\eqref{eq:resolve} holds, and otherwise abstains. Assume (A1) shots are independent given the fixed ansatz state; (A2) each candidate's per-group per-shot value \eqref{eq:groupval} lies in the stated bounded range; (A3) every cumulative sample endpoint used for a decision is fixed before those samples are observed; and (A4) the group radii are valid two-sided fixed-sample empirical-Bernstein bounds. Then the probability that this call returns a resolved operator that is not the true $\arg\max_j|g_j|$ is at most $\delta$. For a trajectory-wide budget $\delta_{\rm tot}$ over at most $K$ selections, one may set the per-call budget to $\delta_{\rm tot}/K$ (or predeclare another valid stepwise allocation). The normal selector is not covered by this finite-sample proposition; its $\delta$ is an asymptotic design target.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
A resolution can be wrong only if some candidate's true $|g_j|$ falls outside its radius at some decision round. A candidate miss implies that at least one of its $q_j$ group means misses. Each $(r,j,g)$ event has probability at most $\delta/(RMq_j)$, so the group union costs at most $\delta/(RM)$ for candidate $j$ at round $r$; the union over the fixed $M$ candidates and $R$ rounds gives $\delta$. Data-dependent elimination does not change $M$ or recycle error budget. Ties in $|g_j|$ are never resolved because Eq.~\eqref{eq:resolve} is strict, so an exact symmetry tie produces abstention, not a wrong selection.
\end{proof}

The guarantee separates two operating modes. A strict empirical-Bernstein run enforces Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} and abstains on ambiguity, yielding a statistically certified---but possibly partial---ansatz. A strict normal run also abstains but is labelled asymptotically resolved, not finite-sample certified. A \emph{fallback} run accepts the empirical leader when the budget is exhausted; those selections are reported as confidence-guided, not certified. Exact-gradient choices are labelled exact rather than statistically certified. We label every selection with its status and evidence level, so certified, asymptotic, exact, and heuristic choices are not conflated. The empirical calibration of these distinctions is the headline result of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-calib}.

\subsection{Shot-allocation policies}
\label{sec:allocation}

Allocation policies plan the \emph{additional} shots for the next round on top of the cache; cumulative reuse is structural. We compare (i) \emph{uniform doubling}, which escalates every active word to a predeclared cumulative target that doubles each round---the fixed-endpoint schedule used for finite-sample empirical-Bernstein certification; and (ii) \emph{variance-proportional}, which splits a growing round budget across active words in proportion to their estimated variance contribution $\sum_j c_{jw}^2\,\widehat\Var(\widehat m_w)$. The latter chooses sample counts from earlier outcomes and is therefore used only with the asymptotic normal bound here; certifying it would require a confidence sequence or a union over all attainable endpoints. Both policies use successive elimination and QWC grouping. A uniform-fixed and a random-selection arm serve as the measurement floor and the zero-measurement baseline respectively.

\section{Computational protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The framework is implemented in the dependency-light \texttt{clifford\_qc} package: a NumPy-only algebra and Pauli-rotor intermediate representation, a gate-by-gate exact multivector backend with support tracking, an independent dense reference backend, and a seeded finite-shot backend; the stabilizer backend of App.~\ref{app:stab} uses Stim \cite{gidney2021stim} and the Gottesman--Knill tableau formalism \cite{aaronson2004stabilizer}, and molecular Hamiltonians use OpenFermion \cite{mcclean2020openfermion} with PySCF \cite{sun2018pyscf}. Only ADAPT \emph{selection} consumes shots; parameter reoptimization is exact (adjoint gradients, L-BFGS-B with a deterministic Adam fallback), which isolates ranking noise. Exact diagonalization provides validation targets only.

The calibration study (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-calib}) sweeps $\delta\in\{0.01,0.05,0.10,0.20\}$ and both confidence forms over $16$ unique-argmax instances $\times\,200$ seeds under a capped budget (base $256$, ceiling $64\times$); the baseline ladder (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-baselines}) runs $15$ seeds per arm on the $n=4$ critical TFIM and random-field Ising. Spin experiments otherwise use $J=h=1$ unless the field is swept. Calibration uses dependence-safe Bonferroni allocation over the fixed family and schedule (plus the QWC-group split for empirical Bernstein). The previously committed normal-bound trajectory sweeps retain explicit Sidak intervals as an asymptotic heuristic; no finite-sample claim is attached to them. The selector otherwise uses error budget $\delta=0.05$, near-optimality tolerance $\varepsilon=0.05$, and gradient threshold $10^{-6}$; uniform doubling starts at a base of 256 shots per word and a ceiling of $64\times$; variance-proportional uses a round budget of 4096 with geometric growth over seven rounds. Operator budgets are eight (spin) and twelve (chemistry). The headline spin matrix runs 100 seeds per (model, method) over five $n=4$ families---TFIM at $h\in\{0.5,1.0,1.5\}$, periodic TFIM at $h=1.0$, and the random-field Ising ensemble; an exploratory $n=6$ matrix runs 20 seeds over TFIM, random Ising, and XXZ. Molecular Hamiltonians use STO-3G and Jordan--Wigner mapping. The H$_4$ benchmark is a linear four-hydrogen chain at $0.9$~\AA{} spacing with four electrons in four spatial orbitals ($8$ qubits) and a $160$-word odd-$Y$ qubit-ADAPT pool derived from particle-number- and $S_z$-conserving singles and doubles. The source generators conserve the symmetries, but the independently parameterized word rotors need not; we therefore report $N$, $S_z$, and target-sector leakage. Molecular runs use three seeds per stochastic arm and one deterministic exact-gradient run, and measure operators and shots to chemical accuracy ($1.6\times10^{-3}$~Ha) of the active-space full-configuration-interaction (FCI) energy. Resource accounting is reported separately for total shots $N_{\text{shots}}$, distinct measurement circuits $N_{\text{circuits}}$, unique Pauli words $|\calW|$, appended operators, optimizer evaluations, and peak active support $S_{\max}$. Throughout, $N_{\text{shots}}$ and $N_{\text{circuits}}$ count \emph{selection} measurements only; parameter re-optimization uses exact adjoint gradients and is not charged shots here, so the measurement-efficiency claims concern selection cost under an exact optimizer, not an end-to-end hardware shot budget. Every experiment is seeded and regenerated by a committed one-command script; the replication protocol and predeclared parameters accompany the source.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Calibration of the certification guarantee}
\label{sec:res-calib}

The central claim is Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}: one strict selection call returns the wrong operator with probability at most its per-call budget $\delta$. We test it directly. From random-field Ising Hamiltonians evaluated on generic (tie-broken) states we build selection instances with a known unique $\arg\max_j|g_j|$ separated from the runner-up by a real gap, so ``wrong selection'' is well defined. For each of a grid of nominal budgets $\delta\in\{0.01,0.05,0.10,0.20\}$, each confidence form (normal, empirical-Bernstein), and $200$ measurement seeds per instance ($16$ instances), we run one strict selection under a capped shot budget and record whether it resolved, whether the resolved operator was the true argmax, whether it abstained, the interval coverage, the regret $|g^\star|-|g_{\text{sel}}|$, and the cost.

Figure~\ref{fig:calib} and Table~\ref{tab:calib} report all eight aggregate conditions ($3{,}200$ selections each; $25{,}600$ total). The empirical wrong-selection rate is $0$ for every $\delta$ and both confidence forms; for empirical Bernstein this is consistent with Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}, while the normal rows are an asymptotic comparator. With zero errors in $3{,}200$ trials, the one-sided $95\%$ binomial upper bound is $9.36\times10^{-4}$ for each condition, below the smallest nominal $\delta=0.01$. Interval coverage is $0.9963$--$1.0000$, and both the conditional wrong-selection rate and median regret are zero whenever a selection is made. The guarantee is not free: its price is abstention. The covariance-aware normal bound resolves $78.72$--$86.31\%$ of instances at a median $3.30$--$6.58\times10^4$ shots; the finite-schedule-valid empirical-Bernstein bound, which pays a union-bound budget for every touched QWC group, resolves only $0$--$3.66\%$ and uses $6.76$--$6.96\times10^4$ median shots. A tighter $\delta$ raises abstention monotonically. Finite-sample certification thus converts shot noise into a controlled, reported abstention rate rather than a silent ranking error, and trades directly against how often the selector commits.

Table~\ref{tab:baselines} places the framework against the full ladder of selection strategies on the $n=4$ critical TFIM and the random-field Ising ensemble ($15$ seeds), at a matched eight-operator budget. \subsection{Molecular systems and the proxy--gradient boundary}
\label{sec:res-chem}

For fermionic chemistry a determinant-population proxy is a natural competitor: the FAST theory ranks excitations by sampled Slater-determinant populations rather than commutator gradients \cite{majland2023fast}, at one computational-basis circuit per step. We implement a FAST-inspired selector---scoring each candidate by a population-weighted Hamiltonian-connectivity term plus a determinant-pair coupling term---and compare it against exact-gradient, confidence-guided finite-shot, and random selection on H$_2$, LiH (2e,\,2o), BeH$_2$ (4e,\,3o), and the H$_4$ chain, using the Jordan--Wigner singles-and-doubles odd-$Y$ pool (Fig.~\ref{fig:chem}, Table~\ref{tab:chem}). The confidence-guided arm is run only through six qubits; the H$_4$ comparison contains exact-gradient, proxy, and random arms.

\emph{The proxy failure is intrinsic, not statistical.} To separate proxy bias from shot noise we evaluate the proxy at $N\to\infty$, i.e.\ on the \emph{exact} computational-basis populations, and rank candidates at the Hartree--Fock reference against the exact commutator gradient. On H$_2$ and BeH$_2$ the infinite-shot proxy picks the true $\arg\max_j|g_j|$ (its top pick carries $100\%$ of the maximum gradient). On the correlated systems it does not: its top pick carries only $9.43\times10^{-7}$ of the maximum gradient for LiH (rank $9$ of $12$) and $8.15\times10^{-7}$ for H$_4$ at $0.9$~\AA{} (rank $81$ of $160$). Sweeping the H$_4$ spacing over $0.7$, $0.9$, $1.1$, $1.5$, and $2.0$~\AA{} shows the misalignment is confined to the equilibrium-to-intermediate regime: the proxy's gradient fraction stays below $1.5\times10^{-5}$ from $0.7$ to $1.5$~\AA{}, but at the fully dissociated $2.0$~\AA{} geometry, where a single excitation dominates, the proxy recovers the true $\arg\max$ (fraction $1.0$). The determinant-population signal is therefore intrinsically misaligned with the gradient wherever several excitations compete, and no number of shots removes that proxy bias. The proxy nonetheless \emph{reaches chemical accuracy} on H$_2$, LiH, and BeH$_2$ at equilibrium---not because its ranking is right on LiH (it is not) but because Hartree--Fock is already within chemical accuracy of the small active-space FCI there, so even a misranked excitation that reoptimizes to a negligible contribution leaves the energy accurate.

\emph{Where correlation is essential, the ranking matters.} On the H$_4$ chain, where Hartree--Fock is $56.0569$~mHa above FCI, the misranking is consequential: the FAST-inspired proxy recovers less than half of that correlation energy and plateaus at $33.89$~mHa on all three seeds---more than an order of magnitude short of chemical accuracy and worse than the random arm ($3.55$--$18.30$~mHa). The deterministic exact commutator-gradient trajectory reaches chemical accuracy after nine operators and, after its twelve-operator budget, gives $E=-2.179923798653$~Ha, only $0.392816$~mHa above $E_{\mathrm{FCI}}=-2.180316614324$~Ha (Table~\ref{tab:h4full}). It uses $141$ optimizer evaluations and recovers $99.2993\%$ of the Hartree--Fock-to-FCI correlation energy. The pool contains individually parameterized Pauli words derived from conserving fermionic generators, not the unsplit conserving generators themselves; the final state therefore has small but nonzero leakage: $\langle N\rangle=4.00000001$, $\operatorname{Var}(N)=3.96\times10^{-8}$, $\langle S_z\rangle=1.45\times10^{-5}$, $\operatorname{Var}(S_z)=8.07\times10^{-4}$, and $99.9798\%$ weight in the target $(N=4,S_z=0)$ sector. An independent four-thread rerun reproduced the final energy to $8.9\times10^{-16}$~Ha and every per-step energy to $2.3\times10^{-15}$~Ha; gradient magnitudes, chemical-accuracy step, operator count, and optimizer evaluations also agree. Nine selected labels are alternate representatives of numerical gradient ties, so the word sequence is not claimed to be bitwise invariant under floating-point tie breaking. This result is the exact-gradient quality ceiling and was obtained with zero measurement shots; it is \emph{not} a certified finite-shot H$_4$ trajectory. The selector calibration of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-calib} is a separate per-selection experiment on well-posed spin instances. A full strict finite-shot trajectory on the eight-qubit chain remains unperformed: grouped selection over $160$ candidates is beyond the classical-simulation budget of the present reference implementation. At equilibrium the proxy is $\sim\!410\times$ cheaper than confidence-guided gradient selection on BeH$_2$ (one operator, $4096$ shots versus $1.69\times10^6$), because a single determinant-population circuit suffices when one excitation dominates; on correlated H$_4$ the proxy is far cheaper but selects a numerically negligible-gradient direction.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The operator-centric language earns its place where it makes selection structure explicit. Because every $G_j$ is a Hermitian multivector over the same Pauli basis as $H$, the union word set \eqref{eq:wordset} and the candidate-by-word matrix are immediate, and global measurement reuse is a property of the data structure rather than a bespoke optimization. The transpose-parity rule (Proposition~\ref{prop:oddy}) is likewise a one-line consequence of transposition being diagonal on Pauli words, and it removes half the candidates before any shot is taken. Certification then rides on the same representation: the estimator, its variance, and the simultaneous bounds are computed from the shared cache without a change of type.

The results delineate three regimes. When the pool is expressive and the state is generic, confidence-guided gradient trajectories are decisively better than random at the fixed operator budgets studied (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-spin}); separately, strict single-step selection makes zero errors in the $25{,}600$ calibration trials while reporting unresolved comparisons as abstentions (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-calib}). When a cheap proxy encodes the relevant signal---dominant determinant populations in equilibrium chemistry---the proxy wins on cost by orders of magnitude. When the proxy signal and the true gradient diverge, as in the correlated H$_4$ chain, the proxy fails intrinsically: even at infinite shots its top pick carries less than $10^{-6}$ of the maximum gradient at the reported geometry, while the exact-gradient trajectory recovers $99.2993\%$ of the correlation energy. A practical solver should therefore treat proxy and confidence-bounded gradient selection as complementary and diagnose which regime it is in, rather than committing to one globally. Stabilizer-seeded initialization, which we explored as a fourth route, does not help: scaffold energy fails to predict the non-Clifford correction cost, symmetry alignment does (App.~\ref{app:stab}).

Our framework is complementary to, and composable with, the measurement-reduction literature. Commuting-gradient measurement \cite{anastasiou2023gradients} and Pauli-outcome reuse with variance allocation \cite{ikhtiarudin2025shot} reduce the cost of estimating a fixed set of gradients; the global bank and QWC grouping here extend reuse across the \emph{whole} pool and across allocation rounds; and best-arm racing decides which candidates should keep receiving shots at a fixed ceiling. Layering and subpool exploration \cite{long2024layering} act on a different axis (depth and processor calls) and could be layered on top. A natural next system groups commuting words, reuses compatible outcomes across candidates and iterations, and races candidates under covariance-aware, empirical-Bernstein confidence regions---each ingredient present here in isolation.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The finite-sample budget $\delta$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} is per selector call at one fixed ansatz state. A trajectory-wide guarantee requires a predeclared allocation across ADAPT steps (for example $\delta_{\rm tot}/K$ for at most $K$ calls), which the reported fallback trajectories do not claim.

The numerical claims are deliberately bounded. First, spin experiments stop at $n=6$ and chemistry at the four-electron, four-orbital ($8$-qubit) H$_4$ model; these verify correctness and qualitative behavior but do not establish favorable scaling. We claim no quantum advantage, and the dense eigensolver remains the validation backend. Second, only ADAPT selection is sampled: optimizer shot noise, device decoherence, state-preparation and measurement error, and compilation constraints are absent. Third, the normal radius \eqref{eq:rnormal} is asymptotic and its stated $\delta$ is a design target; only the empirical-Bernstein radius \eqref{eq:reb} carries Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}'s finite-sample guarantee. The latter is markedly more conservative, and both are evaluated only on the discrete decision-round grid, not continuously in time. Fourth, strict certification and unattended ADAPT are partly at odds: because greedy trajectories pass through symmetric decision points, a strict run typically abstains early. The complete spin and chemistry finite-shot trajectories therefore use fallback mode and are confidence-guided, not certified; certification is demonstrated only for strict empirical-Bernstein per-selection decisions. Fifth, no finite-shot H$_4$ trajectory was executed, so Table~\ref{tab:h4full} is an exact-gradient quality ceiling rather than evidence of eight-qubit certification. Its word-level qubit-ADAPT ansatz is derived from conserving fermionic generators but is not itself exactly particle/spin preserving; the measured target-sector leakage is reported rather than hidden. Sixth, correlated estimators such as classical shadows \cite{huang2020shadows} could lower the effective cost further than the QWC grouping exploited here. Seventh, the pools are problem-specific greedy choices; exact reoptimization guarantees a monotone variational energy but cannot refund a poor slot, and the XXZ result shows confidence control does not substitute for an expressivity analysis. Finally, the FAST-inspired arm is a faithful-in-spirit reimplementation, not the original FAST estimator, so the chemistry comparison bounds a mechanism (population proxy versus gradient) rather than a specific published implementation.

\section{Conclusion}

We have built finite-shot ADAPT operator selection as a confidence-controlled statistical procedure inside a sparse operator-centric Clifford algebra, in which all commutator-gradient observables live in one shared Pauli-word basis. A global commutator bank and cumulative cache pay for each shared word once; QWC grouping collapses the cache to one circuit per commuting group, cutting distinct measurement circuits by $3.1$--$4.0\times$ at unchanged accuracy; and a best-arm selector with simultaneous confidence bounds reports an explicit four-way outcome instead of silently guessing. Across $25{,}600$ strict calibration selections, all eight conditions produce zero wrong selections, $0.9963$--$1.0000$ coverage, and the expected abstention--cost trade-off. Across five spin families at $n=4$ (100 seeds), confidence-guided fallback trajectories beat random by two to more than three orders of magnitude at a fixed operator budget, while variance-proportional allocation lowers the ambiguous-step rate. On molecules, the proxy is $\sim\!400\times$ cheaper than gradient selection on equilibrium BeH$_2$ but its infinite-shot top choice on near-equilibrium H$_4$ carries only $8.15\times10^{-7}$ of the maximum gradient; the independently reproduced exact-gradient H$_4$ baseline reaches $0.392816$~mHa after twelve operators. Stabilizer-seeded initialization is reported as a negative result: scaffold energy does not predict the non-Clifford correction cost, symmetry alignment does. Together these results provide a reproducible baseline for measurement-limited adaptive eigensolvers while keeping strict certification, confidence-guided fallback, exact-gradient quality ceilings, and proxy bias as distinct evidentiary claims.

\section{Confidence construction and selection outcomes}
\label{app:confidence}

For each QWC group the cache stores the joint outcome histogram; a word never measured contributes mean $0$ and variance $+\infty$, so untouched candidates stay maximally uncertain and can never be resolved. A candidate's estimate and covariance-aware group variances are Eqs.~\eqref{eq:covvar}, and the two radii are Eqs.~\eqref{eq:rnormal}--\eqref{eq:reb}. Certification and calibration use Bonferroni across the fixed candidate family and $R$ rounds; empirical Bernstein additionally splits each candidate budget across its touched QWC groups. Neither budget is recycled after data-dependent elimination. The legacy normal-bound performance sweeps explicitly request Sidak intervals and are reported only as asymptotic heuristics. A step returns \textsc{below\_threshold} when every candidate's upper bound is under the gradient threshold; \textsc{resolved\_best} when Eq.~\eqref{eq:resolve} holds above threshold; \textsc{resolved\_near\_optimal} when the leader clears threshold and the residual gap to the runner-up is within $\varepsilon$; and \textsc{budget\_exhausted\_ambiguous} otherwise. Only a strict empirical-Bernstein \textsc{resolved\_best} decision is labelled confidence-certified; an exact-gradient selection is labelled exact, not statistically certified, and a normal-bound resolution is labelled asymptotic. A strict run abstains on both \textsc{resolved\_near\_optimal} and \textsc{budget\_exhausted\_ambiguous}; a fallback run accepts either empirical leader and labels it confidence-guided. Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} controls the wrong-selection probability only for the strict empirical-Bernstein form.

\section{Stabilizer-seeded initialization: a negative result}
\label{app:stab}

We record here a negative result that motivated, but does not belong to, the main framework. Clifford-point initialization and stabilizer/residual splitting have been proposed to reduce the quantum work of variational solvers \cite{cheng2025clifford,robin2025stabilizer,yoo2025symmetry}. We tested whether a classically optimized stabilizer scaffold reduces the non-Clifford correction that adaptive selection must supply, using two variants: (A) discrete Clifford-point search over the HVA angles $\{0,\pi/2,\pi,3\pi/2\}$ evaluated on a stabilizer backend \cite{gidney2021stim,aaronson2004stabilizer}, and (B) a sign-consistent commuting-subset stabilizer approximation of $H$ whose ground state is prepared as a Clifford circuit and used as the ADAPT reference. The outcome is negative on every family tested. Variant A is vacuous for this ansatz class: the optimal Clifford point of the TFIM HVA \emph{is} the $\ket{+\cdots+}$ reference, verified by exhaustive enumeration through depth three. Variant B lowers the starting energy substantially---at $n=24$, $h=0.5$ the scaffold energy is $-23.0$ against the reference's $-12.0$, computed in a fraction of a second on the stabilizer backend---yet residual ADAPT from these scaffolds converges \emph{worse}: it reaches gradient plateaus with final relative errors $1.8\times10^{-2}$ to $3.3\times10^{-2}$ on the open and periodic critical TFIM and four of five disorder realizations, against $\le10^{-4}$ for the unseeded baseline. The lone partial positive is the ordered phase, where the $h=0.5$ scaffold reaches $10^{-2}$ relative error in six operators against the baseline's eight. The predictor of correction cost is thus not the scaffold energy but the alignment of the reference with the ground-state symmetry sector: the $X$-field reference sits in the correct parity sector and the $Z$-cluster scaffold does not. The machinery (stabilizer backend, Clifford-point search, stabilizer approximation) is retained as reproducible infrastructure.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{app:repro}

The replication package contains the NumPy-only algebra kernel and Pauli-rotor IR, the exact and finite-shot backends, the commutator bank, cache, confidence, and allocation modules, the selection algorithms, the model builders (spin and molecular), adversarial regression tests, and the committed per-seed benchmark records with their summary tables and figure script. Each benchmark regenerates from a single command with predeclared seeds, budgets, tolerances, and confidence level; long sweeps shard across workers. Figure values are read from the committed records rather than transcribed. The complete calibration rows in Table~\ref{tab:calib} are stored in \path{benchmarks/reference_results/calibration.jsonl}; the H$_4$ summary is the exact row of \path{benchmarks/reference_results/chemistry.jsonl}, and its labels, parameters, per-step energies, gradients, and sector diagnostics are in \path{benchmarks/reference_results/h4_exact_trajectory.json}. The standalone \path{benchmarks/reproduce_exact_h4.py} entry point isolates that deterministic calculation and can atomically write both summary JSONL and a pretty-printed full trajectory, while \path{benchmarks/watch_reproduction.py} launches it beside the $200$-seed calibration and records commands, package versions, heartbeats, logs, exit codes, row counts, and SHA-256 hashes without overwriting reference artifacts. The project repository is at \url{https://github.com/gutama/clifford_qc}; the submission-associated revision is intended to be released publicly as a tagged, archived version.
