\begin{abstract}
Adaptive variational eigensolvers (ADAPT-VQE) grow a circuit by repeatedly appending the pool operator with the largest energy gradient, but on hardware that ranking must be inferred from finite measurements, and a wrong choice commits the ansatz before optimization begins. We introduce a shared-measurement, confidence-controlled operator-selection framework for ADAPT-VQE. All commutator-gradient observables $G_j=-\tfrac{\ii}{2}[H,P_j]$ are stored in one candidate-by-word matrix over the shared Pauli-word set, so every candidate estimate is a linear reconstruction from a single cumulative cache; qubit-wise-commuting (QWC) grouping collapses that cache to one circuit per group, and each candidate variance is reconstructed covariance-aware from the joint group statistics---the per-word diagonal underestimates it twofold on correlated candidates and would void every interval below. A best-arm selector with simultaneous asymptotic-normal or finite-schedule-valid empirical-Bernstein bounds then returns one of four explicit outcomes and controls the per-call wrong-selection probability: a \emph{strict} selector abstains unless the leader is resolved, while a \emph{fallback} run proceeds but labels its choices uncertified. The novelty is this certification layer, not measurement reuse; we report a variance-based reuse baseline that matches or beats our own reuse arms. Three guarantees are proved: an exact-best certificate, an $\varepsilon$-best certificate that resolves the exact gradient ties symmetric ansatz states force, and a scale-free multiplicative certificate. Across $25{,}600$ calibration selections no wrong return occurs in any of eight conditions, bounding the unconditional resolve-and-err probability by $9.4\times10^{-4}$ at $95\%$ confidence; we report the much weaker conditional bound separately. Certification costs abstention---$13.7$--$21.3\%$ (normal) and $96.3$--$100\%$ (empirical-Bernstein) at the calibration ceiling---but a shot-ceiling sweep shows most of that is budget-limited rather than structural: exact ties never resolve at any budget, while clear-gap instances go from $0\%$ to $92\%$ resolution when the ceiling is raised fourfold. The $\varepsilon$-best rule yields trajectories on spin systems and on H$_2$ and LiH whose appended operators are all finite-sample certified, and we report the tolerance's scale ($\varepsilon/\max_j|g_j|=0.21$--$23$ across steps) alongside it. On gap-stratified instances an uncertified fixed-shot selector commits to a non-argmax in $31.5\%$ of near-ties with no signal that it has, while at wider gaps it is exactly as accurate as every escalating arm and cheaper. Global word sharing and QWC grouping cut measured units per step from $42$ to $6$; grouping alone cuts circuit executions $2.8$--$3.6\times$ at unchanged accuracy. On molecules a FAST-inspired determinant-population proxy is intrinsically misaligned with the commutator gradient---its infinite-shot top pick on near-equilibrium H$_4$ carries $8\times10^{-7}$ of the maximum---and plateaus at $33.89$~mHa, above the random arm's median, whereas an exact-gradient baseline reaches $0.393$~mHa. The implementation is a sparse operator-centric realization of the complex Clifford algebra $\Cl(2n,\C)\cong M(2^n,\C)$, which supplies a uniform typed representation rather than additional expressivity. We claim no asymptotic speedup; the contribution is a reproducible, statistically calibrated account of when finite-shot adaptive selection can be certified and when proxy bias cannot be repaired by more measurement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) pairs a parameterized state with a classical optimizer to approximate low-energy eigenstates \cite{peruzzo2014vqe,mcclean2016theory}. Adaptive variants such as ADAPT-VQE grow the ansatz operator by operator, appending at each step the pool generator with the largest instantaneous energy gradient \cite{grimsley2019adapt,tang2021qubitadapt}. Analytic circuit gradients remove finite-difference bias \cite{mitarai2018qcl,schuld2019gradients,wierichs2022general}, but adaptation relocates the difficulty: the pool gradients must themselves be \emph{ranked} from finite measurements, and because a selection permanently fixes the next ansatz slot before any parameter is optimized, a ranking error is not refunded by later reoptimization \cite{scriva2024challenges}. This selection step, not parameter optimization, is the object of the present paper.

Several lines of work attack the measurement cost of selection from complementary directions. Anastasiou \emph{et al.} reduce the number of distinct commuting gradient measurements \cite{anastasiou2023gradients}; Nyk\"anen \emph{et al.} recycle informationally complete energy measurements to reconstruct every commutator gradient without extra shots \cite{nykanen2025ic}; the FAST theory of Majland \emph{et al.} replaces commutator gradients by a sampled determinant-population proxy for fermionic chemistry \cite{majland2023fast}, while Rossi \emph{et al.} rank operators by a reconstructed one-parameter energy landscape rather than the gradient \cite{rossi2026energy}; Long \emph{et al.} use layering and subpool exploration to cut processor calls and depth \cite{long2024layering}; and Ikhtiarudin \emph{et al.} reuse Pauli outcomes across candidates and allocate shots by variance \cite{ikhtiarudin2025shot}. These reduce or reuse the measurements, but none attaches a stated error probability to the resulting choice. What has been missing is a selection procedure that (i) estimates the \emph{exact} commutator gradient rather than a proxy, (ii) reuses every shared measurement globally across the pool, and (iii) attaches an explicit, user-controlled probability that the chosen operator is correct---so that ``the measurement budget was exhausted without resolving the ranking'' is a reported outcome rather than a silent guess.

The delta of the present work is therefore \emph{certification}, not measurement reuse: Ref.~\cite{ikhtiarudin2025shot} already supplies Pauli-outcome reuse and variance-based allocation, and on the spin families studied here that method matches or beats our own reuse arms (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-baselines}). What we add is the statistical layer on top of any such estimator. Concretely the contribution is fivefold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item a \emph{covariance-aware} candidate variance reconstructed from the joint statistics of qubit-wise-commuting measurement groups, correcting the per-word diagonal---which underestimates the variance twofold on correlated candidates---that would otherwise render every interval below over-confident (Sec.~\ref{sec:bank});
    \item a \emph{global commutator bank} with shared-word cumulative caching and QWC grouping: all selection observables live in one sparse candidate-by-word matrix, each Pauli word is paid for once per round, and the cache collapses to one circuit per commuting group (Secs.~\ref{sec:bank}--\ref{sec:grouping});
    \item a best-arm selector with simultaneous, selectable asymptotic normal or \emph{finite-schedule-valid} empirical-Bernstein confidence bounds and a four-way explicit outcome, carrying three stated guarantees---an exact-best certificate (Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}), an $\varepsilon$-best certificate that resolves the exact gradient ties an ADAPT trajectory repeatedly visits (Corollary~\ref{cor:epsbest}), and a scale-free multiplicative certificate that makes the guarantee comparable across gradient scales (Corollary~\ref{cor:multi})---together with explicit abstention and a trajectory-wide budget split (Sec.~\ref{sec:confidence});
    \item an empirical \emph{calibration} of those guarantees, reporting the unconditional and conditional error bounds separately, plus a shot-ceiling sweep that separates structural from budget-limited abstention and a gap-stratified study identifying the regime where an uncertified fixed-shot selector silently fails (Secs.~\ref{sec:res-calib}--\ref{sec:res-hard});
    \item a molecular analysis including an infinite-shot ($N\to\infty$) proxy diagnostic showing that the determinant-population proxy fails intrinsically, not from shot noise, wherever correlation matters (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-chem}).
\end{enumerate}

The implementation is a sparse operator-centric realization of the complex Clifford algebra $\Cl(2n,\C)\cong M(2^n,\C)$ \cite{somaroo1998operations,hrdina2022clifford,silva2025operator}, in which states, gates, observables, channels, and selection gradients are all elements of one Pauli-word algebra. This is an architectural choice, not a source of the statistical results: $\Cl(2n,\C)$ and $M(2^n,\C)$ are isomorphic, so the realization buys \emph{uniformity}---a candidate's exact gradient, its finite-shot estimator, and its uncertainty are one typed object, and shared structure between candidates is visible as shared Pauli words---rather than expressivity, and every operation below can be written in the Pauli basis. The algebraic foundation---the phase-consistent Pauli-word/Clifford-blade dictionary, the distinction between Pauli-word rotations and Spin rotors, and the exact transpose-parity rule that eliminates the even-$Y$ candidate sector for real Hamiltonians---is developed in a companion manuscript \cite{utama2025operatorcentric}; Sec.~\ref{sec:foundation} recalls only what the selection framework needs.

We benchmark on the open and periodic transverse-field Ising model (TFIM),
\begin{equation}
H(J,h)=-J\sum_{\langle j,k\rangle}Z_jZ_k-h\sum_{j}X_j,
\label{eq:tfim}
\end{equation}
whose critical point is $h/J=1$ \cite{pfeuty1970ising}; a random-field Ising ensemble; the XXZ chain; and the molecules H$_2$, LiH, BeH$_2$, and the H$_4$ chain. All numerical claims are bounded to $n\le6$ for spin systems and active spaces no larger than the four-electron, four-spatial-orbital ($8$-qubit) H$_4$ model; we claim correctness and qualitative behavior, not favorable asymptotic scaling.

\section{Operator-centric selection observables}
\label{sec:foundation}

\subsection{One algebra for gradients and measurements}

Let $\gamma_0,\dots,\gamma_{2n-1}$ generate $\Cl(2n,\C)$ with $\gamma_a\gamma_b+\gamma_b\gamma_a=2\delta_{ab}\mathbf 1$; the standard irreducible representation gives $\Cl(2n,\C)\cong M(2^n,\C)$. Every $n$-qubit operator is stored sparsely in the Hermitian Pauli-word basis $\calP_n=\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}$,
\begin{equation}
A=\sum_{W\in\calP_n}a_W\,W,\qquad \Tr(A^\dagger B)=2^n\!\sum_W a_W^*b_W,
\label{eq:pauliexp}
\end{equation}
with the Jordan--Wigner strings $\gamma_{2j}=Z_0\cdots Z_{j-1}X_j$, $\gamma_{2j+1}=Z_0\cdots Z_{j-1}Y_j$ providing the exact bridge from anticommuting Clifford generators to local Paulis \cite{jordan1928,utama2025operatorcentric}. Density operators, unitaries, Hermitian observables, and Kraus maps are all elements of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pauliexp}; crucially for what follows, so is every ADAPT selection gradient. The advantage exploited here is not expressivity---$\Cl(2n,\C)$ and $M(2^n,\C)$ are isomorphic---but \emph{uniformity}: a candidate's exact gradient, its finite-shot estimator, and its uncertainty are the same typed object, and shared structure between candidates is visible as shared Pauli words.

\subsection{The selection observable and the odd-$Y$ sector}
\label{sec:oddy}

Appending a candidate rotation $R_P(\theta)=e^{-\ii\theta P/2}$ to the current state $\rho$ has initial energy slope
\begin{widetext}
\begin{equation}
g_P=\left.\partial_\theta \Tr\!\left[HR_P(\theta)\rho R_P^\dagger(\theta)\right]\right|_{\theta=0}=\Tr(G_P\,\rho),\quad G_P=-\tfrac{\ii}{2}[H,P],
\label{eq:gp}
\end{equation}
\end{widetext}
with $G_P=G_P^\dagger$. Ranking candidates therefore requires no candidate-specific shifted circuits: it requires the current state and the expectation values of the Pauli words appearing in the Hermitian observables $\{G_P\}$. A single structural fact prunes half of the pool exactly. Writing $\nu_Y(W)$ for the number of $Y$ factors in $W$, computational-basis transposition acts as $W^T=(-1)^{\nu_Y(W)}W$, and one has the following \cite{utama2025operatorcentric}.

\begin{proposition}[Transpose-parity selection rule]
\label{prop:oddy}
Let $H$ and $\rho$ be real symmetric. If a Hermitian Pauli word $P$ has even $\nu_Y(P)$, then $\Tr(\rho[H,P])=0$, so $g_P=0$. Moreover, an odd-$Y$ rotation preserves real symmetry, so for a real reference and an odd-$Y$ ansatz every even-$Y$ candidate has zero gradient at every step.
\end{proposition}

Consequently the pool may be restricted to odd-$Y$ words with \emph{no change} to the exact ADAPT trajectory, only a smaller candidate and observable count. This restriction is the first, exact, layer of measurement reduction, and it holds for every real Hamiltonian in this study; it is an algebraic pool symmetry, distinct from---though in the same spirit as---the symmetry-preserving ansatz constructions of Refs.~\cite{yoo2025symmetry} and the explicit Clifford transformations of fermionic operators developed for many-body computation \cite{evangelista2025exact,magoulas2026clifford}. We verify it numerically in Sec.~\ref{sec:results} by confirming that odd-$Y$-restricted and unrestricted pools produce identical exact trajectories.

\section{Measurement-efficient certified selection}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Global commutator bank}
\label{sec:bank}

For a pool $\{P_j\}_{j=1}^{m}$ and a real Hamiltonian $H=\sum_w h_w W_w$, each selection observable expands as $G_j=\sum_{w} c_{jw}W_w$ with real coefficients $c_{jw}$ (Hermiticity of $G_j$ and reality of $H$). Rather than give each candidate its own accumulator, we form the union word set
\begin{equation}
\calW=\bigcup_{j}\supp(G_j)
\label{eq:wordset}
\end{equation}
once, and store the sparse candidate-by-word matrix $C=(c_{jw})$. The point estimate of candidate $j$ is a linear reconstruction from shared per-word means $\widehat m_w$,
\begin{equation}
\widehat g_j=\sum_{w\in\calW}c_{jw}\,\widehat m_w,
\label{eq:bankest}
\end{equation}
computed against a single cache of measurements. Because pool generators differ by local dressing, the $G_j$ overlap heavily: across the TFIM and random-Ising pools the number of per-candidate word slots exceeds $|\calW|$ by a factor that grows with the pool (naive $42$, shared $28$, and only $6$ measured groups for the $n=4$ local pool; Sec.~\ref{sec:res-baselines}), and every unit of that factor is a measurement the shared cache pays for once rather than $m$ times.

\emph{Covariance-aware variance.} The variance of $\widehat g_j$ cannot be taken as the per-word diagonal $\sum_w c_{jw}^2\,\widehat\Var(\widehat m_w)$: words measured together in one QWC group (below) are read from the same shots, so their sample means are correlated, and the diagonal is generally wrong. On the Bell state, for example, the candidate $Z_0+Z_1$ has true variance $4/N$ while the diagonal reports $2/N$, a twofold \emph{under}-estimate that would render the confidence intervals over-confident and void the guarantee of Sec.~\ref{sec:confidence}. We instead compute the variance group by group. Fixing the grouping for the whole selection step makes each group's shots i.i.d.\ and its joint outcome histogram a sufficient statistic; a candidate's per-shot combined value within group $g$,
\begin{equation}
v_{j,g}=\sum_{w\in g}c_{jw}\,o_w\in[-B_{j,g},B_{j,g}],\quad B_{j,g}=\!\sum_{w\in g}|c_{jw}|,
\label{eq:groupval}
\end{equation}
with $o_w=\pm1$ the word's outcome, has an exact sample mean and variance from the histogram, and because distinct groups are independent circuits,
\begin{equation}
\widehat g_j=\sum_g \overline{v_{j,g}},\qquad
\widehat\Var(\widehat g_j)=\sum_g \frac{\widehat s^2_{j,g}}{N_g},
\label{eq:covvar}
\end{equation}
where $\overline{v_{j,g}}$ and $\widehat s^2_{j,g}$ are the group sample mean and variance and $N_g$ the group shot count. Equation~\eqref{eq:covvar} carries the within-group covariance exactly; we verify it reproduces the empirical variance where the diagonal does not.

\subsection{Cumulative caching and QWC grouping}
\label{sec:grouping}

Within a selection step the state is fixed, so all rounds of measurement accumulate into one cumulative cache; escalation adds shots rather than discarding earlier samples, and the reconstruction \eqref{eq:bankest} reads the running totals. To reduce the number of distinct \emph{circuits}, form the finite simple QWC-incompatibility graph whose vertices are the distinct words of $\calW$ and whose edges join non-QWC pairs, then color it once per step by a deterministic largest-degree-first greedy rule. Each color class is a QWC group: two words qubit-wise commute when at every qubit their letters agree or one is the identity, so an entire class is diagonal in one product measurement basis and is read out by a single circuit. The greedy color count is a constructive upper bound on the minimum QWC circuit count, not a claim of chromatic optimality. The cache stores each group's joint outcome histogram (Sec.~\ref{sec:bank}); sampling a group jointly from its rotated-basis computational distribution yields correlated per-word outcomes with the correct marginals, so grouping changes $N_{\text{circuits}}$ and the covariance structure, but not the point estimate \eqref{eq:bankest}. Fixing the partition for the whole step keeps each group's shots i.i.d.\ across rounds, which is what makes the histogram sufficient. This grouping is the mechanism behind the circuit reductions in Sec.~\ref{sec:res-spin}.

\subsection{Simultaneous confidence bounds and the four-way outcome}
\label{sec:confidence}

From the group variances \eqref{eq:covvar} the selector forms, for each active candidate, a two-sided radius $r_j$ on $|\widehat g_j|$. Let $M$ be the fixed candidate family declared before measurement, let $R$ be the number of rounds in the predeclared allocation schedule, and let $q_j$ be the number of QWC groups touched by candidate $j$. The family size $M$ is not reduced after data-dependent elimination. We use one of two selectable forms:
\begin{widetext}
\begin{align}
\text{normal:}\quad & r_j=z_{1-\delta''/2}\,\sqrt{\textstyle\sum_g \widehat s^2_{j,g}/N_g},
\label{eq:rnormal}\\
\text{finite-schedule-valid:}\quad & r_j=\sum_{g=1}^{q_j}\!\Bigl[\sqrt{\tfrac{2\widehat s^2_{j,g}\ln(3/\delta_{j,g})}{N_g}}+\tfrac{6B_{j,g}\ln(3/\delta_{j,g})}{N_g}\Bigr],
\label{eq:reb}
\end{align}
\end{widetext}
with normal per-test budget $\delta''=\delta/(RM)$ (Bonferroni) and empirical-Bernstein group budget $\delta_{j,g}=\delta/(RMq_j)$. The coefficient $6B_{j,g}$ follows because $v_{j,g}\in[-B_{j,g},B_{j,g}]$ has full range $2B_{j,g}$ and the implemented Audibert--Munos--Szepesv\'ari term is three times that range \cite{audibert2009exploration}. Equation~\eqref{eq:rnormal} is covariance-aware but only asymptotic. Equation~\eqref{eq:reb} is a genuine fixed-$N$ finite-sample bound on each group mean; summing the group radii and union-bounding over all $(r,j,g)$ events makes it valid uniformly over the declared finite schedule (\emph{finite-schedule-valid}). It is not anytime-valid: holding simultaneously over all sample sizes would require a confidence sequence \cite{howard2021timeuniform}, which we do not construct. The selector maintains the intervals $[\,|\widehat g_j|-r_j,\ |\widehat g_j|+r_j\,]$ and applies a best-arm identification rule \cite{evendar2006action,jamieson2014best}: the empirical leader $\hat\jmath$ is \emph{resolved} only when its lower bound strictly exceeds every rival's upper bound,
\begin{equation}
|\widehat g_{\hat\jmath}|-r_{\hat\jmath}\;>\;\max_{k\neq\hat\jmath}\bigl(|\widehat g_k|+r_k\bigr),
\label{eq:resolve}
\end{equation}
and above the gradient threshold. Candidates whose upper bound falls below the leader's lower bound are eliminated (successive elimination). Each selection terminates in exactly one of four states: \textsc{resolved\_best} (Eq.~\eqref{eq:resolve}: the leader is certified the exact $\arg\max_j|g_j|$); \textsc{resolved\_eps\_best} (the leader clears every rival's upper bound up to a tolerance $\varepsilon$, $|\widehat g_{\hat\jmath}|-r_{\hat\jmath}\ge\max_{k\neq\hat\jmath}(|\widehat g_k|+r_k)-\varepsilon$, certifying it is within $\varepsilon$ of the maximum; Corollary~\ref{cor:epsbest}); \textsc{below\_threshold} (an ADAPT stopping signal); or \textsc{budget\_exhausted\_ambiguous} (ceiling reached, ranking unresolved). The selector never silently returns the empirical maximum while ambiguity remains, and it records the resolution kind (exact- vs $\varepsilon$-best) separately from the statistical evidence level.

\begin{proposition}[Certification guarantee]
\label{prop:cert}
Consider one call to the \emph{strict empirical-Bernstein selector} at a fixed ansatz state: it makes decisions only at the $R$ rounds of the predeclared schedule, uses Eq.~\eqref{eq:reb} with the fixed-family and group budget above, returns \textsc{resolved\_best} only when Eq.~\eqref{eq:resolve} holds, and otherwise abstains. Assume (A1) shots are independent given the fixed ansatz state; (A2) each candidate's per-group per-shot value \eqref{eq:groupval} lies in the stated bounded range; (A3) every cumulative sample endpoint used for a decision is fixed before those samples are observed; and (A4) the group radii are valid two-sided fixed-sample empirical-Bernstein bounds. Then the probability that this call returns a resolved operator that is not the true $\arg\max_j|g_j|$ is at most $\delta$. For a trajectory-wide budget $\delta_{\rm tot}$ over at most $K$ selections, one may set the per-call budget to $\delta_{\rm tot}/K$ (or predeclare another valid stepwise allocation). The normal selector is not covered by this finite-sample proposition; its $\delta$ is an asymptotic design target.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
See App.~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Certified $\varepsilon$-best selection]
\label{cor:epsbest}
Under the assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}, replace the resolution rule Eq.~\eqref{eq:resolve} by the $\varepsilon$-best rule $|\widehat g_{\hat\jmath}|-r_{\hat\jmath}\ge\max_{k\neq\hat\jmath}(|\widehat g_k|+r_k)-\varepsilon$. Then the probability that a resolved call returns an operator $\hat\jmath$ with $|g_{\hat\jmath}|<\max_k|g_k|-\varepsilon$ is at most $\delta$. The two rules share the same interval event, so the $\varepsilon$-best guarantee costs no additional budget; it weakens \emph{what} is certified---an $\varepsilon$-best operator rather than the exact argmax---while preserving the confidence level. An exact symmetry tie is resolved for any $\varepsilon$ exceeding twice the confidence radius, whereas Eq.~\eqref{eq:resolve} never resolves it.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
See App.~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

The $\varepsilon$-best guarantee is \emph{absolute}: it says the selected gradient is within $\varepsilon$ of the best in the units of $g$, so its practical strength depends on the gradient scale at that step, which varies over a trajectory and across Hamiltonians. The same interval event also supports a scale-free statement.

\begin{corollary}[Multiplicative certificate]
\label{cor:multi}
Under the assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}, suppose the leader satisfies $L_{\hat\jmath}\ge(1-\eta)\max_{k\neq\hat\jmath}U_k$ for some $\eta\in[0,1]$, where $L_j=|\widehat g_j|-r_j$ and $U_j=|\widehat g_j|+r_j$. Then with probability at least $1-\delta$, $|g_{\hat\jmath}|\ge(1-\eta)\max_k|g_k|$. Consequently the smallest tolerance the measured intervals certify at a given step is
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\rm req}=\Bigl[\bigl(\max_{k\neq\hat\jmath}U_k-L_{\hat\jmath}\bigr)\big/\max_{k\neq\hat\jmath}U_k\Bigr]_0^1 ,
\label{eq:etareq}
\end{equation}
with $[\cdot]_0^1$ clipping to $[0,1]$; $\eta_{\rm req}=0$ is an exact-best resolution and $\eta_{\rm req}=1$ carries no nontrivial information.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
See App.~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

We use Eq.~\eqref{eq:etareq} as a reported diagnostic rather than as a third resolution rule: it is computed from the intervals the selector already forms, costs no budget, and gives a dimensionless number comparable across systems and across steps of one trajectory (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-certtraj}, Sec.~\ref{sec:res-ceiling}).

The guarantee separates two operating modes. A strict empirical-Bernstein run enforces Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} and abstains on ambiguity, yielding a statistically certified---but possibly partial---ansatz. It may use the exact-best rule (Eq.~\eqref{eq:resolve}) or the $\varepsilon$-best rule (Corollary~\ref{cor:epsbest}); the latter is what lets a strict trajectory advance through the symmetric, tied-gradient states an ADAPT ansatz repeatedly visits, at the price of certifying an $\varepsilon$-best rather than exact operator (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-certtraj}). A strict normal run also abstains but is labelled asymptotically resolved, not finite-sample certified. A \emph{fallback} run accepts the empirical leader when the budget is exhausted; those selections are reported as confidence-guided, not certified. Exact-gradient choices are labelled exact rather than statistically certified. We label every selection with its status and evidence level, so certified, asymptotic, exact, and heuristic choices are not conflated. The empirical calibration of these distinctions is the headline result of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-calib}.

\subsection{Shot-allocation policies}
\label{sec:allocation}

Allocation policies plan the \emph{additional} shots for the next round on top of the cache; cumulative reuse is structural. We compare (i) \emph{uniform doubling}, which escalates every active word to a predeclared cumulative target that doubles each round---the fixed-endpoint schedule used for finite-sample empirical-Bernstein certification; and (ii) \emph{variance-proportional}, which splits a growing round budget across active words in proportion to their estimated variance contribution $\sum_j c_{jw}^2\,\widehat\Var(\widehat m_w)$. The latter chooses sample counts from earlier outcomes and is therefore used only with the asymptotic normal bound here; certifying it would require a confidence sequence or a union over all attainable endpoints. Both policies use successive elimination and QWC grouping. A uniform-fixed and a random-selection arm serve as the measurement floor and the zero-measurement baseline respectively.

\section{Computational protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The framework is implemented in the dependency-light \texttt{clifford\_qc} package: a NumPy-only algebra and Pauli-rotor intermediate representation, a gate-by-gate exact multivector backend with support tracking, an independent dense reference backend, and a seeded finite-shot backend; the stabilizer backend of App.~\ref{app:stab} uses Stim \cite{gidney2021stim} and the Gottesman--Knill tableau formalism \cite{aaronson2004stabilizer}, and molecular Hamiltonians use OpenFermion \cite{mcclean2020openfermion} with PySCF \cite{sun2018pyscf}. Only ADAPT \emph{selection} consumes shots; parameter reoptimization is exact (adjoint gradients, L-BFGS-B with a deterministic Adam fallback), which isolates ranking noise. Exact diagonalization provides validation targets only.

The calibration study (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-calib}) sweeps $\delta\in\{0.01,0.05,0.10,0.20\}$ and both confidence forms over $16$ unique-argmax instances $\times\,200$ seeds under a capped budget (base $256$, ceiling $64\times$); the baseline ladder (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-baselines}) runs $15$ seeds per arm on the $n=4$ critical TFIM and random-field Ising. The ceiling sweep (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-ceiling}) reuses the same base of $256$ shots but sweeps the cumulative ceiling over $\{64,256,1024,4096,16384\}\times$ at $\delta=0.10$ on $18$ instances ($6$ per stratum) $\times\,60$ seeds, under both resolution rules. The hard-instance study (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-hard}) builds $32$ instances ($8$ per normalized-gap stratum) $\times\,60$ seeds at $\delta=0.10$, $\varepsilon=0.30$, and compares a one-round fixed-shot arm ($4096$ shots per word) against escalating doubling and the two strict rules; its trajectory part runs $15$ seeds of the $n=4$ random-field Ising family at two fixed-shot budgets and two escalating arms. Spin experiments otherwise use $J=h=1$ unless the field is swept. Calibration uses dependence-safe Bonferroni allocation over the fixed family and schedule (plus the QWC-group split for empirical Bernstein). The previously committed normal-bound trajectory sweeps retain explicit Sidak intervals as an asymptotic heuristic; no finite-sample claim is attached to them. The selector otherwise uses error budget $\delta=0.05$ and gradient threshold $10^{-6}$; the certified $\varepsilon$-best trajectories (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-certtraj}) use $\varepsilon=0.30$ and its recalibration sweeps $\varepsilon\in\{0.15,0.30\}$; uniform doubling starts at a base of 256 shots per word and a ceiling of $64\times$; variance-proportional uses a round budget of 4096 with geometric growth over seven rounds. Operator budgets are eight (spin) and twelve (chemistry). The headline spin matrix runs 100 seeds per (model, method) over five $n=4$ families---TFIM at $h\in\{0.5,1.0,1.5\}$, periodic TFIM at $h=1.0$, and the random-field Ising ensemble; an exploratory $n=6$ matrix runs 20 seeds over TFIM, random Ising, and XXZ. Molecular Hamiltonians use STO-3G and Jordan--Wigner mapping. The H$_4$ benchmark is a linear four-hydrogen chain at $0.9$~\AA{} spacing with four electrons in four spatial orbitals ($8$ qubits) and a $160$-word odd-$Y$ qubit-ADAPT pool derived from particle-number- and $S_z$-conserving singles and doubles. The source generators conserve the symmetries, but the independently parameterized word rotors need not; we therefore report $N$, $S_z$, and target-sector leakage. Molecular runs use three seeds per stochastic arm and one deterministic exact-gradient run, and measure operators and shots to chemical accuracy ($1.6\times10^{-3}$~Ha) of the active-space full-configuration-interaction (FCI) energy. \emph{Measurement units.} Comparing a grouped to an ungrouped run requires saying which quantity is being counted, so we fix five units and use them consistently.
\begin{itemize}
\item A \emph{state preparation} (equivalently a \emph{group shot}) is one execution of one measurement circuit: the ansatz is prepared, a basis rotation is applied, and all qubits are read out. This is the physically charged unit on hardware.
\item A \emph{word-shot} is one $\pm1$ reading of one Pauli word. A group shot yields one word-shot for every word in that QWC group simultaneously, so $N_{\text{word-shots}}=\sum_g |g|\,N_g \ge N_{\text{state-preps}}=\sum_g N_g$, with equality only for singleton groups.
\item A \emph{distinct circuit template} is one basis rotation, i.e.\ one word (ungrouped) or one QWC group (grouped); this is the per-step compilation cost.
\item $N_{\text{circuits}}$ counts \emph{circuit executions} accumulated over all rounds and steps of a run---the quantity reported in the tables and figures.
\item $|\calW|$ is the number of unique Pauli words in the shared bank at a step.
\end{itemize}
Throughout, $N_{\text{shots}}$ denotes state preparations. This is what resolves an apparent tension between two comparisons. QWC grouping does not change the \emph{estimator}: the same $|\calW|$ word means are reconstructed from the same effective word-shot counts, so the point estimate \eqref{eq:bankest} and the trajectory accuracy are unchanged. It does change the number of state preparations needed to obtain them, because one group shot serves every word in the group instead of one. At the $n=4$ local pool the deterministic per-round plan is $28$ words versus $6$ groups, a factor $4.7$ in state preparations at matched per-word coverage; the realized trajectory factor is smaller ($2.8$--$3.2\times$, Sec.~\ref{sec:res-spin}) because the grouped and ungrouped runs take different numbers of escalation rounds and eliminate candidates at different points. Where an earlier version of this manuscript reported grouping as leaving the shot count ``unchanged,'' that statement was reading a word-shot count against a state-preparation count; all tables here are regenerated under the single convention above.

Resource accounting is reported separately for $N_{\text{shots}}$, $N_{\text{circuits}}$, $|\calW|$, appended operators, optimizer evaluations, and peak active support $S_{\max}$. Both $N_{\text{shots}}$ and $N_{\text{circuits}}$ count \emph{selection} measurements only; parameter re-optimization uses exact adjoint gradients and is not charged shots here, so the measurement-efficiency claims concern selection cost under an exact optimizer, not an end-to-end hardware shot budget. Every experiment is seeded and regenerated by a committed one-command script; the replication protocol and predeclared parameters accompany the source.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Calibration of the certification guarantee}
\label{sec:res-calib}

The central claim is Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}: one strict selection call returns the wrong operator with probability at most its per-call budget $\delta$. We test it directly. From random-field Ising Hamiltonians evaluated on generic (tie-broken) states we build selection instances with a known unique $\arg\max_j|g_j|$ separated from the runner-up by a real gap, so ``wrong selection'' is well defined. For each of a grid of nominal budgets $\delta\in\{0.01,0.05,0.10,0.20\}$, each confidence form (normal, empirical-Bernstein), and $200$ measurement seeds per instance ($16$ instances), we run one strict selection under a capped shot budget and record whether it resolved, whether the resolved operator was the true argmax, whether it abstained, the interval coverage, the regret $|g^\star|-|g_{\text{sel}}|$, and the cost.

Figure~\ref{fig:calib} and Table~\ref{tab:calib} report all eight aggregate conditions ($3{,}200$ selections each; $25{,}600$ total). The empirical wrong-selection rate is $0$ for every $\delta$ and both confidence forms; for empirical Bernstein this is consistent with Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}, while the normal rows are an asymptotic comparator. Interval coverage is $0.9963$--$1.0000$, and both the conditional wrong-selection rate and median regret are zero whenever a selection is made.

\emph{What the zero-error count does and does not bound.} Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} controls the \emph{unconditional} event $\Pr(\text{resolved and wrong})\le\delta$; an abstention is a non-occurrence of that event, and counting it as such is what makes the $3{,}200$-trial denominator the right one. No wrong return was observed in $3{,}200$ calls per condition, giving a one-sided $95\%$ Clopper--Pearson upper bound of $9.36\times10^{-4}$ on that unconditional probability---below the smallest nominal $\delta=0.01$---in every one of the eight conditions. The same count does \emph{not} bound the error rate conditional on resolving, $\Pr(\text{wrong}\mid\text{resolved})$, to that precision, because the strict selector abstains on most calls and the conditional denominator is correspondingly small. We therefore report both columns of Table~\ref{tab:calib}: the normal rows resolve $2{,}520$--$2{,}761$ of $3{,}200$ calls, so zero errors give conditional bounds of $1.1$--$1.2\times10^{-3}$, while the empirical-Bernstein rows resolve only $0$, $18$, $65$, and $116$ calls at $\delta=0.01$, $0.05$, $0.10$, and $0.20$, giving conditional bounds of (undefined), $0.153$, $0.045$, and $0.025$. The headline ``zero wrong selections in $25{,}600$ calls'' is therefore a statement about the unconditional event the proposition bounds; the conditional statement is much weaker at the empirical-Bernstein resolution rates reported here, and Sec.~\ref{sec:res-ceiling} raises those rates by raising the ceiling.

The guarantee is not free: its price is abstention. The covariance-aware normal bound resolves $78.75$--$86.28\%$ of instances at a median $3.30$--$6.58\times10^4$ shots; the finite-schedule-valid empirical-Bernstein bound, which pays a union-bound budget for every touched QWC group, resolves only $0$--$3.63\%$ and uses $6.76$--$6.96\times10^4$ median shots. A tighter $\delta$ raises abstention monotonically. Finite-sample certification thus converts shot noise into a controlled, reported abstention rate rather than a silent ranking error, and trades directly against how often the selector commits.

\subsection{Certified $\varepsilon$-best trajectories}
\label{sec:res-certtraj}

The calibration above certifies \emph{one} selection; a full ADAPT trajectory is a sequence of them, and the exact-best rule cannot certify such a sequence. The obstruction is structural: at a symmetric ansatz state several pool operators share the top gradient---for the open TFIM reference, the $12$-operator local pool splits exactly into six operators attaining $\max_j|g_j|=1$ (the nearest-neighbor words $Y_jZ_{j+1}$ and $Z_jY_{j+1}$) and six with exactly zero gradient ($Y_j$ and $Z_{j-1}Y_jZ_{j+1}$), with no intermediate values---so Eq.~\eqref{eq:resolve} faces an exact tie it never resolves at any shot budget. Because an ADAPT ansatz begins at, and repeatedly returns to, such states, a strict exact-best run abstains at operator one; the empirical-Bernstein rows of Table~\ref{tab:calib}, which resolve only $0$--$3.63\%$ of even the generic unique-argmax instances, are the single-step signature of the same effect. No trajectory is then simultaneously useful and certified: a fallback run reaches low energy but certifies no step, and the strict run that certifies stalls immediately.

The $\varepsilon$-best rule of Corollary~\ref{cor:epsbest} removes the obstruction. It commits the empirical leader once the leader's lower bound clears every rival's upper bound up to $\varepsilon$, certifying---on the same interval event, at no extra budget---that the selected operator's gradient is within $\varepsilon$ of the maximum. An exact tie is then resolved, since any tied operator is $\varepsilon$-best, and the strict run proceeds. Table~\ref{tab:certtraj} reports strict $\varepsilon$-best runs under the empirical-Bernstein bound in which \emph{every appended operator is finite-sample certified}, with a trajectory-wide budget $\delta_{\rm tot}=0.10$ split as $\delta_{\rm tot}/K$ and $\varepsilon=0.30$. Each reaches $10^{-6}$--$10^{-5}$ relative energy error on the spin systems and chemical accuracy on H$_2$ and LiH.

\emph{What ``certified trajectory'' does and does not cover.} Each run ends by abstaining---the residual gradients at the terminal step are $9\times10^{-4}$ to $5\times10^{-3}$ (spin) and $5\times10^{-10}$ to $1\times10^{-3}$ (molecules), too small for the budget to separate from zero at the required confidence---and that terminal abstention is a reported \textsc{budget\_exhausted\_ambiguous} outcome, not a certified \textsc{below\_threshold} stop. We therefore claim \emph{fully certified appended-operator sequences}, not a certificate covering the stopping decision. The stronger phrase would require the terminating step to clear the threshold test at confidence, which none of these runs does; certifying termination as well is left open.

\emph{How strong is $\varepsilon=0.30$?} The $\varepsilon$-best guarantee is absolute, so we report the gradient scale at every certified step (Table~\ref{tab:epsscale}). The tolerance is a modest fraction of the leading gradient early in a trajectory ($\varepsilon/\max_j|g_j|=0.21$--$0.47$ on the first three spin steps) and exceeds it later, once the leading gradient has itself fallen ($2.35$, $2.61$ on TFIM steps four and five; $1.65$ on H$_2$; $23.1$ on LiH, where $\max_j|g_j|=0.013$). At those steps the nominal statement ``within $0.30$ of the best'' is weaker than the whole gradient range and carries little information on its own. The realized selections are nevertheless far better than the nominal certificate: $11$ of the $12$ appended operators are in fact the exact $\arg\max_j|g_j|$ by post-hoc evaluation, and the single exception (random-field Ising, step four) has rank two with a relative shortfall $(\max_j|g_j|-|g_{\rm sel}|)/\max_j|g_j|=0.216$; every other appended step has relative shortfall exactly zero. The scale-free diagnostic of Corollary~\ref{cor:multi} makes the same point without reference to $\varepsilon$: the median multiplicative tolerance the intervals support at these budgets is $\eta_{\rm req}\approx0.15$--$0.23$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-ceiling}), i.e.\ a certificate of the form $|g_{\rm sel}|\ge0.77$--$0.85\,\max_k|g_k|$. Reporting $\eta_{\rm req}$ alongside $\varepsilon$ is what makes the certificate comparable across the very different gradient scales of a spin chain and a molecular active space.

We recalibrate wrong selection under the $\varepsilon$-best definition of correctness, $|g_{\rm sel}|\ge\max_k|g_k|-\varepsilon$, the quantity Corollary~\ref{cor:epsbest} bounds. It differs from the exact-argmax criterion of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-calib}: on a tied instance an $\varepsilon$-best selection is correct even when it is not the unique argmax. Over $32$ instances built for this regime---symmetric (exact-tie) reference states, displaced generic states, and actual exact-ADAPT trajectory states---each with $60$ seeds, across both bounds, $\delta\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}$, and $\varepsilon\in\{0.15,0.30\}$, the empirical $\varepsilon$-best violation rate is $0$ in all twelve conditions, with a one-sided $95\%$ Clopper--Pearson upper bound of $1.7$--$2.0\times10^{-3}$ conditional on resolving---below every nominal $\delta$---and it remains $0$ (upper bound $\le1.4\times10^{-2}$) in the ``tight'' stratum where a sub-$\varepsilon$ candidate sits within $0.10$ of the $\varepsilon$-boundary. The $\varepsilon$-best rule resolves $80$--$92\%$ of these instances at $0.995$--$1.000$ coverage, against the $0$--$3.6\%$ the exact-best rule resolves on the harder unique-argmax set. The recalibration is not cosmetic: $20$--$27\%$ of the resolved selections are \emph{not} the exact argmax, so the exact-argmax metric of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-calib} would miscount roughly one resolved selection in four as an error on precisely the tied states the $\varepsilon$-best rule is built for.

Two limitations bound the claim. First, the certificate is genuine but loose: resolving a tie needs $\varepsilon$ larger than about twice the confidence radius, so the conservative empirical-Bernstein radius requires $\varepsilon\gtrsim0.15$ here, and the guarantee is ``within $\varepsilon$ of optimal,'' not ``optimal.'' Second, the rule resolves exact ties but not every degeneracy: the $n=4$ XXZ chain still stalls at $\varepsilon=0.30$, its reference-state gradients being distributed rather than exactly tied. The eight-qubit H$_4$ trajectory remains an exact-gradient baseline (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-chem}); certifying it is obstructed not by the tie problem but by the cost of \emph{simulating} finite-shot grouped measurement over $160$ candidates on a growing $n=8$ state---a property of the reference simulator, not of the method.

\subsection{What the shot ceiling buys: structural versus budget-limited abstention}
\label{sec:res-ceiling}

Table~\ref{tab:calib} reports $96.4$--$100\%$ abstention for the strict empirical-Bernstein selector, but at one ceiling ($64\times$ the $256$-shot base). That number alone cannot distinguish three causes: an exact tie the exact-best rule can never resolve; a real but small gap that more shots would resolve; and a gap large enough that only the conservatism of the union-bounded radius stands in the way. We separate them by sweeping the cumulative ceiling over $64\times$, $256\times$, $1024\times$, $4096\times$, and $16384\times$ on three explicitly constructed strata---exact ties at symmetric TFIM reference states, unique argmaxes with gap below $0.03$, and unique argmaxes with gap above $0.03$---under both the exact-best and the $\varepsilon$-best rule (Table~\ref{tab:ceiling}).

The three causes separate cleanly, and none of them is bound conservatism.

\emph{Exact ties are structural.} Under the exact-best rule the tied stratum resolves $0\%$ of calls at \emph{every} ceiling up to $16384\times$---a cumulative $4.2\times10^{6}$ shots per group---exactly as Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} predicts: the strict inequality \eqref{eq:resolve} cannot separate two candidates with identical $|g_j|$ no matter how narrow the intervals become. The terminal radius does shrink as it should, from $0.092$ to $0.0053$, which confirms the estimator is working and the abstention is a property of the question, not of the bound. The $\varepsilon$-best rule resolves the same stratum at $83\%$ already at the calibration ceiling and $100\%$ from $256\times$ on, at a median $8.2\times10^4$ shots.

\emph{Most of the reported abstention is budget-limited, not structural.} The clear-gap stratum---generic unique-argmax instances of the kind Table~\ref{tab:calib} uses---resolves $0\%$ at the calibration ceiling of $64\times$ but $91.9\%$ at $256\times$ and $100\%$ from $1024\times$, at a median $2.7\times10^5$ shots. The $96.4$--$100\%$ empirical-Bernstein abstention of Table~\ref{tab:calib} is therefore largely an artifact of the ceiling chosen there: raising it fourfold converts almost all of it into certified selections. That $2.7\times10^5$ is the shot price of exact-best finite-sample certification on a well-separated instance---about four times the median cost at which the asymptotic normal bound resolves the comparable instances of Table~\ref{tab:calib}, though the two instance sets are not identical and the comparison is indicative rather than matched.

\emph{Small gaps are expensive but not impossible.} The small-gap stratum still resolves $0\%$ through $4096\times$ and only $16.7\%$ at $16384\times$, where the median resolving run has spent $1.7\times10^{7}$ shots---two orders of magnitude more than the clear-gap stratum. Exact-best certification of a near-tie is thus available in principle and impractical in practice, which is the quantitative case for the $\varepsilon$-best rule rather than a larger budget.

\emph{The multiplicative certificate does not tighten with budget here.} Among $\varepsilon$-best resolutions, $\eta_{\rm req}$ sits at $0.16$--$0.22$ and is essentially flat in the ceiling, because the rule fires at the first round where the intervals clear $\varepsilon$ and then stops---so the reported $\eta$ is the tolerance at that early round, not the best attainable. A run that instead spent its full budget would report a smaller $\eta$; the flatness is a property of the stopping rule, not a limit on the certificate. No wrong return occurred in any of the $30$ conditions, under either rule and either correctness criterion.

Selection noise is simply not the accuracy bottleneck for these families at this operator budget---the same conclusion the $n=6$ matrix reaches from the other direction (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-spin})---so the certified machinery buys a statistical guarantee, not a lower energy. What the stratified study establishes is where the guarantee is worth having: wherever near-ties are common, an uncertified selector is wrong roughly a third of the time and cannot tell you so.

\subsection{Baselines and the cost of reuse}
\label{sec:res-baselines}

Table~\ref{tab:baselines} places the framework against the full ladder of selection strategies on the $n=4$ critical TFIM and the random-field Ising ensemble ($15$ seeds), at a matched eight-operator budget.

\emph{Deterministic plan accounting.} The per-step measurement plan is exact and independent of sampling. Measuring each candidate's observable independently costs $42$ word-measurements (\emph{naive}); the global bank collapses these to $28$ shared unique words---a $42/28=1.5\times$ reduction from \emph{global word sharing} alone; and the specified greedy QWC coloring covers those $28$ words with $6$ measured groups---a further $28/6=4.7\times$ from \emph{grouping}. The two together give $42/6=7\times$ fewer measured units per step, and it is worth attributing them separately: sharing is the smaller factor here and grouping the larger, which is the opposite of what a single ``$7\times$ from grouping'' headline would suggest. Six is an achieved greedy group count, not a proof of chromatic minimality.

\emph{The ladder.} Exact-gradient selection is the quality ceiling at zero shots ($1.5\times10^{-15}$ at criticality, $4.1\times10^{-5}$ on the disordered ensemble where the pool limits the reachable state); random selection is the floor ($2$--$3\times10^{-2}$). The comparison that matters here is unfavourable to the elaborate arms, and we state it plainly: a \emph{single fixed-shot round} is the cheapest finite-shot arm ($1.9\times10^5$ state preparations, $46$ circuit executions) and matches the best accuracy on both families ($5.1\times10^{-5}$ and $4.1\times10^{-5}$), so on this ladder no escalating or certified arm buys accuracy. What it lacks is adaptivity, abstention, and any statement about its own reliability; Sec.~\ref{sec:res-hard} locates the regime---near-ties---where that lack costs a third of its selections. Among the reuse arms, grouping cuts circuit executions $3.1\times$ ($928\to299$) and state preparations $2.8\times$ ($1.86\times10^6\to6.6\times10^5$) without loss; variance-proportional reuse (the reuse-and-variance-allocation family \cite{ikhtiarudin2025shot}) reaches the exact-gradient ceiling on the disordered family ($4.1\times10^{-5}$ against our shared-grouped $1.7\times10^{-4}$) at fewer circuits, which is why we do not claim reuse as the contribution (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}). The strict and fallback rows make the abstention trade-off concrete: fallback completes the eight-operator trajectory, whereas the strict exact-best row, forbidden from guessing, abstains at the first reflection-symmetric tie on all $15$ seeds (the $\varepsilon$-best rule of Corollary~\ref{cor:epsbest} resolves such ties; Sec.~\ref{sec:res-certtraj}). The normal-bound strict row is asymptotically resolved rather than finite-sample certified; the latter label is reserved for strict empirical-Bernstein decisions in Secs.~\ref{sec:res-calib}--\ref{sec:res-hard}.

\subsection{Molecular systems and the proxy--gradient boundary}
\label{sec:res-chem}

For fermionic chemistry a determinant-population proxy is a natural competitor: the FAST theory ranks excitations by sampled Slater-determinant populations rather than commutator gradients \cite{majland2023fast}, at one computational-basis circuit per step. We implement a FAST-inspired selector---scoring each candidate by a population-weighted Hamiltonian-connectivity term plus a determinant-pair coupling term---and compare it against exact-gradient, confidence-guided finite-shot, and random selection on H$_2$, LiH (2e,\,2o), BeH$_2$ (4e,\,3o), and the H$_4$ chain, using the Jordan--Wigner singles-and-doubles odd-$Y$ pool (Fig.~\ref{fig:chem}, Table~\ref{tab:chem}). Two scoping caveats apply throughout. First, this arm reproduces the determinant-population \emph{mechanism} of FAST, not the original estimator of Majland \emph{et al.}\ \cite{majland2023fast} nor its natural excitation-operator pool: all arms share the word-level qubit-ADAPT pool for a controlled comparison, so what follows bounds the mechanism (a population proxy versus the commutator gradient) and is not an indictment of any specific FAST implementation. Second, the confidence-guided arm is run only through six qubits; the H$_4$ comparison contains exact-gradient, proxy, and random arms.

\emph{The proxy failure is intrinsic, not statistical.} To separate proxy bias from shot noise we evaluate the proxy at $N\to\infty$, i.e.\ on the \emph{exact} computational-basis populations, and rank candidates at the Hartree--Fock reference against the exact commutator gradient. On H$_2$ and BeH$_2$ the infinite-shot proxy picks the true $\arg\max_j|g_j|$ (its top pick carries $100\%$ of the maximum gradient). On the correlated systems it does not: its top pick carries only $9.43\times10^{-7}$ of the maximum gradient for LiH (rank $9$ of $12$) and $8.15\times10^{-7}$ for H$_4$ at $0.9$~\AA{} (rank $81$ of $160$). Sweeping the H$_4$ spacing over $0.7$, $0.9$, $1.1$, $1.5$, and $2.0$~\AA{} shows the misalignment is confined to the equilibrium-to-intermediate regime: the proxy's gradient fraction stays below $1.5\times10^{-5}$ from $0.7$ to $1.5$~\AA{}, but at the fully dissociated $2.0$~\AA{} geometry, where a single excitation dominates, the proxy recovers the true $\arg\max$ (fraction $1.0$). The determinant-population signal is therefore intrinsically misaligned with the gradient wherever several excitations compete, and no number of shots removes that proxy bias. The proxy nonetheless \emph{reaches chemical accuracy} on H$_2$, LiH, and BeH$_2$ at equilibrium---not because its ranking is right on LiH (it is not) but because Hartree--Fock is already within chemical accuracy of the small active-space FCI there, so even a misranked excitation that reoptimizes to a negligible contribution leaves the energy accurate.

\emph{Where correlation is essential, the ranking matters.} On the H$_4$ chain, where Hartree--Fock is $56.0569$~mHa above FCI, the misranking is consequential: the FAST-inspired proxy recovers less than half of that correlation energy and plateaus at $33.89$~mHa---identically on all three seeds, the proxy ranking being deterministic at this sampling budget---more than an order of magnitude short of chemical accuracy and worse than the random arm, whose three seeds give $3.55$, $14.26$, and $18.30$~mHa (median $14.26$). The deterministic exact commutator-gradient trajectory reaches chemical accuracy after nine operators and, after its twelve-operator budget, gives $E=-2.179923798653$~Ha, only $0.392816$~mHa above $E_{\mathrm{FCI}}=-2.180316614324$~Ha (Table~\ref{tab:h4full}). It uses $141$ optimizer evaluations and recovers $99.2993\%$ of the Hartree--Fock-to-FCI correlation energy. The pool contains individually parameterized Pauli words derived from conserving fermionic generators, not the unsplit conserving generators themselves; the final state therefore has small but nonzero leakage: $\langle N\rangle=4.00000001$, $\operatorname{Var}(N)=3.96\times10^{-8}$, $\langle S_z\rangle=1.45\times10^{-5}$, $\operatorname{Var}(S_z)=8.07\times10^{-4}$, and $99.9798\%$ weight in the target $(N=4,S_z=0)$ sector. An independent four-thread rerun reproduced the final energy to $8.9\times10^{-16}$~Ha and every per-step energy to $2.3\times10^{-15}$~Ha; gradient magnitudes, chemical-accuracy step, operator count, and optimizer evaluations also agree. Nine selected labels are alternate representatives of numerical gradient ties, so the word sequence is not claimed to be bitwise invariant under floating-point tie breaking. This result is the exact-gradient quality ceiling and was obtained with zero measurement shots; it is \emph{not} a certified finite-shot H$_4$ trajectory. The selector calibration of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-calib} is a separate per-selection experiment on well-posed spin instances. A full strict finite-shot trajectory on the eight-qubit chain remains unperformed: grouped selection over $160$ candidates is beyond the classical-simulation budget of the present reference implementation. At equilibrium the proxy is $\sim\!410\times$ cheaper than confidence-guided gradient selection on BeH$_2$ (one operator, $4096$ shots versus $1.69\times10^6$), because a single determinant-population circuit suffices when one excitation dominates; on correlated H$_4$ the proxy is far cheaper but selects a numerically negligible-gradient direction.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The operator-centric language earns its place where it makes selection structure explicit. Because every $G_j$ is a Hermitian multivector over the same Pauli basis as $H$, the union word set \eqref{eq:wordset} and the candidate-by-word matrix are immediate, and global measurement reuse is a property of the data structure rather than a bespoke optimization. The transpose-parity rule (Proposition~\ref{prop:oddy}) is likewise a one-line consequence of transposition being diagonal on Pauli words, and it removes half the candidates before any shot is taken. Certification then rides on the same representation: the estimator, its variance, and the simultaneous bounds are computed from the shared cache without a change of type.

The results delineate three regimes. When the pool is expressive and the state is generic, confidence-guided gradient trajectories are decisively better than random at the fixed operator budgets studied (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-spin}); separately, strict single-step selection makes zero errors in the $25{,}600$ calibration trials while reporting unresolved comparisons as abstentions (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-calib}). When a cheap proxy encodes the relevant signal---dominant determinant populations in equilibrium chemistry---the proxy wins on cost by orders of magnitude. When the proxy signal and the true gradient diverge, as in the correlated H$_4$ chain, the proxy fails intrinsically: even at infinite shots its top pick carries less than $10^{-6}$ of the maximum gradient at the reported geometry, while the exact-gradient trajectory recovers $99.2993\%$ of the correlation energy. A practical solver should therefore treat proxy and confidence-bounded gradient selection as complementary and diagnose which regime it is in, rather than committing to one globally.

A fourth regime is where certification earns its keep, and it is narrower than we would have guessed before running Sec.~\ref{sec:res-hard}. When the top-two gradient gap is comfortable, every estimator we tried---including a single fixed-shot round---returns the exact argmax, and the certified machinery buys a guarantee about an answer nobody was going to get wrong. When the gap is tight, the uncertified selector is wrong roughly a third of the time and, crucially, cannot say which third; that is the failure mode a stated error probability and an explicit abstention are for. Because greedy adaptive trajectories pass through symmetric states by construction, tight gaps are not a pathological corner of the input space but a recurring feature of the algorithm, which is why the $\varepsilon$-best rule---weakening the claim rather than the confidence---is the practical form of the guarantee.

\emph{What is new here is certification, not reuse.} Measurement reuse is by now a well-developed idea, and Table~\ref{tab:baselines} makes the point against our own arms: the variance-based reuse family of Ikhtiarudin \emph{et al.} \cite{ikhtiarudin2025shot} matches or beats our shared-grouped arm on both spin families, at a comparable circuit count. We therefore do \emph{not} claim reuse as the contribution. Ref.~\cite{ikhtiarudin2025shot} supplies Pauli-outcome reuse across candidates, variance-based shot allocation, and the resulting empirical measurement savings. The delta of the present work is orthogonal to all three: (i) joint QWC statistics with a covariance-aware candidate variance, without which the per-word diagonal underestimates the variance twofold on correlated candidates and every interval below would be void; (ii) simultaneous confidence control over a declared candidate family and a declared finite schedule; (iii) an exact-best guarantee (Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}), an $\varepsilon$-best guarantee (Corollary~\ref{cor:epsbest}), and a scale-free multiplicative one (Corollary~\ref{cor:multi}); (iv) explicit abstention as a reported outcome instead of a silent guess; (v) a trajectory-wide confidence allocation; and (vi) evidence labels that keep certified, asymptotic, exact, and heuristic choices distinct in every table. In short, the earlier work makes the same measurements go further; this work says---with a stated error probability---whether the resulting ranking may be trusted, and refuses to answer when it may not.

The framework is otherwise complementary to, and composable with, the measurement-reduction literature. Commuting-gradient measurement \cite{anastasiou2023gradients} and informationally complete measurement that recycles energy shots into every gradient \cite{nykanen2025ic} reduce the cost of estimating a fixed set of gradients; the global bank and QWC grouping here extend reuse across the \emph{whole} pool and across allocation rounds. Alternative selection signals---the energy-landscape reconstruction of Rossi \emph{et al.} \cite{rossi2026energy} or the determinant-population proxy studied here---are orthogonal to the certification machinery, which applies to any score linear in Pauli expectations. Layering and subpool exploration \cite{long2024layering} act on a different axis (depth and processor calls) and could be layered on top. A natural next system groups commuting words, reuses compatible outcomes across candidates and iterations, and races candidates under covariance-aware, empirical-Bernstein confidence regions---each ingredient present here in isolation.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The finite-sample budget $\delta$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} is per selector call at one fixed ansatz state. A trajectory-wide guarantee requires a predeclared allocation across ADAPT steps (for example $\delta_{\rm tot}/K$ for at most $K$ calls), which the reported fallback trajectories do not claim.

The numerical claims are deliberately bounded. First, spin experiments stop at $n=6$ and chemistry at the four-electron, four-orbital ($8$-qubit) H$_4$ model; these verify correctness and qualitative behavior but do not establish favorable scaling. We claim no quantum advantage, and the dense eigensolver remains the validation backend. Second, only ADAPT selection is sampled: optimizer shot noise, device decoherence, state-preparation and measurement error, and compilation constraints are absent. Third, the normal radius \eqref{eq:rnormal} is asymptotic and its stated $\delta$ is a design target; only the empirical-Bernstein radius \eqref{eq:reb} carries Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}'s finite-sample guarantee. The latter is markedly more conservative, and both are evaluated only on the discrete decision-round grid, not continuously in time. Fourth, strict exact-best certification and unattended ADAPT are partly at odds: because greedy trajectories pass through symmetric decision points where several operators share the top gradient, a strict exact-best run abstains at the first such state. The $\varepsilon$-best rule (Corollary~\ref{cor:epsbest}) resolves these ties and yields the fully certified appended-operator sequences of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-certtraj}, at the cost of certifying an operator within $\varepsilon$ of optimal rather than the exact argmax; the tolerance must exceed roughly twice the confidence radius, and non-tie degeneracies (e.g.\ the $n=4$ XXZ chain) can still stall. Those runs also terminate on an uncertified abstention, so the \emph{stopping} decision is not covered by any certificate: a fully certified trajectory in the strongest sense would additionally require a certified \textsc{below\_threshold} outcome, which none of the runs here achieves. And $\varepsilon=0.30$ is a weak statement wherever the leading gradient is small---at six of the twelve certified steps $\varepsilon$ exceeds $\max_j|g_j|$ (Table~\ref{tab:epsscale})---so the certificate should always be read together with its normalized scale or with $\eta_{\rm req}$. Fifth, no finite-shot H$_4$ trajectory was executed, so Table~\ref{tab:h4full} is an exact-gradient quality ceiling rather than evidence of eight-qubit certification. Its word-level qubit-ADAPT ansatz is derived from conserving fermionic generators but is not itself exactly particle/spin preserving; the measured target-sector leakage is reported rather than hidden. Sixth, correlated or informationally complete estimators such as classical shadows \cite{huang2020shadows} or optimized IC-POVM gradients \cite{nykanen2025ic} could lower the effective cost further than the QWC grouping exploited here, and composing them with the certification machinery is left to future work. A related and more immediately attractive extension is to replace the fixed-endpoint empirical-Bernstein interval by an anytime-valid confidence sequence---a betting or e-value construction for bounded observations \cite{howard2021timeuniform}---which would remove assumption (A3), permit adaptive stopping, and in particular restore finite-sample validity to variance-proportional allocation. We flag that this is not a drop-in substitution: a candidate estimate here sums several independently sampled QWC-group streams, so a rigorous construction needs either a valid combination of group-level e-processes, a direct e-process for each candidate's linear combination, importance-weighted observations under adaptive group selection, or a multivariate confidence sequence. We have not built or validated any of these, and we do not claim the resulting rates; we report the extension as the natural next step rather than as an available option. Seventh, certification did not buy lower energies at the scales studied: on the shot-matched trajectory comparison (Table~\ref{tab:hardtraj}) a single fixed-shot round is the most accurate and the cheapest arm, and the strict $\varepsilon$-best run ends higher because it abstains. Selection noise is not the accuracy bottleneck for these small, expressive-pool systems, so what the framework delivers here is a stated error probability and an explicit non-answer, not a better variational energy; whether the guarantee also buys accuracy at scales where selection errors compound is untested. Eighth, the pools are problem-specific greedy choices; exact reoptimization guarantees a monotone variational energy but cannot refund a poor slot, and the XXZ result shows confidence control does not substitute for an expressivity analysis. Finally, the FAST-inspired arm is a faithful-in-spirit reimplementation, not the original FAST estimator, so the chemistry comparison bounds a mechanism (population proxy versus gradient) rather than a specific published implementation.

\section{Conclusion}

We have built finite-shot ADAPT operator selection as a confidence-controlled statistical procedure. A global commutator bank and cumulative cache pay for each shared Pauli word once, QWC grouping collapses the cache to one circuit per commuting group---cutting circuit executions by $2.8$--$3.6\times$ and state preparations by $2.5$--$3.1\times$ at unchanged accuracy---and a covariance-aware variance keeps the resulting intervals honest where the per-word diagonal would not. On top of that estimator, a best-arm selector with simultaneous confidence bounds reports an explicit four-way outcome instead of silently guessing, and carries three certificates: exact-best, $\varepsilon$-best, and a scale-free multiplicative one. Across $25{,}600$ strict calibration selections no condition produced a wrong return, bounding the unconditional resolve-and-err probability by $9.4\times10^{-4}$; the conditional bound is much weaker where the strict selector rarely resolves, and we report both. A ceiling sweep shows that most of that abstention is budget-limited rather than structural---clear-gap instances reach $92\%$ exact-best resolution at four times the calibration ceiling, while exact ties never resolve at any budget---and the $\varepsilon$-best rule converts the remaining structural abstention into fully certified appended-operator sequences on the spin systems and on H$_2$ and LiH, whose terminal stopping decision is nonetheless not certified. On gap-stratified instances a fixed-shot selector commits to a non-argmax in $31.5\%$ of near-tie selections with nothing to signal it, while the strict selector abstains or certifies; on the easy, wide-gap instances that dominate the usual baseline ladder, the same fixed-shot selector is as accurate as anything else and much cheaper. Across five spin families at $n=4$ (100 seeds), confidence-guided trajectories beat random by two to more than three orders of magnitude at a fixed operator budget, while variance-proportional allocation lowers the ambiguous-step rate by $27$--$48\%$ at $2.2$--$2.7\times$ the shots. On molecules, the determinant-population proxy is $\sim\!410\times$ cheaper than gradient selection on equilibrium BeH$_2$ but its infinite-shot top choice on near-equilibrium H$_4$ carries only $8.15\times10^{-7}$ of the maximum gradient; the independently reproduced exact-gradient H$_4$ baseline reaches $0.393$~mHa after twelve operators. Together these results provide a reproducible baseline for measurement-limited adaptive eigensolvers while keeping strict certification, confidence-guided fallback, exact-gradient quality ceilings, and proxy bias as distinct evidentiary claims.

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

Throughout, $M$ is the candidate family declared before any measurement, $R$ the number of decision rounds of the predeclared allocation schedule, $q_j$ the number of QWC groups touched by candidate $j$, and $N_g^{(r)}$ the cumulative shot count of group $g$ at round $r$---a quantity fixed by the schedule before those shots are drawn (assumption A3). Write $\mu_{j,g}=\mathbb E[v_{j,g}]$ for the population mean of the per-shot combined value \eqref{eq:groupval}, so that $g_j=\sum_{g}\mu_{j,g}$ exactly, and let $\widehat s^2_{j,g}$ be the sample variance with the $1/N$ (biased) convention used by the implementation, which is the convention under which the Audibert--Munos--Szepesv\'ari bound is stated \cite{audibert2009exploration}.

\emph{Group-level bound.} Fix $(r,j,g)$. The $N_g^{(r)}$ shots of group $g$ are i.i.d.\ given the fixed ansatz state (A1) because the QWC partition is fixed for the whole selection step, and $v_{j,g}\in[-B_{j,g},B_{j,g}]$ (A2) with range $2B_{j,g}$. The empirical-Bernstein inequality then gives, for any $\delta_{j,g}\in(0,1)$,
\begin{equation}
\Pr\Bigl[\bigl|\overline{v_{j,g}}-\mu_{j,g}\bigr|>\rho_{j,g}\Bigr]\le\delta_{j,g},\ \
\rho_{j,g}=\sqrt{\tfrac{2\widehat s^2_{j,g}\ln(3/\delta_{j,g})}{N_g^{(r)}}}+\tfrac{6B_{j,g}\ln(3/\delta_{j,g})}{N_g^{(r)}} .
\label{eq:ebgroup}
\end{equation}
The $\ln(3/\delta)$ and the factor $3$ multiplying the range in the second term are the two-sided form: the one-sided statement holds at $\ln(3/\delta)$ with a $3\times$range coefficient, and the standard union over the two tails is already absorbed into the constant $3$ inside the logarithm, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:ebgroup} is two-sided at level $\delta_{j,g}$ as written. With range $2B_{j,g}$ the coefficient is $3\cdot 2B_{j,g}=6B_{j,g}$, which is the term appearing in Eq.~\eqref{eq:reb}. (A4) is exactly the statement that Eq.~\eqref{eq:ebgroup} holds at each decision round with the scheduled $N_g^{(r)}$.

\emph{Candidate-level bound.} Because distinct QWC groups are measured by distinct circuits and are therefore independent, $\widehat g_j=\sum_g\overline{v_{j,g}}$ and $g_j=\sum_g\mu_{j,g}$, so on the intersection of the $q_j$ group events, $|\widehat g_j-g_j|\le\sum_g\rho_{j,g}=r_j$ with $r_j$ as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:reb}. Setting $\delta_{j,g}=\delta/(RMq_j)$ and union-bounding over the $q_j$ groups,
\begin{equation}
\Pr\bigl[\,|\widehat g_j-g_j|>r_j \text{ at round } r\,\bigr]\le q_j\cdot\frac{\delta}{RMq_j}=\frac{\delta}{RM}.
\label{eq:candbound}
\end{equation}
Since $\bigl||\widehat g_j|-|g_j|\bigr|\le|\widehat g_j-g_j|$, the same $r_j$ bounds the deviation of $|\widehat g_j|$ from $|g_j|$, so $|g_j|\in[L_j,U_j]$ with $L_j=\max(0,|\widehat g_j|-r_j)$ and $U_j=|\widehat g_j|+r_j$.

\emph{Simultaneity.} Let $E$ be the event that $|g_j|\in[L_j,U_j]$ for every $j\in M$ at every round $r\le R$. Union-bounding Eq.~\eqref{eq:candbound} over the $M$ candidates and $R$ rounds gives $\Pr[E^c]\le RM\cdot\delta/(RM)=\delta$. Two accounting points make this valid under the selector's adaptivity. First, the family size in $\delta_{j,g}$ is the \emph{declared} $M$, not the post-elimination active-set size; successive elimination therefore never recycles budget, and the union above covers every candidate that could have been examined. Second, decisions are taken only at the $R$ predeclared cumulative endpoints (A3), so no optional-stopping correction is needed---the bound is valid uniformly over the declared finite schedule but is not anytime-valid.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}]
Work on $E$, which has probability at least $1-\delta$. Suppose the selector returns \textsc{resolved\_best} at some round with leader $\hat\jmath$, so $L_{\hat\jmath}>\max_{k\neq\hat\jmath}U_k$ over the active set, and every candidate eliminated earlier satisfied $U_k<L_{\hat\jmath'}$ for the leader $\hat\jmath'$ of that earlier round, whence $|g_k|\le U_k<L_{\hat\jmath'}\le|g_{\hat\jmath'}|\le\max_i|g_i|$ and $k$ was not a maximizer. For any surviving $k\neq\hat\jmath$, $|g_k|\le U_k<L_{\hat\jmath}\le|g_{\hat\jmath}|$. Hence $\hat\jmath=\arg\max_j|g_j|$ and the maximizer is unique. A wrong resolved return therefore requires $E^c$, so its probability is at most $\delta$. Splitting $\delta_{\rm tot}$ as $\delta_{\rm tot}/K$ over at most $K$ calls and union-bounding over calls gives the trajectory statement. Finally, if two candidates have $|g_j|=|g_k|$ exactly then $L_{\hat\jmath}>U_k$ is impossible on $E$ (it would force $|g_{\hat\jmath}|>|g_k|$), so an exact tie yields abstention, never a wrong return.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:epsbest}]
Work on $E$. Suppose the selector returns \textsc{resolved\_eps\_best} with leader $\hat\jmath$, so $L_{\hat\jmath}\ge\max_{k\neq\hat\jmath}U_k-\varepsilon$ over the active set; as above, every eliminated $k$ satisfies $|g_k|<\max_i|g_i|$ and in fact $|g_k|\le U_k<L_{\hat\jmath}\le|g_{\hat\jmath}|$, so eliminated candidates cannot witness a violation. Then
\begin{equation*}
|g_{\hat\jmath}|\ \ge\ L_{\hat\jmath}\ \ge\ \max_{k\neq\hat\jmath}U_k-\varepsilon\ \ge\ \max_{k\neq\hat\jmath}|g_k|-\varepsilon ,
\end{equation*}
and since trivially $|g_{\hat\jmath}|\ge|g_{\hat\jmath}|-\varepsilon$, we get $|g_{\hat\jmath}|\ge\max_k|g_k|-\varepsilon$. A violation therefore requires $E^c$, of probability at most $\delta$. The event $E$ is the same one used in Proposition~\ref{prop:cert}---the radii, the family size, and the round count are unchanged---so the $\varepsilon$-best guarantee consumes no additional budget. If $|g_j|=|g_k|$ for the top set, any member is $\varepsilon$-best for every $\varepsilon\ge0$, and the rule fires as soon as $\max_{k\neq\hat\jmath}U_k-L_{\hat\jmath}\le\varepsilon$, which for a tie means $\varepsilon\gtrsim r_{\hat\jmath}+\max_k r_k$, i.e.\ roughly twice the confidence radius.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:multi}]
Work on $E$ and write $U^\star=\max_{k\neq\hat\jmath}U_k$. If $L_{\hat\jmath}\ge(1-\eta)U^\star$ then $|g_{\hat\jmath}|\ge L_{\hat\jmath}\ge(1-\eta)U^\star\ge(1-\eta)\max_{k\neq\hat\jmath}|g_k|$, and $|g_{\hat\jmath}|\ge(1-\eta)|g_{\hat\jmath}|$ holds trivially for $\eta\ge0$, so $|g_{\hat\jmath}|\ge(1-\eta)\max_k|g_k|$. Eliminated candidates are handled as in the previous proof. Rearranging $L_{\hat\jmath}\ge(1-\eta)U^\star$ for $U^\star>0$ gives $\eta\ge(U^\star-L_{\hat\jmath})/U^\star$, whose clipped value is Eq.~\eqref{eq:etareq}; the complementary event again has probability at most $\delta$.
\end{proof}

For the asymptotic normal radius \eqref{eq:rnormal} none of the above is a finite-sample statement: Eq.~\eqref{eq:ebgroup} is replaced by a central-limit approximation, and the per-test budget $\delta''=\delta/(RM)$ makes $\delta$ a design target rather than a proven bound.

\section{Confidence construction and selection outcomes}
\label{app:confidence}

For each QWC group the cache stores the joint outcome histogram; a word never measured contributes mean $0$ and variance $+\infty$, so untouched candidates stay maximally uncertain and can never be resolved. A candidate's estimate and covariance-aware group variances are Eqs.~\eqref{eq:covvar}, and the two radii are Eqs.~\eqref{eq:rnormal}--\eqref{eq:reb}. Certification and calibration use Bonferroni across the fixed candidate family and $R$ rounds; empirical Bernstein additionally splits each candidate budget across its touched QWC groups. Neither budget is recycled after data-dependent elimination. The legacy normal-bound performance sweeps explicitly request Sidak intervals and are reported only as asymptotic heuristics. A step returns \textsc{below\_threshold} when every candidate's upper bound is under the gradient threshold; \textsc{resolved\_best} when Eq.~\eqref{eq:resolve} holds above threshold; \textsc{resolved\_eps\_best} when the leader clears threshold and the residual gap to the runner-up is within $\varepsilon$; and \textsc{budget\_exhausted\_ambiguous} otherwise. These four names are the literal values of the implementation's \texttt{SelectionStatus} enumeration, and the same strings appear in the committed per-step records, so the manuscript, the pseudocode, and the artifact use one vocabulary.

The commit rule depends on the mode, and only on the mode:
\begin{itemize}
\item In \emph{strict exact-best} mode the selector commits only on \textsc{resolved\_best}, with the finite-sample guarantee of Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} under the empirical-Bernstein radius.
\item In \emph{strict $\varepsilon$-best} mode it commits on \textsc{resolved\_best} \emph{or} \textsc{resolved\_eps\_best}, with Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} and Corollary~\ref{cor:epsbest} supplying the respective guarantees.
\item Both strict modes abstain on \textsc{budget\_exhausted\_ambiguous}, which ends the trajectory without appending an operator.
\item A valid \textsc{below\_threshold} outcome terminates ADAPT---the usual gradient-norm stopping criterion---also without appending an operator.
\item A \emph{fallback} run accepts the empirical leader on \textsc{budget\_exhausted\_ambiguous} as well, and labels that choice confidence-guided rather than certified.
\end{itemize}
Certification level is recorded separately from resolution kind: an exact-gradient selection is labelled exact rather than statistically certified, a normal-bound resolution is labelled asymptotic, and only a strict empirical-Bernstein \textsc{resolved\_best} or \textsc{resolved\_eps\_best} decision is labelled finite-sample certified. Proposition~\ref{prop:cert} and Corollary~\ref{cor:epsbest} control the wrong-selection probability only for those two.

Each record also carries the post-hoc strength diagnostics used in Sec.~\ref{sec:res-certtraj}: the selected operator's exact-gradient rank, the step's true top-two gap, and $\eta_{\rm req}$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:multi}.

\section{Stabilizer-seeded initialization: a negative result}
\label{app:stab}

We record here a negative result that motivated, but does not belong to, the main framework. Clifford-point initialization and stabilizer/residual splitting have been proposed to reduce the quantum work of variational solvers \cite{cheng2025clifford,robin2025stabilizer,yoo2025symmetry}. We tested whether a classically optimized stabilizer scaffold reduces the non-Clifford correction that adaptive selection must supply, using two variants: (A) discrete Clifford-point search over the HVA angles $\{0,\pi/2,\pi,3\pi/2\}$ evaluated on a stabilizer backend \cite{gidney2021stim,aaronson2004stabilizer}, and (B) a sign-consistent commuting-subset stabilizer approximation of $H$ whose ground state is prepared as a Clifford circuit and used as the ADAPT reference. The outcome is negative on every family tested. Variant A is vacuous for this ansatz class: the optimal Clifford point of the TFIM HVA \emph{is} the $\ket{+\cdots+}$ reference, verified by exhaustive enumeration through depth three. Variant B lowers the starting energy substantially---at $n=24$, $h=0.5$ the scaffold energy is $-23.0$ against the reference's $-12.0$, computed in a fraction of a second on the stabilizer backend---yet residual ADAPT from these scaffolds converges \emph{worse}: it reaches gradient plateaus with final relative errors $1.8\times10^{-2}$ to $3.3\times10^{-2}$ on the open and periodic critical TFIM and four of five disorder realizations, against $\le10^{-4}$ for the unseeded baseline. The lone partial positive is the ordered phase, where the $h=0.5$ scaffold reaches $10^{-2}$ relative error in six operators against the baseline's eight. The predictor of correction cost is thus not the scaffold energy but the alignment of the reference with the ground-state symmetry sector: the $X$-field reference sits in the correct parity sector and the $Z$-cluster scaffold does not. The machinery (stabilizer backend, Clifford-point search, stabilizer approximation) is retained as reproducible infrastructure.
