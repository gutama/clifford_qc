\begin{abstract}
Quantum subspace methods differ less in the eigenproblem they solve than in
what a machine must \emph{do} to fill it: which state/parameter contexts are
visited, how many measurement settings and shots are executed there, which
primitive supplies the off-diagonal entries, and whether a new physical
quantity requires a new experiment.  We develop an
architecture in which these answers are fixed by one design choice.  In the
Dyadic Adaptive Clifford-Algebra Subspace Eigensolver (DA-CASE), basis directions are
virtual operators $A_i\ket{\psi}$ acting on a single reference, so every
overlap, Hamiltonian, and observable element reduces to Pauli expectations on
the same $\rho$ and is served from one cached bank.  Several consequences are
measured rather than asserted.  First, the basis uses one reference context
regardless of its size.  On a matched H$_4$ contract, the corresponding exact
workflow has $1$ state-evaluation context for DA-CASE, $8$ or $16$ distinct
generating-function states, and $90$ ADAPT-VQE selection/optimizer contexts;
the last number is \emph{not} a physical preparation count.  At the shot-level
primitive, DA-CASE's $14401$-word selection cache groups into $1672$ QWC
settings once, whereas ADAPT-VQE's $2424$-word selection union is $452$
settings repeated over eight changing states, or $3616$ setting-evaluations.
The generating-function arms additionally require $36/28$ and $136/120$
unique Hamiltonian/overlap state pairs.  Second, because the basis is generated
by operators, generator
resolution is an exposed dial: the same retained subspace---identical energy to
$4\times10^{-16}$ Ha, identical $M=9$ and $\kappa_S=1$, all nine principal
angles zero---costs $7371$ measured words from determinant excitations and
$2240$ from the $160$ words they decompose into, a factor of $3.3$, and a
factor of $6$ on a Hubbard plaquette ($2519\to413$).  At word resolution the
bank is narrower than the Krylov comparator's $4224$, reversing the width
comparison obtained at determinant resolution; the saving is paid in
selection, $1252$ candidate scorings against $180$.  Third, one bank serves
several quantities: the same $63$ words yield energy, a projected
staggered-spin observable, and Lehmann response without materializing a Ritz
state, and grouped resampling of that shared bank propagates finite-shot
variability through the entire nonlinear pipeline.  Holding system, observable,
word set, group count, shot budget, and seeds fixed and varying only the
generator family, acceptance falls from $200/200$ at $\kappa_S=1.02$ to
$139/200$ four orders of conditioning later, with a $\chi(0)$ interval roughly
fifteen times wider.  A reference-conditioned sector test now certifies the
entire nine-dimensional word-resolution span with zero leakage, so the width
saving no longer requires disabling the default filter; post-selecting a
sector-mixed ADAPT warm start onto its declared sector extends the same
certificate to the warm-started arm at a $1.00012\times$ shot overhead, and
improves it from $0.342$ to $0.199$ mHa.  In a separate
equal-shot diagnostic, covariance-aware group allocation reduces the summed
projected-matrix variance by $68.9\%$; a shot-calibrated overlap cutoff read
per mode removes all $>0.1$ Ha outliers and cuts RMSE $22\times$ against the
fixed cutoff, at a factor-of-two cost in median error.  A
measurement-side dyadic hierarchy supplies a fourth
dial: requiring commutation only within blocks of $1,2,4,$ or $8$ qubits
interpolates between QWC and full commuting measurements.  On the retained
$7371$-word determinant DA-CASE bank, the constructive setting count falls
$913\to647\to238\to64$; exact tableau synthesis exposes the corresponding
logical-CX depth rather than treating the reduction as free.  We state the
architecture's costs with equal weight: it
is not the most accurate arm at matched budget, its selection stage reads an
order of magnitude more words than it measures, and its sampling-fed dressing
supplies no advantage over a sample-independent selected-CI control at matched
dimension.  The contribution is a resource profile and a strict, digest-bound
interchange boundary, not an accuracy or scaling claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A materials calculation reaches a correlated solver only after a long
reduction.  Density-functional theory, Wannierization, constrained screening,
and embedding each remove degrees of freedom or redefine interactions
\cite{marzari2012wannier,pizzi2020wannier90,aryasetiawan2004crpa,georges1996dmft}.
What arrives at the end of that chain is an effective many-body Hamiltonian in
an interchange file, not a quantum circuit.  Variational and subspace quantum
algorithms, by contrast, are usually assessed on a qubit Hamiltonian already in
memory \cite{peruzzo2014vqe,mcclean2017qse,huggins2020nonorthogonal}.  A solver
that intends to sit at that junction must therefore make its input contract as
explicit as its eigensolver: orbital order, electron number, spin sector,
energy units, one- and two-body conventions, provenance, and the reference
determinant are part of the scientific result, not configuration.

Quantum subspace diagonalization (QSD) is attractive at this boundary because
it replaces a deep optimized circuit with matrix elements in a small,
nonorthogonal subspace, solved as
\begin{equation}
    H_{\rm sub}c = E S c .
    \label{eq:gep-intro}
\end{equation}
Quantum subspace expansion, nonorthogonal VQE, and quantum Krylov methods
instantiate this with different basis families
\cite{mcclean2017qse,huggins2020nonorthogonal,stair2020krylov}; iterative and
residual-driven variants pursue compactness
\cite{tkachenko2024davidson,oleary2025partitioned}; QSCI and its adaptive
descendants select determinant subspaces from sampled configurations
\cite{kanno2026qsci,nakagawa2024adaptqsci}.  IQAE enlarges a Pauli moment space
\cite{bharti2021iqae}, Q-SENSE exchanges circuit depth for seniority-structured
elements \cite{patel2026qsense}, and AS-SQD uses a finite-shot perturbative
acquisition rule \cite{miura2026assqd}.  ADAPT-GCIM selects UCC/Givens
generating functions adaptively and solves a nonorthogonal generalized
eigenproblem \cite{zheng2024adaptgcim}.

Our starting observation is that this literature is normally compared along one
axis---energy error at a stated basis size $M$---while the axis that binds on
hardware is architectural.  Two methods can solve the same
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gep-intro} at the same $M$ and still differ in whether the
off-diagonal entries come from expectations on a single state, from transition
measurements between distinct prepared states, or from an ancilla test.  Those
are different machines' bottlenecks.  They are also invisible in $M$.

This work therefore develops DA-CASE---\emph{Dyadic Adaptive Clifford-Algebra
Subspace Eigensolver}---as an architecture rather than as a scoreboard entry.  The
design commitment is a single reference density operator $\rho$ with virtual
basis directions $A_i\ket{\psi}$: no distinct state is prepared per $i$.  Every
matrix element reduces to Pauli-word expectations on that one $\rho$, and the
resulting cache is reused for energy, projected observables, and response
alike.  The complex Clifford algebra
$\Cl(2n,\mathbb C)\cong M(2^n,\mathbb C)$ supplies the typed operator
representation, with Jordan--Wigner strings mapping fermionic modes to local
qubit Paulis \cite{utama2026operatorcentric}.  This is a representation choice.
It is not additional expressivity and not a speedup claim.  ``Dyadic'' names
the nested $k=1,2,4,\ldots,n$ measurement-compatibility hierarchy introduced
below; it does not modify the generalized-eigenproblem or adaptive selector.

The contribution is fivefold, and each item is a measured resource property
rather than a performance claim.
\begin{itemize}
 \item \emph{One reference context, any $M$.}  We separate state-evaluation
 contexts from physical shot-level preparations, count QWC settings at every
 changing-state selection step, and show that the closest adaptive
 generalized-eigenproblem method additionally
 requires a measurement primitive---unique $H/S$ state pairs---that a
 single-reference ledger cannot express (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-preparation}).
 \item \emph{Measurement compatibility is a multiresolution dial.}  A dyadic
 block-commuting hierarchy interpolates from product-basis QWC measurement to
 fully commuting Pauli groups, and constructive stabilizer-tableau synthesis
 makes the saved setting-shots and added logical two-qubit gates commensurate
 (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-clifford-hierarchy}).
 \item \emph{Generator resolution is an architectural dial.}  Because
 directions are generated by operators, the same subspace can be reached at
 different generator granularities with materially different measurement
 width, at unchanged energy and conditioning; a reference-conditioned
 whole-span leakage certificate makes the word-resolution arm sector safe, and
 sector post-selection of the reference extends it to warm starts that are
 sector-mixed by construction (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-resolution}).
 \item \emph{One bank, several quantities.}  The same cached expectations
 support energy, projected observables via Eq.~\eqref{eq:projected-q}, and
 projected Lehmann response, with no reconstructed $2^n$-component Ritz state
 (Secs.~\ref{sec:response} and~\ref{sec:results-response}).
 \item \emph{Conditioning is a controllable architectural property with a
 measurable downstream price.}  We propagate finite-shot variability through
 the whole nonlinear response pipeline by grouped resampling, and isolate
 conditioning as the single varied factor; an equal-shot diagnostic then
 measures covariance-aware allocation and shot-calibrated truncation
 (Secs.~\ref{sec:results-conditioning}
 and~\ref{sec:results-shot-optimization}).
\end{itemize}

Neither adaptive subspace growth nor replacing variational optimization by a
generalized eigenproblem is new here.  Table~\ref{tab:relation} states the
boundary against the closest approaches.  DA-CASE is distinguished from
ADAPT-GCIM by its fixed-reference Pauli-expectation bank and its overlap-aware
local energy-lowering score, not by adaptivity.  The matched H$_4$ contract of
Sec.~\ref{sec:matched} includes an exact implementation of ADAPT-GCIM's
published selector and generating-function rule---unused anti-Hermitian
excitations ranked on the cumulative surrogate state, every selected unitary at
the fixed angle $\theta=\pi/4$, basis growth $M=2k$ without parameter
optimization---on the same local pool.  That is an algorithm-level comparison,
not a reproduction of the published molecular curves and not a superiority
claim over that method in its original setting.

\section{The architecture}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Single-reference matrix elements}
\label{sec:elements}

Let $H=\sum_w h_w P_w$ be an $n$-qubit Hamiltonian and
$\rho=\ket{\psi}\!\bra{\psi}$ a normalized reference density operator.
For an ordered generator family $\{A_i\}_{i=0}^{M-1}$ with $A_0=I$, define
\begin{align}
    S_{ij} &= \Tr(\rho A_i^\dagger A_j), \label{eq:s}\\
    H_{ij} &= \Tr(\rho A_i^\dagger H A_j), \label{eq:h}\\
    Q_{ij} &= \Tr(\rho A_i^\dagger Q A_j). \label{eq:q}
\end{align}
The retained Ritz vectors solve Eq.~\eqref{eq:gep-intro} after the overlap
spectrum is thresholded.  Their observable matrix elements are
\begin{equation}
    \avg{Q}_{kl}
    =\frac{c_k^\dagger Q_{\rm sub}c_l}
    {\sqrt{(c_k^\dagger S c_k)(c_l^\dagger S c_l)}}.
    \label{eq:projected-q}
\end{equation}
No reconstructed $2^n$-component Ritz state is needed at any point.

Each operator in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:s}--\eqref{eq:q} is expanded in the Hermitian
Pauli basis.  Although $A_i^\dagger H A_j$ need not be Hermitian, its
coefficients may be complex while each Pauli expectation stays real.  Every
entry is thus a linear reconstruction from expectations measured on the
\emph{same} $\rho$: neither an ancilla-based Hadamard test nor a separately
prepared $A_j\ket{\psi}$ is required.  This is the architectural commitment of
the paper, and it is what Sec.~\ref{sec:results-preparation} prices.  It does
not make the calculation free; it moves the binding cost to the number and
grouping of distinct Pauli words.

\subsection{The bank and its resource ledger}
\label{sec:bank}

For a basis index set $\mathcal B$, the measurement universe is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal W(\mathcal B)
 &=
 \bigcup_{i,j\in\mathcal B}\supp(A_i^\dagger A_j)
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad\cup
 \bigcup_{i,j\in\mathcal B}\supp(A_i^\dagger H A_j),
 \label{eq:word-universe}
\end{align}
with width $W=|\mathcal W|$.  The bank caches the operator-valued entries,
reuses Hermitian pairs, and records whether an accepted generator reuses an
existing word or introduces a new one.  Projected observables extend the same
bank by $\supp(A_i^\dagger Q A_j)$---this reuse is the point of the
construction, and Sec.~\ref{sec:results-response} measures what it buys.  The
word set is partitioned into $G$ QWC measurement settings.  If setting $g$
receives $n_g$ shots, its shot-level circuit executions---and therefore its
physical state preparations---are $n_g$, while every compatible word in that
group is read from the same shots.  Thus $W$ is a representation width, $G$ is
a setting count, and neither is itself a physical preparation count.  The
resource ledger is
\begin{equation}
 (M,\;\rank S,\;\kappa_S,\;W,\;G,\;S_H,\;S_A,\;\ell_N,\;\ell_{S_z}),
 \label{eq:ledger}
\end{equation}
where $S_H$ is the largest support of a cached Hamiltonian element, $S_A$ the
largest generator support, and $\ell_N,\ell_{S_z}$ relative commutator norms
with particle number and spin projection.  A method is not compact merely
because $M$ is small.  Section~\ref{sec:results-resolution} shows that $W$
carries its own qualification and must be quoted with a generator resolution
attached.

\subsection{Dyadic block-commuting measurement hierarchy}
\label{sec:clifford-hierarchy}

QWC is a deliberately shallow endpoint of a larger measurement-design space.
For $n=2^L$ qubits and a dyadic block size $k=2^\ell$, partition the register
into contiguous blocks $B_{k,b}$ of $k$ qubits and call two Pauli words
$P,Q$ $k$-compatible when
\begin{equation}
 [P|_{B_{k,b}},Q|_{B_{k,b}}]=0\qquad\text{for every block }B_{k,b}.
 \label{eq:block-commuting}
\end{equation}
At $k=1$ this is exactly QWC; at $k=n$ it is ordinary Pauli commutation.
For a pairwise $k$-compatible group, the restricted words in each block span
an Abelian Pauli subgroup.  A block-local quantum-information Clifford circuit
therefore simultaneously maps an independent stabilizer basis to $Z$-only
words, so the whole group is readable in one setting
\cite{crawford2021efficient,miller2024hardware}.  The block circuits are
disjoint and can be parallelized.

We use the same constructive largest-conflict-degree greedy coloring as for
the QWC ledger, replacing only its compatibility predicate by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:block-commuting}.  For every resulting group and every block, an
independent binary-symplectic stabilizer basis is reduced by tableau
elimination.  We then verify the stronger circuit invariant directly: every
restricted Pauli in the group, not only the independent basis, conjugates to a
$Z$-only word.  The setting count is therefore constructive rather than an
optimal-coloring claim, while the synthesized circuit supplies a logical CX
count and two-qubit depth.  This use of ``Clifford'' means a stabilizer Clifford
measurement circuit, distinct from the Clifford-algebra operator
representation used by DA-CASE itself.

The hierarchy is wavelet-inspired only in its dyadic locality: it does
\emph{not} form Haar or other linear combinations of measured Pauli words.
Thus it preserves the bank and the DA-CASE subspace exactly and moves only the
boundary between setting count and measurement-circuit depth.
Unless stated otherwise, the matched selection ledgers below retain the
$k=1$ QWC endpoint so that their previously fixed measurement contract is not
silently changed; Sec.~\ref{sec:results-clifford-hierarchy} isolates the
dyadic layer on the retained bank with the eigensolver and word universe held
fixed.

\subsection{Adaptive growth}
\label{sec:growth}

Given a current normalized Ritz pair $(E,\ket{\Psi})$ and a candidate direction
$\ket{\chi_a}=A_a\ket{\psi}$, DA-CASE evaluates the generalized $2\times2$
pencil
\begin{equation}
 \begin{pmatrix}E&h_a\\h_a^*&h_{aa}\end{pmatrix}v
 =\lambda
 \begin{pmatrix}1&s_a\\s_a^*&s_{aa}\end{pmatrix}v,
 \label{eq:two-by-two}
\end{equation}
with $s_a=\langle\Psi|\chi_a\rangle$, $h_a=\langle\Psi|H|\chi_a\rangle$,
$s_{aa}=\langle\chi_a|\chi_a\rangle$, and
$h_{aa}=\langle\chi_a|H|\chi_a\rangle$.  The score is the predicted lowering
$\Delta_a=\max(0,E-\lambda_{\min})$, optionally divided by a penalty for newly
introduced words---the one place the ledger of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ledger} feeds back
into selection.  Keeping the overlap block is essential: replacing it by the
identity would assign a nonzero score to a rescaled duplicate of the current
Ritz state.

Before scoring, the component outside the retained subspace is measured by
\begin{equation}
 \eta_a =
 1-\frac{\|{\cal P}_{\mathcal B}\chi_a\|^2}{\|\chi_a\|^2}.
 \label{eq:orthogonal-fraction}
\end{equation}
Candidates below an orthogonality floor, annihilating the reference, or
exceeding the declared sector-leakage tolerance are rejected.  The accepted
candidate with the largest deterministic score appends one row and column to
the cached pencil.  The exact Ritz energy is variational and non-increasing
under nested growth; that statement does not survive arbitrary finite-shot
perturbations of $(S,H)$, a point Sec.~\ref{sec:results-conditioning} makes
concrete.

The sector test has two levels.  An operator-global commutator norm remains the
first, reference-independent gate.  If it rejects a word, a declared sharp
reference sector $q=(N,S_z)$ may instead be tested with projector $P_q$:
\begin{equation}
 \ell_a^{\rm ref}=1-
 \frac{\langle\psi|A_a^\dagger P_q A_a|\psi\rangle}
      {\langle\psi|A_a^\dagger A_a|\psi\rangle}.
 \label{eq:reference-leakage}
\end{equation}
Accepting each direction separately is not enough, because linear combinations
can interfere.  The final bank is therefore certified as a whole with
\begin{equation}
 L_{ij}=\langle\psi|A_i^\dagger(1-P_q)A_j|\psi\rangle,
 \qquad
 \ell_{\cal B}=\lambda_{\max}(L,S).
 \label{eq:span-leakage}
\end{equation}
Thus $\ell_{\cal B}\le\tau$ bounds the out-of-sector fraction of every
nonzero Ritz combination in the retained span.  The fallback is specific to
the declared reference and target sector; it does not reclassify the word as a
globally symmetry-preserving operator.

The candidate hierarchy contains identity, symmetry-preserving determinant
excitations, commutator-response directions, selected Hamiltonian powers,
compound products, and explicitly reached competing-order configurations.
These are Pauli-sum operators in a Clifford-algebra representation; they are
not quantum-information Clifford transformations.  The hierarchy is
deliberately heterogeneous because no single family spans the molecular,
Hubbard, and spin-model rungs below.

\subsection{Generator resolution}
\label{sec:resolution}

One property of an operator-generated basis has no analogue in a
state-preparation-based one.  A determinant excitation is not an atom: it
decomposes into Pauli words, and DA-CASE can be run with either object as its
candidate.  The two choices explore the same reachable span with different
granularity, so they may converge on the same retained subspace by different
routes and at different bank sizes.  Nothing forces them to agree, and nothing
forces the coarser one to be cheaper.

Rather than declare one resolution canonical, we run both and report both.
Section~\ref{sec:results-resolution} shows that on the matched H$_4$ contract
the two resolutions reach a subspace that is the same to machine precision, at
measurement widths differing by a factor of $3.3$, and that the direction of
the saving is not the one a coarser generator suggests.

\subsection{Pricing a comparator's width}
\label{sec:krylov-width}

A ledger that reports $W$ for one method and leaves it blank for the comparator
cannot support any statement about measurable support.  The obstruction is that
building element operators costs $O(|A_i|\,|H|\,|A_j|)$ per pair, and deep
Hamiltonian powers carry thousands of words each.  For the Krylov family that
cost is avoidable.  With $A_0=I$, $A_k=H^k$, and $H$ Hermitian,
\begin{equation}
 A_i^\dagger A_j = H^{i+j},\qquad
 A_i^\dagger H A_j = H^{i+j+1},
 \label{eq:krylov-collapse}
\end{equation}
so the union in Eq.~\eqref{eq:word-universe} reduces from $O(M^2)$ distinct
element operators to the $2m+2$ powers $H^0,\dots,H^{2m+1}$.  This is linear in
$M$ and runs in seconds.  On rungs small enough for direct enumeration to
finish, the two agree exactly, including against the ladder's own tracked
counts of $24$ and $64$ words for H$_2$ and LiH.

One caveat is quantitative rather than structural.  Repeated multiplication
accumulates round-off, and after seventeen products thousands of words carry
coefficients near $10^{-11}$ that are absent from the exact operator.  The
DA-CASE universe is insensitive to this---every one of its $7371$ words on
H$_4$ survives a $10^{-8}$ cut---while the raw Krylov count nearly doubles.
The untruncated count is not even reproducible: two runs of the same rung
differ by a few words, because the upstream self-consistent field settles on
orbital coefficients differing in the last bits, and a Hamiltonian perturbed at
$10^{-16}$ has a different round-off-level support at the seventeenth power.  A
cutoff is an approximation, so it cannot be paired with the untruncated Krylov
energy without a further check.  Every cutoff in the committed sweep is applied
independently to the same unpruned powers, never fed recursively into the next
multiplication, so the support sets are nested.  At each cutoff we rebuild the
complete Hankel overlap and Hamiltonian pencils and resolve the GEP.  The
reported $10^{-8}$ count is admitted only when effective rank agrees and the
normalized pencil entries, ground energy, and condition number meet the
declared tolerances; on every quoted rung those differences are zero at stored
double precision.

\section{What one bank serves}
\label{sec:boundary-response}

\subsection{Restricted FCIDUMP adapter}
\label{sec:fcidump}

FCIDUMP is a compact interchange record for active-space one- and two-electron
integrals \cite{knowles1989fcidump}.  For a real restricted record, the parser
constructs the chemist-notation Hamiltonian directly,
\begin{align}
 H={}&E_{\rm core}
 +\sum_{pq\sigma}h_{pq}a^\dagger_{p\sigma}a_{q\sigma}\nonumber\\
 &+\frac12\sum_{pqrs}\sum_{\sigma\tau}(pq|rs)
 a^\dagger_{p\sigma}a^\dagger_{r\tau}a_{s\tau}a_{q\sigma}.
 \label{eq:fcidump-h}
\end{align}
The implementation restores packed integral symmetries, accepts Fortran
\texttt{D} exponents, validates \texttt{NORB}, \texttt{NELEC}, \texttt{MS2},
sentinels, duplicates, finiteness, and electron--spin parity, then maps an
interleaved spin-orbital ordering through Jordan--Wigner.  Unrestricted
\texttt{IUHF=1} records are rejected rather than interpreted by guess.  A
SHA-256 digest binds every benchmark result to the exact input gauge.  An
independent OpenFermion reconstruction \cite{mcclean2020openfermion} agrees
over $185$ Pauli terms to a maximum coefficient difference of
$8.74\times10^{-16}$.

This boundary is part of the architecture, not preprocessing: the bank is
built once against a digest-bound operator, and every downstream quantity in
this paper inherits that binding.

\subsection{Projected Lehmann response}
\label{sec:response}

For a Hermitian perturbation $Q$, the projected positive-frequency lines are
\begin{equation}
 \omega_f=E_f-E_0,\qquad
 w_f=|\langle\Psi_f|Q|\Psi_0\rangle|^2,
 \label{eq:lehmann}
\end{equation}
and the nondegenerate zero-temperature static susceptibility is
\begin{equation}
 \chi(0)=2\sum_{f>0}\frac{w_f}{\omega_f}.
 \label{eq:chi}
\end{equation}
A Lorentzian of width $\eta$ converts the discrete lines into
\begin{equation}
 S_\eta(\omega)=\sum_f
 \frac{w_f\eta}{\pi[(\omega-\omega_f)^2+\eta^2]}.
 \label{eq:broadening}
\end{equation}
Every ingredient comes from the bank of Sec.~\ref{sec:bank} through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:projected-q}; the response requires no new prepared state and no
new experiment beyond $\supp(A_i^\dagger Q A_j)$.  These quantities are exact
only for the chosen projected subspace.  Response completeness is a separate
requirement from ground-state accuracy: $Q\ket{\Psi_0}$ must be represented by
the retained roots \cite{colless2018spectra,umeano2025response}.

\subsection{Finite-shot propagation}
\label{sec:bootstrap}

One qubit-wise-commuting (QWC) partition \cite{verteletskyi2020grouping} is
built for the union of the words required by $S$, $H$, and $Q_{\rm sub}$.  Each
group contributes a joint outcome histogram, retaining the covariance between
compatible Pauli words read from the same shots.  Because a single bank feeds
every stage, the finite-shot response is not a fixed linear functional of the
measured data:
\begin{align}
 \{\text{histograms}\}
 &\longmapsto(S,H,Q)
 \longmapsto\text{thresholded GEP}
 \nonumber\\
 &\longmapsto(\omega_f,w_f,\chi,S_\eta).
 \label{eq:nonlinear-map}
\end{align}
Linear word-mean intervals cannot be substituted through this map and called
spectral confidence intervals.

We therefore use a grouped nonparametric bootstrap \cite{efron1979bootstrap}.
Each replica draws a multinomial histogram within every fixed QWC group and
reruns all operations in Eq.~\eqref{eq:nonlinear-map}.  Ordered-root matching
is used only when the point estimate and the replica have isolated adjacent
roots.  Replicas with a changed retained rank, a root collision, or a solver
failure are counted and excluded, so percentile intervals are
\emph{conditional on the surviving replicas}.  That exclusion is not neutral:
the failed draws are precisely the ones that can widen a spectrum in an
ill-conditioned regime.  We report the acceptance rate beside every interval.

The evidence label is always \texttt{heuristic}, and \texttt{certified=False}
is part of the result contract.  A finite-sample certificate would require a
confidence set for the correlated matrix pencil and observable together with a
valid root-identification argument.  Pointwise intervals for $S_\eta(\omega)$
are not a simultaneous spectral band.

The same joint histograms support two finite-shot controls before bootstrap.
After a uniform pilot, the physical group budget is apportioned as
$n_g\propto\sqrt{\sum_t V_{tg}}$, where $V_{tg}$ is the plug-in per-shot
variance of target functional $t$ in group $g$, including all within-group
covariances.  Our target family contains the independent real components of
the projected $S/H$ upper triangle and the pilot Ritz derivative
$c^\dagger(H-ES)c$.  Counts are constant within a QWC group, so one physical
setting-shot is never mistaken for separate word shots.

For the downstream GEP, let $\bar S=D^{-1}\hat S D^{-1}$ with
$D_{ii}=\sqrt{\hat S_{ii}}$, formed on the live generators only---those with
$\sqrt{\hat S_{ii}}>\eta$ for the solver's norm floor $\eta$.  Deriving the
floor from a different matrix than the one being truncated is not a floor for
it: a direction that annihilates the reference, normalized by $\eta$ rather
than dropped, inflates its radius by $\eta^{-2}$ and can exceed every
eigenvalue of the live block.  Each observed mode $u_k$ then defines the
linearized functional $u_k^\dagger\bar S u_k$, whose grouped covariance gives a
simultaneous normal radius $r_k$, and mode $k$ is retained when
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_k>\max\!\left(\tau_S,\ \lambda_{\max}/\kappa_{\max},\ r_k\right).
  \label{eq:per-mode-cutoff}
\end{equation}
The per-mode form matters.  Collapsing the radii to one number,
$\tau=\max(\tau_S,\lambda_{\max}/\kappa_{\max},\max_k r_k)$, asks whether each
mode stands above the \emph{worst} mode's shot noise rather than its own; it is
strictly more aggressive and, as Sec.~\ref{sec:results-shot-optimization}
shows, that difference is the whole distance between a usable rule and an
unusable one.  We retain it as the \texttt{uniform} comparator.  The measured
$D$, modes, and radii are data-derived and the same cache is used to choose the
rank and solve the pencil, so this is explicitly a shot-calibrated
\texttt{heuristic}, not a coverage guarantee.  A more conservative entrywise
spectral-radius policy is retained as an option.

\subsection{One contract, explicit currencies}
\label{sec:matched}

Table~\ref{tab:warm} varies $\rho$ at a fixed DA-CASE budget, which prices the
warm start against cold DA-CASE but not against the alternatives.  We therefore
fix one contract and run every arm inside it: the same frozen FCIDUMP and
digest, the same $(N=4,S_z=0)$ sector, the same Hartree--Fock reference, the
same eight-addition budget, and exact arithmetic throughout.  Every arm runs
its budget out, with no early exit.  The pool is the same operator content for
every arm: $26$ symmetry-preserving determinant excitations, which decompose
into $160$ odd-$Y$ Pauli words.  ADAPT-GCIM necessarily needs two rows, since
its published basis rule gives $M=2k$: $k=4$ gives the nearest basis-size match
($M=8$) to the nine-vector subspaces, while $k=8$ matches the number of
adaptive iterations and gives $M=16$.

We do not assign a \emph{partition-independent} exchange rate between a
``state preparation'' and a Pauli word, because neither is the correct atomic
hardware unit.  A shot executes a prepared state in one measurement setting,
and one compatible setting can return many words.  Once the partition is
fixed, $W/G$ is the average number of word samples returned per preparation in
a uniform-shot sweep; Sec.~\ref{sec:results-clifford-hierarchy} shows that this
conditional yield itself changes with the allowed measurement circuit.  We
therefore report word width $W$, constructive QWC group
count $G$, and state-evaluation contexts separately.  For a selector whose
state changes across steps, $G_\Sigma=\sum_s G_s$ sums the settings over those
contexts; for fixed-reference DA-CASE, $G_\Sigma$ is the one cached selection
union.  Under a uniform $R$ shots per setting, the selection execution count is
simply $R G_\Sigma$.  Off-diagonal state-pair measurements remain a separate
primitive because the matched record does not prescribe their physical
implementation.

\subsection{Broader validation ladder}
\label{sec:ladder}

The broader $72$-run committed ladder covers H$_2$, LiH(2e,2o), equilibrium and
stretched H$_4$, stretched H$_2$O CAS(4e,4o), H$_2$O CAS(8e,6o), open-boundary
$2\times2$ and $2\times3$ Hubbard clusters, and a $2\times2$ Kitaev cluster.
The comparison set is the reference determinant, sector-exact diagonalization,
fixed Pauli QSE, Hamiltonian-power Krylov, a strided generator-coordinate
basis, exact ADAPT-VQE \cite{grimsley2019adapt}, and DA-CASE.  Default exact
subspace arms use $M=9$ wherever available; level-4 DA-CASE is additionally run
to $M=26$ on the Hubbard clusters.  Chemical accuracy is defined as $1.6$ mHa
only on molecular rungs; lattice-model errors remain in the Hamiltonian's own
units.

The paper uses four evidence labels, and they are enforced in the result
records rather than in prose:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \texttt{reference}: sector-exact energy against which errors are formed;
 \item \texttt{exact}: noiseless arithmetic on a declared subspace;
 \item \texttt{oracle\_sampled}: exact-ground-state draws used only to test
 sampling-fed selector and subspace construction; no state-preparation claim;
 \item \texttt{finite\_sample}: only the stated sample-split growth decision
 carries a finite-sample bound; the noisy Ritz energy is not certified or
 variational;
 \item \texttt{heuristic}: grouped-bootstrap response diagnostics.
\end{itemize}
This taxonomy prevents a confidence statement about one selection event from
silently becoming a confidence statement about the final spectrum.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The hardware unit is a prepared-state measurement shot}
\label{sec:results-preparation}

Table~\ref{tab:matched} prices the architectural commitment of
Sec.~\ref{sec:elements}.  The entries $1$, $8$, $16$, and $90$ in ``Ctx.'' are
state-evaluation contexts, not physical preparation executions.  DA-CASE and
the fixed operator bases evaluate observables on one reference context;
ADAPT-GCIM has $8$ or $16$ distinct generating-function states; and
ADAPT-VQE visits eight selection states plus $82$ optimizer evaluation
contexts.  On hardware, however, every shot of every measurement setting
requires a fresh preparation.  Calling the $90$ contexts ``90 state
preparations'' therefore undercounts physical preparations and creates a false
exchange rate against a word count.

Grouping puts both sides on a common shot-level primitive.  ADAPT-VQE's
$2424$-word selection union has $452$ QWC settings; replaying the shrinking
active pool over its eight changing states gives $G_\Sigma=3616$ setting
evaluations.  DA-CASE measures its fixed-reference selection union once:
$15783$ words group into $1681$ settings at determinant resolution and $14401$
into $1672$ at word resolution.  Thus with a uniform $R$ shots per setting the
selection schedules require $3616R$ versus $1681R$ or $1672R$ physical
circuits/preparations.  The raw word counts suggest the opposite ordering.
This uniform-shot comparison is a scheduling diagnostic, not an equal-precision
claim: estimator variances can require different $R$.  Weighting each selection
setting by the number of adaptive rotors in its prepared state gives $12656$
rotor-setting evaluations for ADAPT-VQE and zero adaptive-rotor settings for
the cold DA-CASE reference; the record stores this depth-weighted diagnostic,
which is not a total gate count and does not price fixed Hartree--Fock
preparation gates.

The ADAPT optimizer strengthens the same caution.  Its $82$ exact optimizer
evaluations would require at least $82\times68=5576$ additional Hamiltonian
setting-evaluations merely to read the energy, so selection plus energy gives
$9192$ settings per uniform shot before gradient measurements.  The benchmark
uses an exact adjoint gradient and specifies no physical gradient protocol;
we therefore report $5576$ as a lower bound and do not manufacture an
end-to-end hardware count from it.

A second architectural distinction appears in the last column and is easy to
miss in an energy-only comparison.  ADAPT-GCIM's final pencils require $36/28$
and $136/120$ unique $H/S$ state pairs.  Those are transition measurements
between distinct prepared states; they are not expectations on a single
reference, and they cannot be converted into a single-reference $W$ without an
assumption about the hardware primitive.  The comparison in
Table~\ref{tab:matched} therefore reports them in their own column rather than
folding them into a shared width.  A method's measurement cost is not always
expressible in the same unit as its comparator's, and a benchmark that forces
it into one has made a modelling choice it should declare.

On energy at this contract, the exact ADAPT-GCIM comparison changes the
baseline without changing the conclusion.  At the nearest size match, $k=4$
gives $M=8$, effective rank $6$, $\kappa_S=32.9$, and $13.364$ mHa; at the
iteration match, $k=8$ gives $M=16$, effective rank $12$,
$\kappa_S=1.09\times10^3$, and $10.674$ mHa.  Both are variationally above the
sector oracle, and the larger nested basis lowers the energy.  There is no
optimizer: the selector uses $98$ and $180$ candidate gradients at the
published fixed angle.  These numbers establish the exact algorithm on the
matched $26$-excitation local pool; they do not reproduce ADAPT-GCIM under a
larger generalized pool.

\subsection{Dyadic commuting groups expose the preparation--CX exchange}
\label{sec:results-clifford-hierarchy}

The retained determinant-resolution DA-CASE bank provides a controlled test of
the hierarchy in Sec.~\ref{sec:clifford-hierarchy}: $M=9$, $W=7371$, and the
energy and subspace are held fixed while only the measurement compatibility
rule changes.  Table~\ref{tab:clifford-hierarchy} gives the resulting logical
circuits.  The $k=1$ endpoint reproduces the committed $913$-setting QWC
count.  Increasing the block size gives $647$, $238$, and finally $64$
settings.  At a uniform $R=8000$ shots per setting this changes the number of
physical state preparations from $7.304$ million to $0.512$ million, a

$RW$ word samples using $RG$ preparations; the conditional yield is therefore

$115.17$ at $k=8$, a factor of $14.3$.

The entangling basis change is not free.  The synthesized $k=8$ settings use

dominates the intermediate levels in both setting count and aggregate CX count
on this instance, while the intermediate levels retain the advantage in
per-circuit depth.

The resource exchange can now be stated without equating a raw word with a
state preparation.  In the illustrative additive logical cost
\begin{equation}
 C_k=R\left(c_{\rm prep}G_k+c_{\rm CX}N_{{\rm CX},k}\right),
 \label{eq:clifford-cost}
\end{equation}
where $c_{\rm prep}$ prices a base preparation/readout setting-shot and
$c_{\rm CX}$ the marginal logical-CX cost, the $k=8$ circuit beats QWC when
\begin{equation}
 \frac{c_{\rm CX}}{c_{\rm prep}}
 <\frac{913-64}{2006}=0.423.
 \label{eq:clifford-break-even}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, each preparation avoided by the uniform-shot schedule costs
$2006/849=2.36$ added logical CX applications.  The shot count cancels from
this crossover.  All synthesized groups pass the exact $Z$-only conjugation
invariant.  These are logical-circuit resource counts, not a hardware advantage
claim: limited connectivity, routing, calibration, and two-qubit noise can
reverse the ordering, precisely because the $k=8$ circuits are deeper
\cite{miller2024hardware}.

\subsection{Generator resolution controls measurement width}
\label{sec:results-resolution}

One consequential architectural result in the table is the pair of DA-CASE
rows.  Run on the determinant excitations, adaptive DA-CASE needs $7371$ words;
run on the $160$ words those excitations decompose into, it needs $2240$.

The two arms are not merely close in outcome.  They return the same energy to
$4\times10^{-16}$ Ha, the same $M=9$ and $\kappa_S=1$, and their retained
subspaces have all nine principal angles zero.  They are the same subspace,
reached by generators of different granularity, at $3.3\times$ different
measurement cost.  The effect is not particular to this rung: on the
$2\times2$ Hubbard plaquette the same substitution takes $2519$ words to $413$,
a factor of six, again at unchanged basis size and energy.

Two consequences follow, and they point in different directions.

The first is a caution about the ledger itself.  The column introduced in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ledger} to stop $M$ from flattering a method is itself
representation-dependent, so a $W$ quoted without its generator resolution is
not comparable across methods.  This applies to our own numbers: the ladder
rows of Sec.~\ref{sec:results-ladder} are all determinant-resolution and
therefore price one representation of DA-CASE rather than the method's floor.

The second is that the dial is real and usable.  At word resolution DA-CASE
needs fewer words than the Krylov arm's $4224$, which reverses the width
comparison obtained at determinant resolution on this rung.  The saving is paid
for in selection---$1252$ candidate scorings against $180$---so the finer
resolution shifts work from the quantum measurement budget to the classical
selection budget.  For a device where word count binds and classical scoring
does not, that is a favorable exchange available to an operator-generated basis
and unavailable to a basis defined by prepared states, because there is no
corresponding way to subdivide a prepared circuit into cheaper measurable
pieces spanning the same space.

We note one asymmetry that keeps this from being a free win.  Every odd-$Y$
word has operator-global leakage $\sqrt2$ out of particle number, so the
operator-only negative control rejects the pool outright, leaving $M=1$ and
the Hartree--Fock energy.  The default word arm now uses the cascade of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:reference-leakage} and~\eqref{eq:span-leakage}: all accepted
words preserve the declared $(N=4,S_z=0)$ sector on this determinant, and the
final $M=9$ span has $\ell_{\cal B}=0$ and minimum sector weight one.  It still
returns the same energy as the determinant arm to $4\times10^{-16}$ Ha while
using $2240$ rather than $7371$ words.  The saving is therefore enabled by
default for this declared reference without pretending that the words preserve
the sector as abstract operators.

The warm-started arm marks the boundary of the certificate, and shows how to
cross it.  Its reference is an ADAPT state assembled from symmetry-breaking
word rotors, so even the identity direction begins with sector weight
$0.999882$ rather than one.  As reported, that arm retains the operator-global
generator rule and receives no reference-conditioned certificate: there is no
sharp $(N,S_z)$ to condition on, and inference refuses rather than rounding an
average occupation into an undeclared projection convention.

Post-selecting the reference onto the declared sector removes the obstruction
at its source.  Replacing $\rho$ by $P\rho P/\operatorname{tr}(P\rho)$ makes
the sector weight one by construction, so the cascade and the whole-span
certificate both apply; the retained weight is the acceptance probability, and
the $1/\text{weight}$ shot overhead is charged to the prelude rather than
hidden.  On this instance the overhead is $1.00012\times$, and the projected
arm is both certifiable---$\ell_{\cal B}=0$ over the full $M=9$ span---and more
accurate than the arm it replaces, $0.199$ against $0.342$ mHa, because the
discarded $1.2\times10^{-4}$ of out-of-sector weight was contaminating the
projected matrices rather than helping them.  It is the most accurate A-CASE
arm in Table~\ref{tab:matched}, and the only warm-started one carrying a sector
certificate.  We note the obvious limit: post-selection is affordable here
\emph{because} the warm start was already nearly in sector, and a reference at
$60\%$ sector weight would pay $1.67\times$ instead.  Preparing the warm start
with symmetry-preserving rotors remains the route that avoids the overhead
entirely.

not reach chemical accuracy from a determinant reference.  ADAPT-VQE's margin
over DA-CASE also widens on those rungs rather than staying slight---$7.4$
against $39.2$ mHa on stretched H$_4$, and $11.9$ against $55.4$ mHa on
H$_2$O---so from a determinant reference the $M=9$ DA-CASE arm is the weaker of
the two adaptive methods wherever both run on a fermionic rung.

At determinant resolution the width comparison does not favor DA-CASE either.
On both H$_4$ rungs Krylov needs $4224$ words against DA-CASE's $7371$ and
$7715$, and on the Hubbard plaquette $3968$ against $15191$; on the Kitaev
cluster the two coincide at $140$, which they must, since DA-CASE there
\emph{is} a pruned Krylov construction.  The single rung where the
operator-generated basis is narrower at this resolution is stretched H$_2$O
(4e,4o), at $7783$ against $8192$.  Section~\ref{sec:results-resolution} is the
reason this is a statement about a representation and not about the method.

Against the fixed local bases the width comparison runs the other way, which is
the comparison the ledger was built to make: on H$_2$O (8e,6o) DA-CASE is both
more accurate than the generator-coordinate arm and narrower, $143117$ words
against $232515$.

Level-4 configuration generators change the $2\times2$ Hubbard result: DA-CASE
reaches the sector ground state at $M=26$ with $\kappa_S=1$.  The same pool adds
no selected level-4 direction on $2\times3$, leaving a $1.09t$ error at the
same $M$.  The $2\times2$ closure demonstrates that the missing span can be
supplied; the $2\times3$ failure demonstrates that the current selection rule
cannot reliably discover a useful competing-order configuration.  On the Kitaev
cluster, DA-CASE selects six Hamiltonian-power directions and reaches the exact
energy, but its $\kappa_S=3.8\times10^4$ resembles Krylov, because in that case
it is a pruned Krylov construction.

and solver-failure counts before the width of a surviving-replica interval is
interpreted.

\subsection{Allocation and regularization trade variance for bias}
\label{sec:results-shot-optimization}

We isolate the two finite-shot controls on a fixed four-qubit TFIM projected
bank with $M=9$, exact effective rank eight, $\kappa_S=194.94$, $189$ words,
and $52$ QWC groups.  Every arm receives exactly $104{,}000$ physical
setting-shots in each of $200$ independent replicas.  The uniform arm uses
$2000$ shots per group.  The adaptive arm spends $200$ per group on its pilot
and allocates the remaining $93{,}600$ shots by the covariance-aware rule of
Sec.~\ref{sec:bootstrap}.  Fixed and shot-calibrated GEPs reuse the same cache,
so the regularization comparison costs no additional measurements.

The allocation does optimize the quantity it declares.  Relative to uniform
sampling, it reduces the median summed variance of the projected-matrix target
family from $272.46$ to $84.75$ ($68.9\%$) and the exact-Ritz first-order
variance from $3.447\times10^{-5}$ to $2.759\times10^{-5}$ ($20.0\%$).  With
the fixed numerical overlap cutoff this changes the median absolute energy
error only slightly, $4.48$ to $4.44$ mHa, but reduces $>0.1$ Ha failures from
$4/200$ to $1/200$ and the RMSE from $1.786$ to $0.285$ Ha.  Rare admitted
near-null modes dominate both RMSE values.

Both calibrated cutoffs remove those catastrophic replicas outright, in both
allocation arms.  What separates them is how much accuracy that costs.  The
uniform rule of Sec.~\ref{sec:bootstrap} pays heavily: it retains only six or
seven modes and \emph{never} the exact rank eight in $200$ replicas, leaving
median absolute errors of $21.4$ mHa (uniform allocation) and $81.4$ mHa
(adaptive) against a $4.4$ mHa fixed-cutoff baseline.  The per-mode rule of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:per-mode-cutoff} recovers most of that: it reaches rank eight in
$71/200$ and $67/200$ replicas and cuts the median absolute error to $10.6$ and
$8.7$ mHa, the $95$th percentile to $24.0$ and $23.7$ mHa, and the RMSE to
$13.3$ and $12.9$ mHa.  Table~\ref{tab:finite-shot} reports the full cross.

coverage, hardware, or end-to-end advantage claim.

\section{What the architecture does not buy}
\label{sec:limits}

The preceding section states resource properties.  This one states, with equal
weight, the claims the same evidence refuses.

\emph{It is not the most accurate arm at matched budget.}  On the ladder of
Sec.~\ref{sec:results-ladder}, fixed Krylov is more accurate than DA-CASE on
every rung where both run, often by orders of magnitude, and exact ADAPT-VQE is
more accurate on every fermionic rung.  Among the matched-budget arms of a
separate five-system Phase 12 ladder at retained budget $M=7$---open-boundary
$2\times2$ and $2\times3$ Hubbard, linear H$_4$ at $0.9$ and $1.8$ \AA, and the
committed equilibrium-H$_4$ FCIDUMP---fixed Krylov at depth six is the
lowest-error arm on all five rows.  The architecture's case rests on preparation
count, quantity reuse, and conditioning, not on energy at matched $M$.

\emph{Its selection stage is expensive in the currency it saves.}  DA-CASE reads
$14401$--$15783$ words to score candidates and $2240$--$7371$ to measure the
retained subspace.  The scoring cache is strictly larger than the retained
ledger, and it includes rows for candidates later rejected.  A deployment that
cannot amortize selection across many runs on the same pool does not see the
width numbers this paper reports.

\emph{Operator dressing supplies no advantage over a fair classical control at
matched dimension.}  We tested whether augmenting a sampled configuration
program with symmetry-compatible excitation products improves the retained
subspace, against a sample-independent selected-CI comparator that takes the
reference determinant plus the highest-scoring connected determinants under one
Epstein--Nesbet criterion, without consulting the quantum sample.  On the
$2\times3$ Hubbard rung at $M=7$, bare DA-CASE, dressed DA-CASE, and that
matched control return the same error, $2.45704$, agreeing to
$3.6\times10^{-15}$ in the stored double-precision record, across eighty draws.
The dressing is inert.  An earlier comparator that ranked sampled and outside
determinants on one sample-informed scale appeared to show an advantage; that
appearance was an artifact of the control, not a property of the method, and we
withdraw it.  This is a useful negative result for the architecture's scope: a
shared operator bank is a measurement-reuse mechanism, not a selection
improvement.

\emph{Sampling-fed refinement is not established as a general gain.}  Replacing
selected configuration directions with a support-pruned orthonormal Haar
hierarchy separates from the dressed arm in specific system, ordering, and
budget cells, but the policy-pooled effect at the $M=7$ budget is statistically
consistent with zero on both Hubbard systems, and the favorable configuration
ordering itself changes with the retained-direction budget.  Every sampling-fed
row in this programme draws from the exact ground state and is labelled
\texttt{oracle\_sampled}: it is the most favorable possible input distribution,
a selector and subspace capability test, not an implementable
state-preparation protocol.  Studies of exact-ground-state SQD show that
configuration-only selection can require rapidly growing support even under
ideal probability ordering \cite{gaberle2026critical}, and QSCI-style sampling
motivates the boundary we are deliberately not crossing here
\cite{kanno2026qsci}.  We report these experiments as scope limits rather than
as results, and a full account belongs elsewhere.

This negative Haar result does not conflict with
Sec.~\ref{sec:results-clifford-hierarchy}.  The latter leaves every DA-CASE word
and matrix element unchanged and makes only the \emph{measurement
compatibility} multiresolution.  The evidence therefore favors a dyadic
Clifford measurement hierarchy, not wavelet-combined DA-CASE operators.

\emph{Finite-shot growth is not certified.}  Eight-qubit certified-growth
attempts spend $1.5\times10^8$ to $4.6\times10^8$ physical shots and frequently
stop or abstain with inaccurate energies.  A noisy thresholded overlap can even
yield an energy below the exact reference; this does not violate
Rayleigh--Ritz, because the measured pencil no longer represents an exact
subspace projection.  Sample-split certification of a growth coupling must not
be reported as certification of the final energy.

\emph{No scaling or advantage claim is made.}  Every system here is small
enough for exact sector diagonalization.  Nothing in this paper speaks to
asymptotic cost, and the ill-conditioning literature
\cite{epperly2022theory,lee2024sampling,zhang2024measurement} makes clear that
the conditioning behaviour we exploit at these sizes is exactly what requires
independent study at larger ones.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The architecture's defensible claim is narrow and, we think, useful: fixing one
reference and reconstructing every matrix element from expectations on it turns
a family of downstream quantities into a cache lookup, with one reference
context independent of basis size, conditioning under the designer's control,
and a measurement width that the generator resolution can tune.  Physical
preparations still occur once per setting-shot. Each clause is a measured
property in Sec.~\ref{sec:results}, and none of them is an energy comparison.

There is consequently no \emph{partition-independent} scalar ``exchange
rate'' between one state preparation and one measured word.  The common
primitive is a setting-shot.  Once a partition is fixed, however, a uniform
complete sweep has an exact conditional yield: $R W$ word samples from $R G$
preparations, or $W/G$ word samples per preparation.  The dyadic experiment of
Sec.~\ref{sec:results-clifford-hierarchy} shows why that qualification matters:
the same $W=7371$ bank moves from $W/G=8.07$ to $115.17$ solely by changing the
allowed measurement circuit.
For method $X$ a device-level runtime model is
\begin{equation}
 T_X=\sum_{s,g} n_{sg}
 \left[t_{\rm prep}^{X}(s)+t_{\rm basis}(g)+t_{\rm ro}+t_{\rm reset}\right],
 \label{eq:hardware-cost}
\end{equation}
where $s$ is a state/parameter context, $g$ a compatible measurement setting,
and $n_{sg}$ its shot allocation.  State-preparation depth enters through
$t_{\rm prep}$; word packing enters through the number of $g$ values.  They are
therefore priced in the same execution rather than exchanged as independent
objects.

The H$_4$ record supplies a dimensionless break-even curve.  Let
$q=R_{\rm A}/R_{\rm D}$ be the ratio of uniform shots per setting and let
$\bar t_{\rm A}/\bar t_{\rm D}$ be the ratio of mean setting-shot durations in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hardware-cost}.  For selection alone, word-resolution DA-CASE is
faster whenever
\begin{equation}
 q\,\frac{\bar t_{\rm A}}{\bar t_{\rm D}}
 < \frac{3616}{1672}=2.16.
 \label{eq:break-even}
\end{equation}
If the $5576$ optimizer energy settings are included but gradient measurement
is still omitted, $3616$ becomes the lower bound $9192$, giving $5.50$ as a
sufficient threshold against that lower bound.  These are scheduling curves,
not accuracy claims: the required shot ratio $q$ is determined by precision.

At fixed target precision, a principled comparison allocates shots rather than
holding $R$ fixed.  For a linearized target with independent QWC-group
contributions $V_g/n_g$, minimizing $N=\sum_g n_g$ subject to
$\sum_g V_g/n_g\le\epsilon^2$ gives
\begin{equation}
 n_g^*=\frac{\sqrt{V_g}}{\epsilon^2}\sum_h\sqrt{V_h},\qquad
 N^*=\frac{\left(\sum_g\sqrt{V_g}\right)^2}{\epsilon^2}.
 \label{eq:optimal-shots}
\end{equation}
Here $V_g$ must include within-group covariance and the sensitivity of the
reported quantity to that group.  For a thresholded nonlinear GEP those
sensitivities must be propagated through the solve.  The fixed-bank diagnostic
of Sec.~\ref{sec:results-shot-optimization} implements this allocation for the
projected matrices and a pilot Ritz derivative, and shows why rank
regularization must be reported with it.  It does not supply ADAPT-VQE's
missing physical gradient protocol or an equal-precision end-to-end winner.
What is resolved is the accounting: contexts, settings, shots, and depth have a
common hardware expression, and raw words do not stand in for executions.

Three directions follow naturally.  A selection stage that reuses its scoring
cache across runs on a shared pool would address the one currency in which the
architecture is clearly expensive.  The reference-aware cascade should next be
tested on shallow sector-sharp multideterminant references rather than the
symmetry-breaking warm start used here.
The dyadic hierarchy now also makes hardware-aware compilation an explicit
next test: route the exact block-local diagonalizers onto a declared topology
and inject a calibrated two-qubit noise model to locate the $k=1,2,4,8$
crossover rather than extrapolating it from logical CX counts.  Finally, the
conditioning result of Sec.~\ref{sec:results-conditioning} suggests
that acceptance rate, not interval width, is the quantity a subspace response
calculation should be judged on; we would like to see it reported alongside
spectra elsewhere.

\section{Reproducibility and evidence ledger}
\label{sec:reproducibility}

The implementation, frozen inputs, raw benchmark records, table and figure
generators, tests, and this manuscript are maintained together in the
private development repository \texttt{clifford\_qc} while its operator,
measurement, subspace, and backend interfaces are being stabilized.  Public
access to the moving development branch is not claimed.  For independent
review, the authors will provide editors and referees, on request, an
access-controlled frozen snapshot that identifies the exact source revision
and contains the code, data, environment specification, and artifact
generators used here.  A tagged archival release with a persistent identifier
is intended once the framework interfaces and evidence contracts are stable.
Private development changes the distribution channel, not the executable
evidence standard described next.

For provenance stability, source APIs and stored benchmark schemas retain the
identifier \texttt{acase} and the record label \texttt{A-CASE} for the adaptive
subspace engine.  In this manuscript \emph{DA-CASE} denotes that unchanged
engine together with the dyadic Clifford measurement layer of
Sec.~\ref{sec:clifford-hierarchy}.  Table generation maps the stored arm label
to the manuscript name without relabeling the underlying records.

Every number in this manuscript is generated from a committed record rather
than typed.  The table fragments are produced by \texttt{paper\_acase/make\_tables.py}
and the figures by \texttt{paper\_acase/make\_figures.py}; a structural checker,
\texttt{paper\_acase/check\_manuscript.py}, verifies environment balance,
label and citation resolution, generated-fragment presence, table column
counts against their declared preambles, the absence of hand-typed numeric
table cells, and figure freshness against a source-digest manifest.

The interchange boundary is bound by SHA-256 digest to each benchmark result.
The frozen H$_4$ FCIDUMP is committed with its geometry, basis, PySCF version
\cite{sun2018pyscf}, RHF energy, and an external determinant-FCI reference; the
mapped $(N=4,S_z=0)$ sector energy agrees with that reference to
$3.1\times10^{-15}$ Ha, and an independent OpenFermion reconstruction
\cite{mcclean2020openfermion} agrees over $185$ Pauli terms to
$8.74\times10^{-16}$.  After checkout, the H$_4$ path requires only NumPy.

Evidence labels travel with the records, not with the prose.  Exact-arithmetic
rows carry \texttt{exact}; bootstrap diagnostics carry \texttt{heuristic} and
\texttt{certified=False}; sampling-fed rows carry \texttt{oracle\_sampled} and
are excluded from any statement about implementable state preparation.  The
Krylov width counts of Sec.~\ref{sec:krylov-width} are admitted only when the
truncated pencil reproduces the untruncated rank, normalized entries, ground
energy, and condition number within declared tolerances, so every reported
Krylov width is a support count certified to describe the same numerical
pencil as the energy beside it.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented DA-CASE as an architecture for effective many-body
Hamiltonians rather than as a competitor on energy error, and measured what the
architecture costs and returns.  Fixing a single reference and reconstructing
every overlap, Hamiltonian, and observable element from Pauli expectations on
it yields one reference context at any basis size.  The matched H$_4$ context
counts are $1$ against $8$, $16$, and $90$, but they are not physical
preparation counts: at the shot-level primitive the word-resolution DA-CASE
selection bank is $1672$ QWC settings once, against $3616$ ADAPT-VQE selection
setting-evaluations across changing states; the latter's optimizer adds at
least $5576$ energy settings before gradient measurements are priced.  The
same bank architecture makes energy, projected observables, and Lehmann
response available from one cached set of $63$ words on the finite-shot dimer
without materializing a Ritz state.  Because the basis is operator-generated, its
measurement width is tunable: the same retained subspace, identical to machine
precision in energy, dimension, conditioning, and every principal angle, costs
$7371$ or $2240$ words depending on generator resolution, and $2519$ or $413$
on a Hubbard plaquette.  For H$_4$ the word-resolution span now carries a
reference-conditioned whole-bank certificate with zero sector leakage, while
the operator-global negative control continues to reject the words; sector
post-selection carries the same certificate to the ADAPT warm start, which is
sector-mixed by construction, and improves it from $0.342$ to $0.199$ mHa at a
$1.00012\times$ shot overhead.
Measurement compatibility supplies an independent dial: the $7371$-word H$_4$
determinant bank needs $913$ QWC settings but only
$64$ fully commuting settings under the constructive dyadic hierarchy.  At
$8000$ uniform shots this is $7.304$ versus $0.512$ million preparation
executions; the reduction is paid for by $2006$ logical CX gates per one pass
over all $64$ settings and mean/max two-qubit depth $25.72/42$, with the logical break-even
$c_{\rm CX}/c_{\rm prep}<0.423$.  Conditioning is likewise a design variable with a
measured downstream price: at matched system, observable, word set, groups,
shots, and seeds, four orders of $\kappa_S$ take bootstrap acceptance from
$200/200$ to $139/200$ and widen the $\chi(0)$ interval roughly fifteenfold.
At a separate matched $104{,}000$-shot fixed bank, covariance-aware allocation
reduces the projected-matrix variance target by $68.9\%$, while a calibrated
overlap cutoff read per mode removes every catastrophic energy and cuts RMSE
$22\times$ against the fixed cutoff, at a factor-of-two cost in median error
and a residual upward bias no truncated rank can avoid.

The costs are equally definite.  The architecture is not the most accurate arm
at matched budget on any rung tested; its selection stage reads an order of
magnitude more words than it measures; its sampling-fed operator dressing is
inert against a sample-independent selected-CI control at matched dimension;
and nothing here supports a scaling claim.  What we offer is a resource profile
bound to a strict interchange contract and reproducible from committed records.
Contexts, QWC settings, shots, and preparation depth are connected by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hardware-cost}; a variance-optimal fixed-precision comparison
still requires the method-specific group variances and a physical ADAPT
gradient protocol.  Making that missing information explicit is the point: no
raw state-context or word count may stand in for end-to-end performance.
We make no quantum advantage claim.
